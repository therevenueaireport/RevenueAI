# MOMENTUM: Intervention Design, Early Wins, and the Rescue Protocol

**Use this file when:**
- You are designing a program of 6 to 12 weeks and want the mid-program dip handled in the calendar, not in a panic.
- Usage of a new AI tool fell off after the first two or three weeks and you need to know why and what to do this week.
- A program is stalling and you need a diagnosis, a reset session, and scripts for managers and executives.
- You need thresholds that tell you when to intervene, before the lagging metrics confirm the stall.

This file is the depth layer for `enablement-momentum`. Pair it with `enablement-measure` for metric definitions and `enablement-coach` when the stall cause is coaching quality.

---

## 1. Why Momentum Breaks: The Three Curves

Three curves run through every behavior-change program. The stall happens where they cross.

| Curve | Shape | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Novelty | High in weeks 1 to 2, falls steadily | Curiosity, launch energy, executive attention |
| Effort | Rises as learners move from Practice to Apply | Conscious Competence: the skill works but takes deliberate thought |
| Payoff | Flat early, rises only after enough live reps | Wins need reps; reps need weeks |

The valley sits where novelty has dropped and payoff has not yet arrived, usually weeks 3 to 6. Learners are at Competency Level 3, Conscious Competence. The new behavior is slower and clumsier than the old habit, and the old habit is still available.

Published transition research calls this the neutral zone: the old way has ended, the new way is not yet natural. People in the neutral zone are uncomfortable and look for an exit. Your job is to make the neutral zone shorter, safer, and visibly worth it.

**What the science says to design for:**

| Principle | Originator | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Self-efficacy has four sources: mastery experiences, vicarious experience, verbal persuasion, physiological and emotional state | Self-efficacy research | Early wins (mastery), peer models (vicarious), specific manager encouragement (persuasion), safe practice (emotional state) |
| Small, visible progress in meaningful work raises motivation | Workplace-motivation research, the progress principle | Make progress visible weekly, not only at the end |
| Small wins accumulate and build momentum for larger change | Organizational research on small wins | Break the skill into wins that can be completed in days |
| Behavior happens when motivation, ability, and a prompt converge | Behavior-design research | When motivation drops, raise ability (simplify) and attach the behavior to an existing prompt |
| If-then plans increase follow-through | Goal-pursuit research, implementation intentions | Turn every commitment into "When X happens, I will do Y" |
| Autonomy, competence, and relatedness sustain motivation | Self-determination theory | Give choice in how, show competence growth, connect learners to peers |
| Coping models (peers who struggle, then succeed) can build self-efficacy more than polished experts | Peer-modeling research | Spotlight peers who were stuck last week, not only top performers |
| Forgetting is fast without spaced retrieval | Memory research (the forgetting curve) | Space reinforcement through the valley; do not front-load everything |

## 2. Early Warning Signals and Thresholds

Use these in the first 8 weeks, before you have enough data for control charts. All thresholds are rules of thumb. Calibrate them to your team and write your versions down before launch. After 8+ weeks of data, switch to the control chart rules in `metric-health-dashboard`.

| Signal | Metric | Watch (rule of thumb) | Intervene (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attendance decay | Attendance vs week 1 | Down 10% or more | Down 20% or more, or 2 consecutive drops |
| Pre-work decay | Pre-work completion | Below 80% | Below 65% |
| Relevance decay | Pulse R1 mean (1 to 5) | Below 4.0 | Below 3.5, or down 0.5 from week 1 |
| Commitment decay | Q3 answers coded Specific | Below 60% | Below 40% |
| Confidence stall | Self-rated confidence | Flat 2 weeks during Practice or Apply | Falling 2 weeks |
| Practice avoidance | Practice reps completed vs plan | Below 80% of plan | Below 60% of plan |
| Apply delay | Learners with first live rep | Under half by end of first Apply week | Under one-third by end of first Apply week |
| Coaching drift | Coaching sessions delivered to standard | Below 90% of plan | Below 75%, or any manager at zero for a week |
| AI usage decay | Weekly active users / eligible | Down 2 consecutive weeks | Down 3 consecutive weeks, or below half of week-2 peak |
| AI shallow use | Workflow coverage | Flat 2 weeks | Falling while logins hold steady |
| Social proof drought | Wins posted to the wins channel | None in 5 business days | None in 10 business days |
| Executive silence | Leader touchpoints | Missed one planned touchpoint | Missed two, or a competing initiative announced with no word on this one |

**Firing rule:** One Intervene signal, or three Watch signals at the same time, triggers the stall diagnostic (Section 8). Do not wait for win rate or pipeline to confirm it. By then the cohort has already reverted.

**Read signals in clusters.** Clusters point to the root cause:

| Signal cluster | Likely root cause |
|---|---|
| Relevance decay + commitment decay + "doesn't apply to me" quotes | WHY erosion |
| Practice avoidance + confidence stall + regression on live calls | Conscious Competence discomfort |
| Attendance decay + coaching drift + new initiative launched | Environmental competition |
| Social proof drought + low Q3 specificity + isolation in quotes | Absent social proof |
| Coaching drift + shorter, vaguer coaching log entries | Coaching quality degradation |
| AI usage decay + low output acceptance + "it's faster to do it myself" quotes | Tool friction (check this before blaming people) |

**Tool friction note:** A bad tool fails every rung. If the AI output does not meet the bar on sampled work, momentum methods will not save it. Score 10 recent outputs against the standard before running a motivation intervention.

## 3. Early-Win Architecture

An early win is a moment in weeks 2 to 3 where each learner succeeds at a simplified version of the skill, on real or realistic work, and knows it. This is a mastery experience, in the self-efficacy research sense, delivered on purpose.

### 3.1 The five tests for a good early win

| Test | Question | Fails if |
|---|---|---|
| Small | Can a learner complete it in one sitting or one call? | It needs a closed deal |
| Attributable | Is it clearly caused by the new skill? | It could have happened anyway |
| Verifiable | Can a manager or the rubric confirm it? | It relies on the learner's say-so |
| Reachable | Can most of the cohort hit it by end of week 3? | Only top performers can |
| Visible | Will the learner and peers see it? | It happens silently in a log |

### 3.2 Win ladder (design one per program)

Build three rungs of win, from easiest to hardest. Everyone should hit rung 1 by end of week 2.

| Rung | Definition | Example: discovery questioning | Example: AI research assistant for AEs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Win 1 (week 2) | Did the simplified behavior once, to standard, in Practice | Scored 3 on the follow-up question rubric in a role play | Generated an account brief from the template prompt and marked 3 facts as verified |
| Win 2 (week 3) | Did it on live work once | Asked two follow-ups on a live call; manager confirmed from the recording | Used the brief on a live first call and cited one insight the buyer confirmed |
| Win 3 (weeks 4 to 6) | Did it on live work and it changed something | Buyer stated impact in their own words, captured in CRM | Brief surfaced a stakeholder or initiative that changed the call plan |

### 3.3 Simplify, do not shrink

When learners are struggling, the behavior-design model says raise ability. Reduce the size of the rep, not the standard. The zone of proximal development says the task should be just beyond what they can do alone, with support.

| Instead of | Do |
|---|---|
| "Run the full discovery framework on every call" | "Ask one follow-up question on the first problem the buyer names" |
| "Use the AI tool for all account research" | "Use the AI tool for the first call of the day, with this one template prompt" |
| "Lower the bar for this group" | Keep the rubric. Shrink the scope of the rep |

### 3.4 Early-win spec template

```
PROGRAM: [name]
WIN 1 (week 2): [simplified behavior], done in [setting], verified by [who/how]
WIN 2 (week 3): [live behavior], done on [type of call/account], verified by [who/how]
WIN 3 (weeks 4-6): [live behavior + observable effect], verified by [who/how]
WHERE WINS ARE RECORDED: [coaching log field / channel / tracker]
WHO ANNOUNCES: [manager / enablement], within [24 hours]
TARGET: [x]% of cohort at Win 1 by [date]; [x]% at Win 2 by [date]
IF BELOW TARGET: [simplify rep size / add pairing / extra practice block]
```

## 4. Social Proof System

Social proof is not a celebration channel. It is the Observe rung running live during the program: people see a peer like them do the skill and get a result. Research on social proof shows people look to similar others when uncertain; social-learning research shows modeling builds self-efficacy.

### 4.1 Components

| Component | What it is | Cadence | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wins channel | Dedicated channel with a structured post format (below) | Ongoing; target 3+ posts per week | Managers seed, enablement curates |
| Show-the-work spotlight | 3 to 5 minute clip or screenshot of the behavior, with the learner narrating what they did | Weekly, in team meeting | Enablement |
| Coping-model story | A peer who was stuck, what they changed, what happened | Weeks 3 to 5 | Enablement interviews, learner presents |
| Practice pairs | Advancing learner paired with struggling learner for one 20-minute rep per week | Weeks 3 to 6 | Enablement assigns |
| Cohort progress board | Aggregate only: % at Win 1, Win 2, Win 3 | Weekly | Enablement |

### 4.2 Structured win post (pin this format in the channel)

```
WHAT I DID: [the specific behavior]
WHERE: [deal or account type, no customer names if policy requires]
WHAT HAPPENED: [buyer response or result]
WHAT I'D TELL SOMEONE TRYING THIS: [one sentence]
```

The last line is a mini Teach rep. It turns social proof into retrieval for the poster and modeling for readers.

### 4.3 Rules

- **Show the work, not just the result.** "Closed a deal" teaches nothing. "Asked this follow-up and the buyer told me the real budget owner" teaches the skill.
- **Aggregate in public, individual in private.** Cohort-level progress is public. Individual rankings stay between rep and manager. Public bottom-of-board rankings during Practice break the safety that learning depends on.
- **Spread the spotlight.** Rotate featured learners. If the same three people are always featured, everyone else concludes the skill is for top performers.
- **Seed early.** If nothing is posted by day 3 of week 2, managers post the first wins from their own observations, with the rep's permission.

## 5. WHY Re-Activation Event (Week 4)

The original WHY was delivered at launch, when novelty carried it. By week 4 it has eroded. The re-activation event reconnects learners to the reason, using new evidence from their own cohort.

### 5.1 Evidence types, strongest first

1. Data from this cohort: behavior scores rising, early outcome signals, before-and-after examples
2. A live peer story from the cohort (coping model preferred)
3. A customer voice: a buyer quote or call clip showing the behavior landing
4. A leader connecting the skill to a business priority they will be measured on
5. External evidence (use sparingly; it is the weakest because it is not "us")

### 5.2 Agenda: 45 minutes

| Minutes | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Open | Facilitator names the dip directly: "Week 4 is where this gets hard. That is normal and expected." |
| 3 to 10 | Cohort evidence | Show 2 to 3 cohort data points: % at Win 1 and Win 2, rubric movement, one before/after artifact |
| 10 to 18 | Peer story | One learner, coping model: what was hard, what they changed, what happened. Q&A |
| 18 to 23 | Customer voice | One clip or quote where the behavior landed with a buyer |
| 23 to 28 | Leader | Sales leader or exec: why this matters to the number this quarter, what they will look for |
| 28 to 40 | Personal WHY and commitment | Each learner writes: "This matters to me because ___. When [situation] happens this week, I will [behavior]." Share in pairs |
| 40 to 45 | Close | Restate Win 2 and Win 3 targets and the support available. Announce the next spotlight |

### 5.3 Personal WHY prompts (pick 2)

- "Which deal in your pipeline right now would go differently if you did this well?"
- "What would you stop having to do if this skill were automatic?"
- "What would you want a new hire on your team to learn from you about this in 3 months?"

The third prompt previews Teach, and gives learners a reason grounded in competence and relatedness, not compliance.

## 6. Executive Visibility and Competing Priorities

### 6.1 Executive cadence

| When | Touch | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Kickoff message or appearance | 3 to 5 min | Why this, why now, what I will look for |
| Week 5 | Mid-program acknowledgement | 2 to 3 min, in team meeting or recorded | Name the hard middle, cite a cohort win, reaffirm priority |
| End | Recognition and next step | 5 min | Recognize behavior change, announce what happens next (wave 2, Teach roles) |
| Any time | Competing priority reaffirmation | 1 message | Within 48 hours of a competing initiative launching |

### 6.2 Competing priority protocol (agree before launch)

| Situation | Decision | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| End-of-quarter push | Keep coaching sessions, pause new content for 1 week, shrink practice reps by half | Program sponsor |
| New initiative launches for the same cohort | Sponsor and new initiative owner agree which comes first; communicate within 48 hours | Both owners, escalate to CRO |
| Manager reassigned or out | Backup coach named in advance takes their sessions | Enablement |
| Reorg or territory change | Pause outcome measurement, keep behavior measurement, re-baseline after | Sponsor plus RevOps |
| Cohort under heavy pipeline pressure | Reduce scope to the single most important behavior; extend program 1 to 2 weeks | Sponsor |

**Rule:** The default answer to competition is to reduce scope, not to pause. A paused behavior program loses the reps already invested; a scoped-down one keeps the habit alive.

## 7. Week-by-Week Momentum Map

Base map for an 8-week program. Stretch or compress using the notes below.

| Week | LOPAFT focus | Momentum risk | Designed move | Watch metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Learn, Observe | Low; novelty high | Exec kickoff. Personal baseline. Good and bad examples library live | Attendance, R1 relevance, calibration accuracy |
| 2 | Observe, Practice | Low to medium; first effort | Win 1 in Practice. Seed wins channel. AI tool: template prompts from power users | Practice reps vs plan, % at Win 1 |
| 3 | Practice, first Apply | Medium; novelty dropping, AI novelty drop begins | Win 2 on live work. First spotlight. Practice pairs assigned | Time to first live rep, WAU trend |
| 4 | Apply, Feedback | High; the valley | WHY re-activation event. Normalize the dip. Coping-model story | Confidence, Q3 specificity, coverage |
| 5 | Apply, Feedback | High | Exec mid-program touch. Coaching calibration check. Simplify reps for strugglers | Coaching delivered to standard, behavior frequency |
| 6 | Feedback, early Teach | Medium; payoff starting | Win 3 stories. First teach-backs from advancing learners | Behavior change rate, rubric trend |
| 7 | Feedback, Teach | Medium; complacency | Learners who hit Win 3 coach a peer. Spaced retrieval check | Retention check score, teach-back quality |
| 8 | Teach, transition | Medium; drop-off after "graduation" | Exec recognition. Hand-off to manager coaching cadence. Announce next cycle | Live rubric score, coverage holding |

**12-week version:** Insert a second Practice-Apply-Feedback loop at weeks 6 to 9 at the next level of skill complexity (LOPAFT is a spiral). Add a second WHY touch at week 8 and a second exec touch at week 9. Teach runs weeks 10 to 12.

**6-week version:** Compress weeks 1 and 2 into week 1 only if learners already passed the baseline at Level 2 or higher (`enablement-assess`). Hold the WHY event at week 3 and the exec touch at week 4. Do not cut Practice or Feedback to save time.

**After the program:** Spaced reinforcement continues at roughly 2, 4, and 8 weeks after the end: a 10-minute retrieval check or one scored live artifact each time. The habit is not formed at week 8. Habit-formation research found automaticity takes weeks to months for simple daily behaviors, with wide variation between people. Complex sales behaviors take longer.

## 8. Mid-Program Reset and Rescue Protocol

Run this when the firing rule in Section 2 triggers.

### 8.1 Timeline

| Phase | Time | Actions | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnose | Within 72 hours | Pull signal clusters. Run the Quick Stall Diagnostic in `enablement-momentum`. Talk to 3 to 5 learners (mix of advancing and stalled) and 2 managers. Score 10 recent artifacts. Name one root cause | Enablement lead |
| Decide | Day 4 | Choose the intervention from Section 9. Set the recovery metric and the check date. Brief the sponsor | Enablement plus sponsor |
| Reset | Within 7 days | Run the reset session (8.2). Ship the intervention | Enablement plus managers |
| Verify | 10 business days after reset | Check the recovery metric. If not recovering, re-diagnose; do not repeat the same fix | Enablement |

### 8.2 Reset session: 60 minutes

| Minutes | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Honest open | "Usage and practice dropped. That is on the program design, not on you. Here is what we are changing." |
| 5 to 15 | What we heard | Share 3 anonymized learner quotes and the one root cause. Ask: "Did we get this right?" |
| 15 to 25 | What changes | Announce the scope change, the simplified rep, or the support added. Be specific |
| 25 to 45 | Live rep | Everyone does one simplified rep now: role play, or the AI tool on a real account. Pairs give one piece of feedback against the rubric |
| 45 to 55 | Re-commit | Each learner writes one if-then plan for the next 5 business days |
| 55 to 60 | Close | Next win target, date of next check, who to ask for help |

### 8.3 Scope reduction rules

- Cut to the single behavior with the strongest link to the outcome. Park the rest for the next loop.
- Keep the rubric standard. Shrink the rep size.
- Extend the program 1 to 2 weeks rather than compressing what remains.
- Re-baseline the behavior metric after the reset so recovery is measured from the new starting point.

### 8.4 When to pause or stop

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| Tool output fails the standard on sampled work | Pause the tool rollout. Fix or replace the tool. Keep the skill program if it stands without the tool |
| Sponsor will not protect calendar time after the competing priority protocol | Pause formally, with a restart date. Communicate it. A silent fade teaches the org that programs can be ignored |
| Two reset cycles, no recovery in behavior metrics | Stop. Run a success case review (the success case method) to learn why. Redesign before relaunch |
| Behavior moved but outcome did not after a full sales cycle | Do not stop the behavior on momentum grounds. Review the causal hypothesis with `enablement-measure` |

## 9. Intervention Playbook by Stall Cause

| Root cause | First move (within 48 hours) | Second move (within 2 weeks) | Owner | Recovery metric | Escalate if |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WHY erosion | Pull cohort evidence and one peer story; schedule a WHY re-activation (Section 5) | Leader connects skill to a metric managers are measured on | Enablement plus sales leader | R1 relevance back to 4.0+; Q3 specificity rising | Relevance still falling after event: check whether the skill fits the actual deals |
| Conscious Competence discomfort | Managers run the "dip is normal" script (10.2). Shrink rep size | Practice pairs; celebrate attempts that meet the standard, not only outcomes | Managers | Practice reps back to plan; confidence rising | Practice stays low: check if Practice is truly safe (no scores shared publicly, no customer stakes) |
| Environmental competition | Sponsor sends reaffirmation within 48 hours. Apply competing priority protocol | Calendar holds for coaching protected by sponsor | Sponsor | Attendance and coaching delivered back to Watch level or better | Sponsor will not reaffirm: formal pause with restart date |
| Absent social proof | Managers post the first 3 wins from their own observation. Run a spotlight this week | Coping-model story; cohort progress board | Managers plus enablement | 3+ wins posted per week | No wins exist to post: the problem is upstream at Practice or Apply |
| Coaching quality degradation | Enablement observes 2 coaching sessions per manager and scores them | Coach the Coach intervention via `enablement-coach` | Enablement | Coaching delivered to standard at 90%+ | One manager stays at zero: escalate to their leader |
| Tool friction (AI programs) | Score 10 outputs against the standard. Collect "faster to do it myself" examples | Fix prompts or templates using power-user versions; or pause the tool | Enablement plus RevOps | Output acceptance rising; workflow coverage rising | Outputs still fail the standard: tool decision, not a momentum decision |

## 10. Manager Scripts

Use these word for word the first time, then adapt. Every script ends with an if-then commitment.

### 10.1 Weekly huddle opener (2 minutes)

"Quick check on [skill]. This week, [number] of you hit [win]. [Name] did [specific behavior] on [deal type] and [result]. [Name], what's one thing you'd tell the team? ... This week the target is [win]. When you [situation], [behavior]. I'll be listening for it on [call type]."

### 10.2 The dip is normal (1:1 with a struggling rep)

"This feels slower and clunkier than how you used to do it. That's exactly where you should be in week [n]. It means you're doing it on purpose instead of on autopilot, and that's the stage before it gets automatic. Let's make the next rep smaller. On your next [call type], just do [one simplified behavior]. When you get off that call, send me the recording link and I'll give you one thing to adjust."

### 10.3 "This doesn't apply to my deals"

"Tell me about a deal where it wouldn't fit. ... Okay. Show me one where it might. ... Let's try it on that one only, this week. If it doesn't help, I want to hear that, because it tells us something about how we built this."

(Take the objection seriously. If several reps in one segment say it, the skill may not fit that segment. That is a design finding, not resistance.)

### 10.4 The rep who reverted

"On the [date] call, I heard the old version: [describe observed behavior]. Two weeks ago on [deal] you did [new behavior] and [result]. What got in the way this time? ... What's one situation this week where you'll do it the new way? Say it as: when ___ happens, I'll ___."

### 10.5 The top performer who says they don't need it

"You're probably already doing a lot of this. I'd like you to do two things: score yourself on the rubric for two of your calls so we can confirm, and if you're at a 3 or 4, teach it to [peer] in a 20-minute session. Explaining it will sharpen it for you too."

(This uses the Teach rung and gives autonomy and status instead of compliance.)

### 10.6 AI tool: "it's faster to do it myself"

"Show me the last time you used it. ... Where did it slow you down? ... That's useful. Try [power user]'s version of the prompt on your next three accounts and tell me if it's still slower. If it is, I'll take that back to enablement as a tool problem, not a you problem."

## 11. Executive Scripts

### 11.1 Week 1 kickoff (3 minutes)

"We're investing [time] in [skill] because [business priority, in the number leadership watches]. This is not a one-time training. For the next [n] weeks, your managers will coach this every week, and I'll be listening to calls for it. Weeks 3 to 5 will feel harder before they get easier. That's expected. What I'll be looking for is [observable behavior], not perfection."

### 11.2 Week 5 mid-program acknowledgement (2 minutes)

"We're at the hardest part of [program]. I want to name that. I've seen [specific cohort win, with name and permission]. [Number] of you are already doing [behavior] on live deals. This stays a priority even with [competing thing] going on. Your managers have protected time for coaching through [date]."

### 11.3 Competing priority reaffirmation (written, within 48 hours)

"You've seen the announcement on [new initiative]. To be clear on priorities: [program] continues. Here's what changes: [scope adjustment from protocol]. Here's what doesn't: [coaching cadence, core behavior]. Questions to [owner]."

### 11.4 Program close (5 minutes)

"Here's what changed: [behavior metric, before and after]. Here's what we're seeing early in [outcome]. Thank you to [names of learners who taught peers]. What happens next: [wave 2 / next loop / manager coaching cadence]. [Names] will help teach the next group."

### 11.5 Formal pause (when needed)

"We're pausing [program] until [date] because [honest reason]. The skill still matters. When we restart, we'll [change]. In the meantime, managers will keep [minimal behavior] in your 1:1s."

## 12. AI-Tool Adoption Specifics

AI tool rollouts follow the same three curves, with a sharper novelty drop. In many rollouts, usage peaks in weeks 1 to 2 and drops in weeks 2 to 3 as curiosity runs out and the tool has not been anchored to a workflow. Plan for it as a rule of thumb, not a surprise.

### 12.1 Why the AI novelty drop happens

| Cause | What learners say | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity use, no workflow anchor | "I played with it the first week" | Tie use to one existing workflow trigger (a behavior-design prompt): "After every discovery call, run the review" |
| Blank-page prompting | "I don't know what to ask it" | Ship 3 to 5 template prompts sourced from internal power users |
| Output below the bar | "It's generic" / "I have to rewrite it" | Observe rung: show good and bad AI outputs; teach what context to add |
| Verification burden | "It's faster to do it myself" | Practice judging outputs: when is it wrong, what to check, in under 2 minutes |
| No feedback on AI-assisted work | "Nobody looks at it" | Managers score a sample of AI-assisted work against the standard weekly |
| Trust break after one bad output | "It made something up" | Normalize it; show the check that catches it; make catching errors a skill to practice |

### 12.2 Segment the cohort by usage

Sort every eligible user into four buckets from the admin usage export at week 3:

| Bucket | Definition | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Never used | No sessions | Manager 1:1, 10 minutes, do the first use together on a live account |
| Tried and dropped | Used in week 1, none since | Ask what broke. Give the template prompt for their most common workflow |
| Occasional | 1 to 3 uses per week | Anchor to one daily trigger; pair with a power user |
| Power user | Daily, in workflow | Interview for prompts and workflows; recruit as peer teachers |

### 12.3 AI-specific momentum metrics

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Week-N retention (users active in week N who were active in week 1) | Shows the novelty curve directly |
| Workflow coverage | Separates real adoption from logins |
| Output acceptance rate | Shows whether the output meets the bar |
| Override or redo rate | Shows where trust is breaking |
| Time to first in-workflow use | Shows whether Apply has a trigger |

Report these as adoption metrics, never as outcome metrics. Pair them with behavior and outcome measures per `enablement-measure`.

### 12.4 Judgment is the skill

For AI tools, the behavior you are building is not "use the tool." It is "use the tool and know when it is wrong." Build Practice around judging outputs (find the error, fix the missing context) and Feedback around scoring the final work, not the tool usage.

## 13. Worked Example (Illustrative, Invented for Teaching)

**Context:** A mid-market sales team rolls out an AI call-review tool alongside a discovery questioning skill. Cohort: 30 AEs, 4 managers. 8 weeks. Pre-launch, managers agreed on a 4-level discovery rubric and calibrated on 5 shared calls.

**Weeks 1 to 2.** Exec kickoff. Examples library with 3 strong and 3 weak discovery calls, annotated. Template prompts for the review tool sourced from two AEs who used it in a pilot. Win 1 (role play scored 3) hit by 24 of 30 by end of week 2. Weekly active users: 28 of 30.

**Week 3. Signals fire.**
- Weekly active users fell to 19 of 30, second consecutive drop. (Watch, then Intervene next week.)
- Wins channel: one post in 6 business days. (Watch.)
- Practice reps at 70% of plan. (Watch.)
- Two managers logged no coaching sessions this week. (Intervene.)

Firing rule met. Enablement runs the 72-hour diagnostic.

**Diagnosis.** Interviews with 5 AEs and 2 managers. Quotes: "I open the review but I don't know which part to act on." "My manager hasn't looked at my calls since week 1." Score of 10 recent AI reviews against the standard: 8 of 10 acceptable, so the tool is not the problem. Coaching log shows two managers pulled into an end-of-month forecast push.

Root cause named: **coaching quality degradation**, compounded by environmental competition. Absent social proof is a symptom.

**Intervention.**
- Sponsor sends competing priority reaffirmation within 48 hours; coaching sessions protected through week 6.
- Enablement observes one coaching session for each of the two managers and runs a 30-minute Coach the Coach session on using the AI review to pick one adjustment per call.
- Managers seed the wins channel with 4 observed wins from week 2 calls.
- Reps get a simplified rule: "After each discovery call, open the review, find the one follow-up question you could have asked, and post it in your coaching thread."

**Week 4.** WHY re-activation event using cohort data (24 of 30 at Win 1, rubric mean up from 1.8 to 2.4) and a coping-model story from an AE who was stuck in week 2.

**Weeks 5 to 8.** Weekly active users recover to 26 of 30. Coaching delivered to standard back to 95%. 21 of 30 AEs hit Win 2, 12 hit Win 3. Six AEs run teach-backs for the next cohort. Outcome measurement continues per `enablement-measure` with a staggered comparison group.

**What made the difference:** the stall was caught in week 3 from leading signals, the root cause was named from evidence (not assumed to be "rep motivation"), and the fix targeted the coaching layer instead of adding another training session.

## 14. Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Re-running the launch training when usage drops | Solves a Learn problem when the gap is at Practice, Apply, or Feedback | Diagnose the rung first |
| Gamification with public leaderboards | Rewards volume, shames strugglers, invites gaming | Aggregate progress public, individual private |
| Celebrating only closed deals | Wins arrive too late to carry the valley | Celebrate verified behavior wins in weeks 2 to 5 |
| Featuring only top performers | Others conclude the skill is for the gifted | Rotate spotlights; use coping models |
| Lowering the standard to make wins easier | Wins stop meaning anything | Shrink the rep, keep the rubric |
| Executive attention only at launch | Silence reads as "this no longer matters" | Weeks 1, 5, and end, plus reaffirmations |
| Letting a program fade instead of pausing | Teaches the org that programs can be ignored | Formal pause with a restart date |
| Blaming reps for AI tool drop-off without checking output quality | A bad tool fails every rung | Score sampled outputs first |
| Waiting for win rate to confirm a stall | The cohort has reverted by then | Act on leading signal clusters |
| "Graduating" the cohort at week 8 with no follow-up | Habits are not formed yet; forgetting curve wins | Spaced checks at roughly 2, 4, 8 weeks after |
