# P: Practice

The step-three playbook: safe, deliberate reps with no customer on the line. Moves the learner from Level 2 (Conscious Incompetence) to Level 3 (Conscious Competence).

**Use this file when:**
- Designing role plays, sandboxes, simulations, dry runs, or AI roleplay.
- Reps "practice but don't improve," or avoid practice altogether.
- A complex skill needs to be broken into parts and rebuilt.
- You are about to point reps at live customers with a new tool or method and have not given them a safe place to try it first.

---

## 1. What Practice is for, and what breaks without it

Practice is deliberate, low-consequence repetition. It is not a test, and nobody's job depends on it. Stakes come at Application.

| Practice does | Failure if missing |
|---|---|
| Converts recognition (from Observe) into production | Reps can spot good discovery and still cannot do it |
| Lets the learner fail cheaply and try again | First attempts happen on live deals; deals and trust in the tool are burned |
| Builds the self-efficacy to try it live | Reps revert to the old habit the moment a real buyer pushes back |
| Generates data on where each rep is weak | Coaching targets impressions instead of evidence |

**Where rollouts stall.** In failed rollouts the problem most often sits between Practice and Apply, because nobody got safe reps. Handing a rep a new tool and pointing them at a live customer burns the deal and the rep's trust in the tool.

**Practice that does not improve anyone** usually has one of four defects: no clear target (missing O), no feedback within the rep (missing F), the difficulty is wrong (too easy or too hard), or it is not safe (graded, public, or tied to performance reviews). Section 4 diagnoses each.

## 2. The science you are applying

| Principle | Research base | What it means for Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Deliberate practice | Expertise research | Improvement comes from practice with a specific goal, full focus, immediate feedback, and difficulty just beyond current ability. Repetition alone is not deliberate practice. |
| Zone of proximal development | Developmental psychology | The useful zone is what the learner can do with help but not yet alone. Practice lives there. |
| Scaffolding | Developmental psychology | Temporary support (scripts, prompts, simplified scenarios) that is removed as competence grows. |
| Self-efficacy | Self-efficacy research | The strongest source of belief that "I can" is mastery experience: succeeding at a real attempt. Practice manufactures early mastery experiences. |
| Social learning | Social-learning research | People learn from watching and being watched by peers. Triads and group practice use this. |
| Affective filter | Language-acquisition research | Anxiety blocks acquisition. LOPAFT is explicit: Practice must be safe. |
| Psychological safety | Team-learning research | Teams learn when members believe they will not be punished for mistakes. |
| Desirable difficulties | Memory research | Some difficulty (variation, spacing, interleaving) slows practice performance and improves long-term retention. |
| Interleaving | Memory research | Mixing problem types after initial learning improves the ability to choose the right approach. |
| Deep practice | Skill-acquisition and myelination research | Slow, error-focused, chunked practice at the edge of ability. |

## 3. Design patterns

### Pattern A: Sub-skill isolation, then integration

Do not practice the whole call first. Break the skill into sub-skills, isolate one at a time, then integrate.

| Phase | What happens | Example (discovery) |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce | Learner tries the sub-skill in its simplest form, with a script or prompt card | Read a hypothesis opener from a card to a cooperative partner |
| Isolate | Drill only that sub-skill, many short reps, with a partner who pushes back on that element only | 10 reps of D3: partner gives vague pain, rep must get a buyer-confirmed number |
| Integrate | Combine with previously isolated sub-skills in a realistic scenario | Full 15-minute discovery call, D1-D5, partner improvises |

This sequence mirrors how athletic coaching progresses a technique: introduce it, isolate it against increasing resistance, then integrate it into live, unscripted play. The LOPAFT author note cites athletic coaching science as a source for this kind of progression.

**Isolation rules**
- One sub-skill per drill. If the rep is working on D3, the partner does not punish weak D1.
- Short reps. Rule of thumb: 2-5 minutes per rep, so a 30-minute session gets many attempts.
- Resistance rises inside isolation: cooperative partner, then realistic partner, then difficult partner.
- Integrate only when the sub-skill is at standard in isolation against a realistic partner.

### Pattern B: Resistance ladder

Practice that is always cooperative builds false confidence. Practice that starts at maximum difficulty destroys confidence. Climb the ladder.

| Rung | Partner behavior | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Scripted | Partner follows a script; rep may use a prompt card | Learn the shape |
| 2 Cooperative | Partner answers openly, no tricks | Build fluency |
| 3 Realistic | Partner answers like a typical buyer: partial info, some deflection | Build adaptability |
| 4 Difficult | Partner deflects, challenges, changes the subject | Build composure |
| 5 Live-like | Unscripted full scenario with surprises | Readiness check for Apply |

Decision rule: move up a rung after the rep meets standard on 2-3 consecutive reps. Drop down a rung after 2 consecutive failures. That keeps practice in the zone of proximal development.

### Pattern C: Scaffolds and fading

| Scaffold | Fade by |
|---|---|
| Prompt card with exact questions | Card with only the five behavior names |
| Partner pauses the role play to give a hint | Hints only after the rep asks |
| Pre-written buyer scenario with known answers | Unknown scenarios |
| Coach present in the room | Peer observer with a checklist |
| Rep allowed to pause and restart | No pauses |

Rule: every scaffold has a planned removal date. Scaffolds left in place become crutches.

### Pattern D: The practice triad (social learning)

Three roles, rotated each rep: **Rep**, **Buyer**, **Observer**.
- The Observer scores one behavior only, using the rubric, and gives one piece of feedback.
- Rotation means everyone gets reps, watches peers (vicarious learning), and practices scoring (strengthens discrimination from O).
- Triads mix levels where possible: one Level 3 peer in a triad gives the other two a live model.

### Pattern E: Interleaving after isolation

Once sub-skills are each at standard, mix them. Random scenarios force the rep to choose which behavior the moment calls for, which is what real calls demand.

### Pattern F: Safety by design

| Safety rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Practice scores never go into performance reviews | The moment they do, reps optimize for looking good instead of improving |
| No public scores or rankings | Public ranking raises the affective filter and kills attempts |
| Managers practice first, badly, in front of the team | Signals that mistakes are expected |
| "Restart" is allowed at rungs 1-3 | Lowers the cost of trying something new |
| Feedback is one adjustment, not a list | A list overloads working memory and feels like a verdict |

## 4. Exit criteria: ready to move to Application

Practice targets Level 3: can do it, with effort, in a safe setting.

| Signal | Not ready | Ready for A |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated sub-skills | Below standard on any sub-skill at rung 3 | Each sub-skill at standard (rubric 3+) at rung 3 or higher on 2-3 consecutive reps |
| Integrated performance | Falls apart when combining | Full scenario at standard on most behaviors at rung 4 |
| Effort | n/a | Still effortful and deliberate. That is expected at Level 3. Do not wait for automaticity. |
| Self-assessment | Cannot say what went wrong | Can name what went wrong and the adjustment for next time |
| Confidence | Avoids attempts, asks to skip | Willing to try it on a live call; can name the first call they will use it on |

**Decision rules**
- Do not wait for Level 4 before Application. Automaticity comes from real reps plus Feedback, not from more drills.
- If a rep is stuck below standard after many isolated reps, check Observe: can they discriminate that behavior? If not, go back to O.
- If reps perform well in practice but avoid trying it live, the gap is confidence or environment. Plan the first live attempt with them (see A-application.md).
- If practice scores are high but integrated scores are low, isolation was too long. Start interleaving sooner.

### Diagnosing practice that does not improve anyone

| Symptom | Likely defect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Reps practice, scores flat | No feedback within the rep, or feedback is a list | Observer gives one adjustment per rep; next rep tries it |
| Reps cannot say what to aim at | Missing O | Re-run contrast pairs for that behavior |
| Reps breeze through | Too easy | Climb the resistance ladder |
| Reps freeze or quit | Too hard or unsafe | Drop a rung; check whether scores are visible to anyone else |
| Reps game the role play | Partner too predictable | Rotate scenarios; interleave |
| Attendance drops | Practice feels like a test | Remove grading; manager practices first |

## 5. Instruments and templates

### 5.1 Practice session agenda: isolated sub-skill (45 minutes, triads)

| Min | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Target | Replay the contrast pair for today's sub-skill (from the O library). State the standard in one line. |
| 5-8 | Demo | Facilitator or manager does one rep live, deliberately imperfect, and self-corrects out loud |
| 8-35 | Triad rounds | 3 rounds x 9 min. Each round: 3-min rep, 2-min observer feedback (one adjustment), 1-min retry of just the adjusted moment, rotate roles |
| 35-40 | Harvest | Each triad shares one moment that worked. Best moment becomes a library candidate. |
| 40-45 | Commit | Each rep writes: "On my next practice, I will ___." Pick a rung for next session. |

### 5.2 Practice session agenda: integration (60 minutes)

| Min | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Frame | Full scenario, all behaviors in play, unscripted buyer |
| 5-50 | 3 rounds x 15 min | 10-min call, 3-min self-assessment first, then observer, 2-min reset |
| 50-60 | Readiness check | Each rep names their first live call for Application and the one behavior most at risk |

### 5.3 Observer checklist (one behavior)

```
Rep: ______   Behavior: ______   Rung: ______
[ ] Behavior attempted
[ ] Key element 1 present: ______
[ ] Key element 2 present: ______
[ ] Buyer responded as intended
Score (1-4): ___
The one moment that worked: [what was said] ______
The one adjustment for next rep: ______
```

### 5.4 Buyer persona card (for role-play partners)

```
Role: ______   Company context: ______
Your real problem (do not volunteer it): ______
Your number (only give it if asked well): ______
How you buy (only reveal if asked): ______
Rung behavior: [Cooperative / Realistic / Difficult]
Your deflection lines: 1. ______ 2. ______
What would make you agree to a next step: ______
```

Write 4-6 personas per skill. Rotate them so reps cannot memorize answers.

### 5.5 Practice log (rep-owned)

| Date | Sub-skill | Rung | Score | What I adjusted | Next rep focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 5.6 Practice design audit

- [ ] Each drill targets one sub-skill.
- [ ] A contrast pair from O exists for every sub-skill practiced.
- [ ] A resistance ladder is defined with movement rules.
- [ ] Every scaffold has a removal date.
- [ ] Feedback inside the session is one adjustment per rep.
- [ ] Practice scores are invisible to anyone outside the triad and the coach.
- [ ] Practice scores are excluded from performance reviews, in writing.
- [ ] Interleaved practice is scheduled after isolation.
- [ ] Reps get many short reps per session, not one long one.
- [ ] An integration session precedes the first live application.

**Scoring**: 9-10 = strong. 6-8 = workable. Below 6 = reps are rehearsing, not practicing.

## 6. Practice in the AI era

AI changes Practice more than any other step. LOPAFT's AI role at P: AI tutors and simulators give unlimited practice; question-first coaching keeps the learner thinking.

| Shift | Implication |
|---|---|
| AI roleplay | A rep can practice a discovery call at 10pm against a simulated buyer with a persona you wrote. Practice volume is no longer limited by manager time. |
| Practice data | Simulation tools show where each rep is strong and weak, repeatedly, without a manager grading each session by hand. |
| Instant mechanical feedback | The tool can score each rep against the rubric immediately, which tightens the deliberate practice loop. |
| Safe by default | A machine buyer removes the social cost of failing in front of peers. Useful for reps with a high affective filter. |

**Design rules for AI roleplay**
1. **Write the personas yourself.** Feed the simulator your buyer persona cards (5.4). A generic AI buyer is too cooperative or randomly hostile.
2. **Configure isolation.** Set the simulated buyer to resist on one sub-skill at a time, matching the resistance ladder. A full-difficulty simulated call on day one is a test, not practice.
3. **Calibrate the simulator's scoring.** Same rule as call review: score a sample of simulated sessions by hand and compare before trusting the tool's feedback.
4. **Keep scores private.** Simulator scores follow the same safety rules as human practice scores. No leaderboards of AI-detected weakness.
5. **Keep humans in the loop.** AI roleplay adds volume; it does not replace the triad. Peer practice builds vicarious learning, scoring skill, and team norms that solo AI practice does not.
6. **Use question-first prompts.** Configure the AI coach to ask "what did you notice when the buyer said X?" before telling the rep the answer. Telling first skips the thinking.

**Practicing judgment of AI output.** When the AI drafts the review, summary, or email, the rep must practice catching what is wrong. Build drills where the correct answer is "this output contains an error, here it is."
- Give reps AI call reviews with one planted error. The rep finds it.
- Rule of thumb from agent-era ramp design: do not let a rep use an AI tool for a task until they have done that task manually enough times to spot when the output is wrong.
- Practice restraint: drills where the right answer is "do not send this" or "do not book this meeting."

## 7. Worked example (illustrative)

> **Program scenario (illustrative, used in all six step files).** A 12-AE mid-market team is rolling out a new discovery method and an AI call-review tool at the same time. Two frontline managers (6 AEs each), one enablement lead, one RevOps analyst. The method has five scored behaviors: **D1** Hypothesis opener, **D2** Layered problem questions, **D3** Quantified impact confirmed by the buyer, **D4** Buying process mapped, **D5** Dated next step agreed live. The AI tool scores every recorded call against D1-D5 and drafts a call review. The AEs' second skill is judging that review: catching wrong or missing scores before acting on it. Traffic light: red. Program: 10 weeks plus a day-90 check. The week-0 baseline (enablement-assess) placed most AEs at Level 1 or 2 on D3 and D4, and at Level 1 on judging AI output.

**Practice runs weeks 2-5**, overlapping with Application from week 3 (a sub-skill goes live once it is at standard in practice).

| Week | Live triad session (45 min, Tue) | AI roleplay (async) | AI review judgment drill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | D1 + D2 isolation, rungs 1-3 | 3 sims, buyer resists on D2 only | 2 AI reviews, one planted error each |
| 3 | D3 isolation, rungs 2-4 | 3 sims, buyer gives vague pain, resists numbers | Self-score own call before opening AI review; log differences |
| 4 | D4 isolation, rungs 2-4 | 3 sims, buyer withholds approval process | AI review with a missed D4 moment |
| 5 | Integration, full call, rung 4-5 | 2 full sims, randomized personas | Full review of own live call, correct the AI where wrong |

**Key design decisions**
- The enablement lead wrote 6 buyer personas and loaded them into the AI roleplay tool. Each persona had a "resist on" setting matching the week's sub-skill.
- Triads mixed levels. The senior AE at Level 3 on D3 sat in a different triad each week in week 3 as the Buyer, giving every triad a strong difficult-buyer partner.
- Managers did the first D3 rep in front of their teams, got it wrong, and self-corrected out loud.
- Practice and simulator scores were visible only to the rep and their manager, and the program charter stated they would not be used in performance reviews.

**What happened (illustrative)**
- D3 was the hardest. Most AEs plateaued at Level 2 at rung 3: they suggested a number and the simulated buyer did not engage. Diagnosis using section 4: they could discriminate D3 in O, so the target was clear. The defect was feedback inside the rep: the AI scored the attempt but did not show why. Fix: the observer in the triad gave one adjustment ("ask how they measure it today before you offer a number"), and the rep immediately retried only that moment. Scores moved to standard within two sessions for most.
- Two AEs avoided AI roleplay entirely. Their managers learned that they assumed the scores were reported upward. The manager showed them the privacy setting. Both started using it that week.
- On the judgment drills, the most common miss was the tool crediting D3 when the AE, not the buyer, said the number. By week 5, 10 of 12 AEs caught it.

**Exit decision at end of week 5**

| Group | Evidence | Next |
|---|---|---|
| 9 AEs | Each sub-skill at standard at rung 3+; integrated full call at standard at rung 4 on most behaviors | Full method in Application |
| 2 AEs | At standard on D1, D2, D5; below on D4 | Apply D1-D3 and D5 live; one more D4 isolation week |
| 1 AE | Cannot discriminate D4 on unlabeled clips | Back to O for D4: one extra contrast pair and a calibration check |

## 8. Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping Practice | First attempt is a live deal; trust in the tool is burned | Give every sub-skill safe reps before it goes live |
| The big role play in front of the team | Performance anxiety; reps perform for the audience | Triads, private scores, manager goes first |
| Whole-skill practice only | Too many things to fix; nothing improves | Isolate sub-skills, then integrate |
| Practice graded for performance reviews | Reps optimize for looking good | Write the exclusion into the program charter |
| Always-cooperative partner | False confidence; collapse on the first real objection | Resistance ladder with movement rules |
| Feedback list after each rep | Overload; nothing changes on the next rep | One adjustment, retried immediately |
| Scaffolds that never fade | Reps cannot perform without the prompt card | Removal date for every scaffold |
| AI roleplay as the whole program | No peer learning, no observer skill, no team norms | AI adds volume; triads remain |
| Generic AI buyer | Too easy or randomly hostile; not your buyer | Your personas, your rubric, configured resistance |
| Practicing with the AI tool before the skill | Reps learn to accept the output | Manual first; then judgment drills on AI output |
