# A: Application

The step-four playbook: real work, real deals, real risk. Stress-tests and solidifies Level 3 (Conscious Competence) and moves the learner toward early Level 4.

**Use this file when:**
- Skills "work in drills, not real calls."
- You are planning the first live reps after practice, or a phased go-live for a new tool or method.
- Managers need a plan for supporting transfer on the job, not just in the classroom.
- Adoption looks fine in week one and then decays as the old workflow reasserts itself.

---

## 1. What Application is for, and what breaks without it

Application takes the practiced skill into an actual work situation. Not a simulation, not a classroom exercise. Real stakes, real output. This is where Conscious Competence gets stress-tested and either holds or reveals new gaps.

| Application does | Failure if missing |
|---|---|
| Moves the skill from the training context to the work context | "Works in drills, not real calls." Skill is context-bound to the practice room. |
| Reveals gaps practice could not | Practice scenarios are cleaner than real buyers. Untested gaps surface later, at a worse moment. |
| Builds the habit loop in the real workflow | The old habit wins by default, because it is wired into the CRM, the calendar, and the call template |
| Produces the evidence Feedback measures | Without live reps, there is nothing real to measure against the standard |

**Where rollouts stall.** In failed rollouts the problem most often sits between Practice and Apply. The second most common pattern: the work moved into live deals with nobody named as owner, so nothing was checked and the skill quietly decayed.

**Application is not Practice with higher stakes.** It needs its own design: which situations, which order, what support, what is measured, and who owns it.

## 2. The science you are applying

| Principle | Originator | What it means for Application |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer of training model | Baldwin and Ford | Transfer depends on three things: learner characteristics, training design, and the work environment (manager support, opportunity to use the skill). Most programs only design the second. |
| Identical elements | Edward Thorndike | Transfer is more likely when the training situation shares elements with the work situation. |
| Near and far transfer | Learning science (widely used) | Near transfer (similar situation) comes first. Far transfer (different situation) needs explicit bridging. |
| Implementation intentions | Peter Gollwitzer | "When situation X arises, I will do Y" plans make follow-through more likely than goal statements. |
| Behavior model (B = MAP) | BJ Fogg | Behavior happens when motivation, ability, and a prompt converge. Make the new behavior easy and prompted in the workflow. |
| ADKAR | Jeff Hiatt / Prosci | Individual change needs Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement. Application is where Ability is proven and Reinforcement begins. |
| Success Case Method | Robert Brinkerhoff | Study the most and least successful appliers to find what in the environment made the difference. |

### ADKAR and LOPAFT side by side

ADKAR describes the change outcome; LOPAFT describes how to produce it. They align roughly as follows.

| ADKAR | Produced mainly by |
|---|---|
| Awareness | L (gap surfacing) |
| Desire | L (WHY stack), reinforced throughout |
| Knowledge | L, O |
| Ability | P, A |
| Reinforcement | F, T |

## 3. Design patterns

### Pattern A: The application ladder (near to far)

Do not go live on everything at once. Sequence live situations from most similar to practice to least similar.

| Rung | Live situation (discovery example) | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Internal dry run with a friendly customer champion or partner | Real person, low stakes |
| 2 | First discovery calls with inbound, smaller-deal prospects | Closest to practice scenarios |
| 3 | Standard first discovery calls | The core use case |
| 4 | Multi-stakeholder calls | Higher cognitive load |
| 5 | Late-stage re-discovery, renewals, expansions | Far transfer |

Rule of thumb: move up a rung when the behavior meets standard on most eligible calls at the current rung for two consecutive weeks.

### Pattern B: Phase the behavior, not just the people

Go live one behavior or sub-skill group at a time, in the same order as Practice. A rep at standard on D1-D2 applies those live while still practicing D3.

### Pattern C: Engineer the environment (Baldwin and Ford)

| Environment factor | Design move |
|---|---|
| Opportunity to use | Make sure reps have enough eligible calls. A rep with 2 discovery calls a month needs a different plan than one with 12. |
| Manager support | Manager reviews the plan before the first live call and debriefs after it |
| Workflow prompts | Put the behavior into the tools: call-prep template, CRM fields, calendar invite agenda |
| Removing the old path | Retire the old template or field. If the old path stays easier, it wins. |
| Peer support | A named application buddy at the same or higher level |
| Protected capacity | Reduce competing asks during the first application weeks |

### Pattern D: Implementation intentions

Each rep writes if-then plans for the moments most likely to trigger the old habit.

```
When the buyer says "____________," I will ____________.
When I feel the urge to start the demo before ____________, I will ____________.
Before every first discovery call, I will ____________ (prompt in the workflow: ____________).
```

### Pattern E: Before, during, after (manager role)

| When | Manager action | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|
| Before the first live call | Review the rep's call plan; agree on the one behavior to focus on | 10 min |
| During | Nothing. Do not take over the call. | 0 |
| Right after | 5-minute debrief: what happened at the moment, what to keep, one adjustment | 5 min |
| Weekly | Review one live call against the rubric (see F-feedback.md) | 20-30 min per rep |

### Pattern F: Named ownership

Every live application has an owner and a check. If no one owns checking whether D3 happened on live calls, it will decay without anyone noticing.

### Pattern G: Change management for the team, not just the individual

| Lever | Action |
|---|---|
| Leadership signal | Senior leader asks about the new behavior in pipeline reviews, not just the number |
| Inspection | Deal reviews use the new fields and language |
| Competing priorities | Pre-decide what gets paused if a new initiative lands mid-program (see enablement-momentum) |
| Visible early wins | Surface the first real deals where the behavior made a difference |
| Consistency | All managers inspect the same behaviors the same way, or reps learn it is optional |

## 4. Exit criteria: ready to rely on Feedback and Teach

Application targets a stress-tested Level 3 with early Level 4 signs. The cycle does not end here: Feedback continues throughout Application, and Teach begins once a rep is consistently at standard.

| Signal | Still early Level 3 | Stress-tested Level 3 / early Level 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Consistency on live calls | At standard on some calls, mostly the easy ones | At standard on most eligible calls, including harder ones, across consecutive weeks |
| Under pressure | Drops the behavior when the buyer pushes back or time runs short | Keeps the behavior when the call goes off-script |
| Effort | Needs the prep template open and a reminder | Prepares with the template; performs without reading from it |
| Self-correction | Notices the miss only in review | Notices the miss during the call and recovers |
| Adaptation | Uses the same phrasing every time | Adapts the behavior to the buyer and situation |
| Far transfer | Only on standard discovery calls | Starts using it in situations not trained (renewals, expansions) |

**Decision rules**
- If reps perform in practice and not live, check the environment before re-training: opportunity, manager support, workflow prompts, and whether the old path is still easier.
- If performance drops on harder calls only, move back down one rung on the application ladder and add Practice at that difficulty.
- If one manager's team applies and another's does not, the gap is coaching, not the reps. See enablement-coach.
- If a rep applies consistently, start Teach for that behavior. Do not wait for the whole cohort.

## 5. Instruments and templates

### 5.1 Application plan (one page per rep)

```
Rep: ______   Manager: ______   Buddy: ______   Start date: ______
Behaviors going live now: ______    Still in Practice: ______
Eligible calls per week (expected): ______
Application ladder rung: ______   Move-up rule: ______
First live call: [account] [date]   Planned focus: ______
If-then plans:
  1. When ______, I will ______.
  2. When ______, I will ______.
Workflow prompts in place: [ ] prep template  [ ] CRM fields  [ ] invite agenda
Manager before/after scheduled: [ ] Y
Weekly review slot: [day/time]
What will I do if I miss the behavior on a call? ______
```

### 5.2 Post-call self-debrief (5 minutes, rep-owned)

```
Call: ______   Date: ______
Did I attempt the focus behavior? Y / N
The moment it happened (or should have): [timestamp] ______
Self-score (1-4): ___
What the buyer did in response: ______
If I missed it, what triggered the old habit? ______
One adjustment for next call: ______
```

### 5.3 Transfer environment audit (run before go-live)

- [ ] Every rep has enough eligible calls to apply the skill weekly.
- [ ] The behavior is prompted in the workflow (template, field, or agenda).
- [ ] The old template, field, or path has been retired or made harder.
- [ ] Every manager has a before/after routine on the calendar.
- [ ] Deal and pipeline reviews ask about the new behavior.
- [ ] Each rep has a named application buddy.
- [ ] Competing asks are reduced for the first application weeks.
- [ ] A competing priority protocol exists.
- [ ] There is a named owner for checking live application each week.
- [ ] Early wins will be collected and shared.

**Scoring**: 9-10 = environment supports transfer. 6-8 = at risk. Below 6 = expect "works in drills, not real calls."

### 5.4 Weekly application tracker (team view)

| Rep | Eligible calls | Calls with behavior attempted | At standard (3+) | Ladder rung | Manager debriefs done | Flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Flags: attempted rate falling for 2 weeks; at-standard rate flat for 3 weeks; no debrief in 2 weeks. For telling real change from normal week-to-week noise, see metric-health-dashboard (control-chart rules).

### 5.5 Success case interview (Brinkerhoff), 20 minutes

Interview the 2-3 strongest and 2-3 weakest appliers.

1. Walk me through the last call where you used [behavior]. What happened?
2. What made it easier to do on that call?
3. What got in the way on calls where you did not use it?
4. What did your manager do that helped or did not?
5. What in the tools or process helped or got in the way?
6. What result, if any, can you trace to it?

Compare the two groups. The differences are usually environmental. Fix those for everyone.

## 6. Application in the AI era

LOPAFT's AI role at A: AI as co-pilot; the human provides judgment.

| Shift | Implication |
|---|---|
| AI in the live workflow | Prep assistants, live prompts, and post-call reviews put support inside the real call. That raises ability and lowers effort. |
| Full coverage | Call-review tools show application on every call, not a sample. Managers see whether the behavior happened without listening to everything. |
| Just in time help | Help can arrive on the deal calendar, not the training calendar: a prompt before the call the system knows is a first discovery. |
| New failure mode: unexamined action | Plausible AI drafts produce action nobody checked. Application now includes judging the machine's output before using it. |

**Rules for applying with an AI tool**
1. **Human judgment stays with the rep.** The AI drafts the review; the rep decides what is true. Build in a step where the rep confirms or corrects before anything goes to the CRM or the buyer.
2. **Watch for prompt dependency.** If a live in-call prompt tells the rep what to ask, the rep may never internalize it. Fade live prompts the same way Practice fades scaffolds.
3. **Track reversals.** Count how often reps had to correct or undo what the AI produced. A falling correction rate can mean the tool improved or the reps stopped checking. Spot-check which.
4. **Do not let the tool become the inspection.** A manager who only reads AI scores is not coaching. Scores route attention; clips change behavior.
5. **Phase the tool like the skill.** Start reps on AI review only for behaviors they can already judge. Unlock the rest as their judgment is verified.

## 7. Worked example (illustrative)

> **Program scenario (illustrative, used in all six step files).** A 12-AE mid-market team is rolling out a new discovery method and an AI call-review tool at the same time. Two frontline managers (6 AEs each), one enablement lead, one RevOps analyst. The method has five scored behaviors: **D1** Hypothesis opener, **D2** Layered problem questions, **D3** Quantified impact confirmed by the buyer, **D4** Buying process mapped, **D5** Dated next step agreed live. The AI tool scores every recorded call against D1-D5 and drafts a call review. The AEs' second skill is judging that review: catching wrong or missing scores before acting on it. Traffic light: red. Program: 10 weeks plus a day-90 check. The week-0 baseline (enablement-assess) placed most AEs at Level 1 or 2 on D3 and D4, and at Level 1 on judging AI output.

**Application runs weeks 3-10** and continues after the program.

**Phased go-live**

| Week | Behaviors live | AI review use | Ladder rung |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | D1, D2, D5 | AE self-scores first, then opens AI review, logs disagreements | 2 (inbound, smaller deals) |
| 4 | Adds D3 | Same | 2-3 |
| 5 | Full method (D4 for those at standard in Practice) | AE confirms or corrects AI review before it syncs to CRM | 3 |
| 6-8 | Full method | Same; AI D2 and D4 scores unlocked for reps after recalibration passed | 3-4 |
| 9-10 | Full method | Same | 4, plus far-transfer trial on renewal calls for reps at standard |

**Environment changes made before week 3 (transfer audit scored 9 of 10)**
- RevOps added four required CRM fields to the discovery stage: hypothesis, quantified impact (buyer's number and words), decision process, next step date. The old free-text "discovery notes" field was removed.
- The calendar invite template for first calls was rewritten to include a hypothesis line, which prompts D1.
- The VP of Sales changed the weekly pipeline review question from "what's the deal size?" to "what number did the buyer put on the problem?"
- Each AE had an application buddy. The senior AE at Level 3 on D3 was buddy to two AEs.
- Gap left open: SDR-booked meetings sometimes arrived with no research time. Logged as a risk.

**What happened (illustrative)**
- Week 4 tracker: D3 attempted on most eligible calls, but at standard on few. Self-debriefs showed the trigger: when buyers gave a vague pain, AEs moved to the demo to fill the silence. Fix: an if-then plan ("When the buyer says 'it's a big problem,' I will ask how they measure it today") and one extra D3 practice round at rung 4.
- One manager's team applied D4 at a much higher rate than the other's. A success case interview showed the difference: that manager did a 10-minute pre-call plan review for every first discovery in week 5. The other manager adopted it in week 6. See enablement-coach for the coaching side.
- The AI tool kept crediting D3 when the AE said the number. Because AEs had to confirm or correct before CRM sync, the correction log made the pattern visible. RevOps fed the logged corrections back for a rubric prompt fix. Correction rate on D3 fell afterward; a spot-check of 10 calls confirmed the tool had improved, not that reps had stopped checking.
- The SDR-booking gap was the main reason for missed D1 on some calls. The SDR handoff template was changed to include two research notes.

**Status at week 8**

| Group | Evidence | Next step in the cycle |
|---|---|---|
| 7 AEs | At standard on D1-D5 on most eligible calls for 2+ weeks, including multi-stakeholder calls | Teach begins (T-teach.md); Feedback spacing widens |
| 4 AEs | At standard on D1, D2, D5; D3 or D4 inconsistent on harder calls | Back one rung for D3/D4; weekly Feedback stays tight |
| 1 AE | Low eligible call volume (territory change) | Extended timeline; more simulated reps to keep the skill warm |

## 8. Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Go use it on your calls" | No sequence, no owner, no check | Application plan with ladder, owner, and weekly check |
| Big-bang go-live of every behavior | Cognitive overload on live calls; everything drops | Phase behaviors in practice order |
| Training-only design | Ignores the work environment, where most transfer fails | Run the transfer environment audit before go-live |
| Old path left in place | The old habit is easier and wins | Retire the old template or field |
| Manager takes over the call | Rep learns nothing; confidence drops | Manager is silent during, debriefs after |
| Leadership asks only about the number | Signals the behavior is optional | Inspect the behavior in pipeline reviews |
| Treating every rep's volume as equal | Low-volume reps never get enough reps | Plan by eligible calls; supplement with simulation |
| Measuring tool logins as application | Usage is not behavior change | Measure the behavior on calls against the rubric |
| AI review synced to CRM without rep check | Unexamined action; wrong data spreads | Rep confirms or corrects before sync |
| Live AI prompts never faded | Rep depends on the prompt; skill never internalizes | Fade prompts on a schedule |
