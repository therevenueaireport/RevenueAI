# T: Teach

The step-six playbook: the learner articulates, explains, and creates for someone else. Bridges Level 3 (Conscious Competence) to Level 4 (Unconscious Competence) and produces the deepest encoding in the cycle.

**Use this file when:**
- Skills "plateau and stop growing," or performance is fine but fragile.
- You need proof that the skill lives in the person, not in their tools.
- Designing peer teaching, teach-backs, certification, or creation exercises.
- You want the program to outlive the cohort: the next hire should be taught by this cohort, not by a new event.

---

## 1. What Teach is for, and what breaks without it

LOPAFT reframes teaching. It does not mean standing at a whiteboard. It means articulating what you learned: "Tell your partner what went well and what didn't." Teaching forces retrieval, forces synthesis, and reveals gaps the learner did not know existed. It is metacognitive by nature.

T is the proof point. If you can teach it, you own it. If you cannot, you do not, regardless of what AI helped you produce.

| Teach does | Failure if missing |
|---|---|
| Forces retrieval and reorganization of the skill | Skill stays a set of memorized moves; it does not adapt to new situations |
| Reveals hidden gaps (explaining exposes what you cannot explain) | Gaps surface later, on a live deal, at a worse moment |
| Proves the knowledge lives in the person | Performance looks fine while the tool or the script is doing the work |
| Builds organizational capability | Untaught skill stays fragile. When the person leaves the role, the organization loses the capability. |
| Restarts the spiral | Teaching shows the next layer of complexity, which becomes the next Learn |

## 2. The science you are applying

| Principle | Research base | What it means for Teach |
|---|---|---|
| Revised taxonomy of learning objectives | Educational-objectives research | Six cognitive process levels: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create. Teaching and creation exercises push learners to the top three. |
| Learning by teaching | Learning-science research | Preparing to teach and, more so, actually explaining to others improves the teacher's own learning. |
| Self-explanation effect | Cognitive-science research | Learners who explain material to themselves while learning understand it more deeply. |
| Generation effect | Memory research | Information you generate is remembered better than information you read. |
| Retrieval practice | Testing-effect research | Teaching without notes is retrieval practice under realistic demand. |
| Cognitive apprenticeship: articulation and reflection | Apprenticeship-learning research | The learner states their reasoning and compares it to an expert's. |
| Reciprocal teaching | Reading-comprehension research | Learners take turns leading: questioning, clarifying, summarizing, predicting. |
| Jigsaw | Cooperative-learning research | Each learner masters one piece and teaches it to the group. Everyone teaches. |
| Peer instruction | Classroom-teaching research | Learners answer, then convince a peer, then answer again. Explaining to a peer corrects misconceptions. |

### The taxonomy of learning objectives is not the Four Levels

Do not confuse the two. The Four Levels of Competency describe where a learner is on a specific skill (from not knowing the gap exists to doing it without thought). The revised taxonomy of learning objectives describes the cognitive demand of a task (from remembering to creating). Teach uses the taxonomy to design tasks that move a learner across the Four Levels.

### Taxonomy levels mapped to Teach exercises

| Taxonomy level | What the learner does | Teach exercise (revenue example) |
|---|---|---|
| Remember | Recall facts or terms | Not Teach. This is Learn and spaced retrieval. |
| Understand | Explain in own words | Teach-back: explain D3 and why it matters to a peer, no notes |
| Apply | Use in a new situation | Demonstrate D3 live in front of a peer, then explain each choice |
| Analyze | Break down, find causes | Walk a peer through a call: where D3 failed, why, and what caused it upstream |
| Evaluate | Judge against a standard | Score a peer's call and defend each score; judge an AI review and justify every correction |
| Create | Produce something new | Build a contrast pair, write a buyer persona, design a drill, or write the one-pager for the next hire |

Design rule: a Teach step that only reaches Understand is a teach-back, which is good but not the ceiling. Aim each learner's final Teach exercise at Evaluate or Create.

## 3. Design patterns

### Pattern A: The teach-back (Understand to Apply)

The minimum Teach. After a practice or live call, the learner explains to a partner: what went well, what did not, and why. No notes. The partner asks two questions.

### Pattern B: Peer coaching (Evaluate)

A rep at Level 3 observes and coaches a peer on one behavior, using the rubric and the one-adjustment rule. The coach learns more than the coachee: they must discriminate, diagnose, and explain. Have the manager review the peer coaching note.

### Pattern C: Jigsaw (Understand to Analyze)

Each rep owns one behavior (D1 to D5). They prepare a 10-minute segment on it: the standard, one contrast pair, the most common mistake, and one drill. Each teaches their segment. Everyone teaches; everyone is taught.

### Pattern D: Creation exercises (Create)

| Creation output | What it proves | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| A new contrast pair from their own calls, annotated | Can discriminate and explain the difference | O library |
| A buyer persona card with deflection lines | Understands how buyers resist this behavior | P practice set |
| A drill with a planted error (including an AI review with an error) | Can predict where others go wrong | P and F |
| A one-page "how I do D3" guide | Can articulate the skill as a method | Onboarding for the next hire |
| A 10-minute onboarding segment for a new hire | Can sequence the skill for a novice | Ramp program |

Teach outputs feed the program. That is how the library stays current without enablement building it alone.

### Pattern E: Gap detection through teaching

When a learner cannot explain a part of the skill, that is the finding. Capture it.

```
While teaching, I could not explain: ______
The question I could not answer: ______
What I think the answer is now: ______
What I will check (call, clip, coach): ______
```

### Pattern F: Teach the next cohort (spiral)

Learners who reach stress-tested Level 3 teach the next cohort or new hires. The skill moves from the program to the team. The teaching also exposes the next layer of complexity (multi-threaded discovery, partner deals), which becomes the next Learn.

### Pattern G: Safety in Teach

Teaching in front of peers is exposure. Keep the affective filter low:
- First teach-backs are 1:1 with a partner, not to the room.
- Group teaching only for reps at standard on that behavior.
- The audience asks questions; it does not grade.

## 4. Exit criteria: Level 4 signals

Teach targets Level 4. Level 4 is hard to see directly because the skill no longer needs conscious attention. Look for these together.

| Signal | Level 3 | Level 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Live performance | At standard with deliberate effort | At standard on complex calls while attention is on the buyer, not the method |
| Delayed checks | Needs reinforcement to hold | Holds at day-60 and day-90 checks |
| Explanation | Can explain the steps | Can explain the why, the exceptions, and when to break the pattern |
| Teaching | Teach-back with notes | Teaches without notes; answers unexpected questions |
| Evaluation | Scores peers accurately with the rubric open | Diagnoses a peer's root cause quickly and accurately |
| Creation | Needs a template to build materials | Builds new examples, drills, and personas that others use |
| AI independence | Relies on the AI review to notice misses | Notices misses before the AI review, and catches the AI's errors |

**Decision rules**
- If a rep performs at standard live but cannot teach it, treat the skill as fragile. Run teach-back and gap detection before declaring certification.
- If a rep can teach it but does not perform it live, the gap is Application, not knowledge. Go back to A.
- If a rep's teaching reveals a misconception, fix it privately first, then decide whether the misconception is common enough to add to the O library.
- When a rep reaches Level 4 on a behavior, re-enter LOPAFT at the next layer of complexity. What was Application at this level becomes Practice at the next.

## 5. Instruments and templates

### 5.1 Teach-back protocol (10 minutes, pairs)

| Min | Step |
|---|---|
| 0-4 | Teacher explains the behavior: the standard, why it matters, and one example from their own calls. No notes. |
| 4-7 | Partner asks two questions: one "why," one "what if the buyer..." |
| 7-9 | Teacher names one part they found hard to explain |
| 9-10 | Partner gives one piece of feedback on clarity |

### 5.2 Teach-back rubric

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Wrong or missing key elements | Mostly right, one key error | Accurate against the rubric | Accurate, with exceptions and edge cases |
| Why | No reason given | Generic reason | Connects to buyer and deal outcome | Connects to outcome and explains when not to do it |
| Example | None | Hypothetical only | Own real example | Own real example plus a contrasting one |
| Handling questions | Cannot answer | Answers with help | Answers both questions | Answers and extends the idea |
| No notes | Reads from notes | Checks notes often | Checks once | None |

Certification threshold (rule of thumb): 3 or higher on every dimension.

### 5.3 Peer coaching note (rep as coach)

```
Coach: ______   Peer: ______   Behavior: ______   Call/clip: ______
Peer's self-score: ___   My score: ___
The moment: [timestamp] ______
Root cause (not the symptom): ______
One adjustment I suggested: ______
What I learned about my own [behavior] by coaching this: ______
Manager review: [agree / adjust] ______
```

### 5.4 Creation exercise brief

```
Create: [contrast pair | persona | drill | one-pager | onboarding segment]
Behavior: ______   Audience: [peer | new hire | next cohort]
Must include: the standard, one real example, the most common mistake, one way to practice it
Constraint: produced by you, without AI drafting (see section 6)
Review: manager or enablement checks accuracy against the rubric
Destination: [O library | P set | onboarding] ______
```

### 5.5 Jigsaw session agenda (75 minutes, 5 behaviors)

| Min | Block |
|---|---|
| 0-5 | Frame: each person teaches one behavior; the room asks, it does not grade |
| 5-65 | 5 segments x 12 min: 8 min teaching (standard, contrast pair, common mistake, drill), 4 min questions |
| 65-72 | Each listener writes the one behavior they now understand better and why |
| 72-75 | Collect segments into the onboarding library |

### 5.6 Teach program audit

- [ ] Every learner has at least one teach-back scheduled.
- [ ] Teach exercises reach Evaluate or Create for learners at stress-tested Level 3.
- [ ] Teach outputs are reviewed for accuracy and added to the library.
- [ ] Gap detection is captured and acted on.
- [ ] First teach-backs are 1:1, not to the room.
- [ ] Certification includes a teach-back, not only a performance check.
- [ ] The next cohort or new hires are taught partly by this cohort.
- [ ] The teach act is done without AI producing the explanation.

## 6. Teach in the AI era

LOPAFT is explicit about AI at T: **no AI**. Teach proves the knowledge lives in the learner. A rep who prompts a model to write their teach-back has proven the model can explain the skill, not that they can.

This matters more now, not less. When agents draft the email, summarize the call, and suggest the next step, the scarce skill becomes judging the output. Judgment lives in the person. Teach is how you verify it is there.

| AI may | AI may not |
|---|---|
| Schedule teach-back sessions | Draft the explanation, the one-pager, or the segment |
| Record and transcribe a teach-back after it is delivered | Be used live during the teach-back for prompts or answers |
| Help enablement check a delivered teach-back's accuracy against the rubric afterward | Generate the contrast pair annotations the rep is supposed to write |
| Surface candidate clips the rep then selects and annotates | Answer the audience's questions on the rep's behalf |

**AI-era Teach exercises that prove human judgment**
- **Correct the machine, out loud.** The rep takes an AI call review of their own call, finds what it got wrong or missed, and teaches a peer why. This is Evaluate on the taxonomy and directly tests the second skill.
- **Build the planted-error drill.** The rep creates an AI review with a realistic error for others to catch. To plant a good error, you must understand where the machine fails.
- **Teach when not to use the tool.** The rep explains a situation where the AI output should not be acted on, and why.

## 7. Worked example (illustrative)

> **Program scenario (illustrative, used in all six step files).** A 12-AE mid-market team is rolling out a new discovery method and an AI call-review tool at the same time. Two frontline managers (6 AEs each), one enablement lead, one RevOps analyst. The method has five scored behaviors: **D1** Hypothesis opener, **D2** Layered problem questions, **D3** Quantified impact confirmed by the buyer, **D4** Buying process mapped, **D5** Dated next step agreed live. The AI tool scores every recorded call against D1-D5 and drafts a call review. The AEs' second skill is judging that review: catching wrong or missing scores before acting on it. Traffic light: red. Program: 10 weeks plus a day-90 check. The week-0 baseline (enablement-assess) placed most AEs at Level 1 or 2 on D3 and D4, and at Level 1 on judging AI output.

**Teach starts per AE once they hold at standard in Application (from week 6), with team-wide teach-backs in week 9 and certification in week 10.**

| Week | Teach activity | Taxonomy level |
|---|---|---|
| 2-5 | Informal teach-backs inside Practice triads ("tell your partner what went well and what didn't") | Understand |
| 6-8 | AEs at standard: peer coach one AE on one behavior per week, manager reviews the note | Evaluate |
| 8 | Each AE at standard builds one creation output from their own calls | Create |
| 9 | Jigsaw: five AEs each teach one behavior to the team; one AE teaches "correcting the AI review" | Understand to Evaluate |
| 10 | Certification: one live call at standard plus a 10-minute teach-back scored on 5.2 | Apply, Evaluate |
| Day 90 | Delayed check: live calls plus a fresh teach-back on a behavior chosen on the day | All |

**What happened (illustrative)**
- **Gap detection.** In week 6, an AE at standard on D4 in live calls could not explain why mapping the past purchase matters. Her teach-back scored 2 on "Why." She had been following the prep template's prompt without understanding it. A 15-minute conversation with the senior AE fixed it; her D4 performance on a harder multi-stakeholder call the next week held where it had previously slipped.
- **Creation outputs fed the program.** Week 8 produced 7 new contrast pairs, 3 buyer personas, and 2 planted-error AI reviews. Enablement reviewed them; most went into the library. The library roughly doubled without enablement building it alone.
- **The AI judgment segment** in the jigsaw was taught by an AE who had logged the most corrections on AI reviews. She showed three patterns where the tool erred and how to catch each. Two other AEs said it was the most useful segment.
- **Certification (week 10).** 7 AEs certified on all five behaviors. 4 certified on D1, D2, D3, D5, with D4 continuing in the feedback cadence. 1 AE on the extended timeline.
- **Spiral.** The two most recent hires were onboarded in the following quarter using the AEs' one-pagers and jigsaw segments, with the certified AEs as their application buddies. Teaching the new hires exposed the next layer: discovery on multi-threaded deals with procurement involved early. That became the Learn content of the next spiral.

**Day 90**

| Signal | Result (illustrative) |
|---|---|
| Live calls at standard without reminders | Most certified AEs held on all five behaviors |
| Teach-back without notes on a behavior picked on the day | Certified AEs scored 3+ on every dimension |
| Caught AI review errors before the review was opened | Common among certified AEs |
| Read | Level 4 signals on D1, D2, D5 for most; D3 and D4 still deliberate for several. Keep spaced touches; re-enter LOPAFT for multi-threaded discovery. |

## 8. Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping Teach | Untaught skill stays fragile; capability leaves with the person | Schedule teach-backs and creation outputs for every learner |
| Teach means "present to the room" | High exposure, few participants | Pairs first, jigsaw so everyone teaches |
| Teach-backs that stop at Understand | Misses the deepest encoding | Push to Evaluate and Create |
| AI-written teach-backs or one-pagers | Proves the model knows it, not the rep | No AI in the teach act |
| Certification on performance only | Misses fragile skill | Certification includes a teach-back |
| Creation outputs that go nowhere | Learners see it as busywork | Review and publish into O, P, and onboarding |
| Only top performers teach | Others never get the encoding benefit | Every learner teaches something at their level |
| Ignoring what teaching reveals | Misses the gap and the next spiral | Capture gap detection; feed the next Learn |
| Treating Teach as the end | LOPAFT is a spiral | Re-enter at the next layer of complexity |
