# L: Learn

The step-one playbook: knowledge transfer and WHY activation. Moves the learner from Level 1 (Unconscious Incompetence) to Level 2 (Conscious Incompetence).

**Use this file when:**
- You are designing the kickoff, pre-work, or first content of a red-light change (new motion, new methodology, AI tool rollout).
- Reps "don't know where to start," or say the change does not apply to them.
- A rollout produced attendance but no felt need to change.
- You need to turn a 60-slide deck into something adults will actually absorb.

---

## 1. What Learn is for, and what breaks without it

Learn does two jobs, and most programs only do the first.

| Job | What it means | Failure if missing |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge transfer | Concepts, vocabulary, the mental model of the new behavior | Reps cannot name what they are supposed to do. Observe and Practice have nothing to attach to. |
| WHY activation | The learner feels a specific gap between how they work now and what good produces | Learning stays theoretical. Reps nod, then go back to the old habit, because nothing told them the old habit costs them. |

LOPAFT is direct on this: most education stops at L. Learn is necessary and radically insufficient. A great Learn step does not create competence. It creates a learner who knows what they cannot yet do and wants to close it. That is the whole deliverable.

**The core trap.** Treating Learn as the program. The training event is where learning happens, not where behavior changes. If your plan is a kickoff plus a refresher a month later, you have a Learn step and nothing else.

**The second trap.** An information dump that tries to jump from Level 1 to Level 3. A 3-hour session covering every nuance overloads working memory and produces Level 1 learners who are now also tired.

## 2. The science you are applying

| Principle | Research base | What it means for Learn |
|---|---|---|
| Andragogy | Adult-learning research | Adults need to know why before what, bring experience that must be used, and learn when content solves a problem they have now. |
| Cognitive load theory | Instructional-design research | Working memory is small. Cut extraneous load (decoration, tangents, tool tours) so capacity goes to the core model. |
| Nine events of instruction | Instructional-design research | A sequence: gain attention, state objectives, recall prior knowledge, present content, guide, elicit performance, give feedback, assess, support retention and transfer. Learn covers the first four or five; the rest of LOPAFT covers the remainder. |
| Advance organizers | Educational psychology | Give the big picture first so new detail has somewhere to land. |
| Pretesting effect | Memory research | Attempting questions before instruction, even getting them wrong, improves later learning of that material. Also surfaces the gap. |
| Segmenting | Multimedia-learning research | Short, learner-paced chunks beat one long stream. |
| Affective filter | Language-acquisition research | Anxiety blocks intake. Surfacing a gap must not feel like a public verdict. |

### The six assumptions of andragogy, translated for revenue teams

| Assumption | Design move | Revenue example |
|---|---|---|
| Need to know | Open with the cost of the current behavior, not the agenda | "Deals where impact was never quantified slip at proposal stage. Here are ours from last quarter." |
| Self-concept | Treat reps as professionals who choose; give them control of pace and order | Self-paced pre-work; they pick which of their own calls to diagnose |
| Experience | Use their calls, deals, and objections as the content | Reps bring one lost deal and diagnose it against the new model |
| Readiness | Tie learning to a near-term task | Launch the week before a pipeline push, not during quarter close |
| Problem orientation | Organize around problems, not topics | Module titled "Why my deals stall after the demo," not "Module 3: Impact Questions" |
| Internal motivation | Connect to their goals: commission, time, craft, status | Show how the behavior shortens their cycle, not how it helps forecasting |

## 3. Design patterns

### Pattern A: Gap-first activation (the Level 1 to Level 2 move)

Getting someone to Level 2 is itself a teaching act. You cannot motivate someone to close a gap they do not feel. Surface it without shame.

1. **Private evidence first.** Each learner sees their own baseline (scored call, pre-test, a deal outcome) privately before any group session.
2. **Normalize.** State the team distribution, never individual scores. "Most of us are at Level 1 or 2 on this. That is the starting line, not a grade."
3. **Make the gap concrete.** One behavior, one artifact, one consequence. Not "discovery could be better."
4. **Give the name.** Vocabulary turns a vague feeling into a gap they can see. Once a rep knows the term "quantified impact," they can hear its absence on their own call.

### Pattern B: The WHY stack

Build WHY at three layers. Most programs only do the first.

| Layer | Question it answers | Who delivers it |
|---|---|---|
| Business WHY | Why is the company doing this? | Senior sales leader, 5 minutes, live |
| Role WHY | What changes in my week, my deals, my number? | Frontline manager |
| Personal WHY | What do I want out of this? | The learner, written in their own words |

Rule: if the learner cannot state the role and personal WHY in one sentence each at the end of Learn, the activation failed. Re-run before moving to Observe.

### Pattern C: Minimum viable model

Teach the smallest version of the model that lets Observe and Practice start. Everything else waits for later spirals.

- One-page model: the behavior, the 3-5 components, the standard.
- Rule of thumb: if the core model takes longer than 20 minutes to present, it is too big for this pass. Split it across spirals.
- Exceptions, edge cases, and advanced variants are the Learn content of the next spiral.

### Pattern D: Pre-work that diagnoses, not informs

Pre-work is a pretest plus a self-diagnosis, not reading.

- 5 scenario questions they answer before instruction (pretesting effect).
- One of their own recent calls or emails, reviewed against a 3-question prompt.
- One sentence: "The part of this I think I do least well is ___."

### Pattern E: Chunked delivery over a single event

| Format | Use for | Length (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|
| Live activation session | WHY, the model, gap surfacing, Q&A | 60-90 min |
| Micro-modules | Single components of the model | 5-10 min each |
| Written one-pager | Reference during Practice and Apply | 1 page |
| Manager huddle | Role WHY, local objections | 15 min |

### Decision table: how much Learn does this change need?

| Traffic light | Learn design |
|---|---|
| Green (informational) | A message, an email, or a short video. Learn is the whole intervention. Do not run LOPAFT. |
| Yellow (process adjustment) | Short Learn with an example (O) and light reinforcement |
| Red (behavior change) | Full Learn using the patterns above, followed by the full cycle. Learn alone will fail. |

## 4. Exit criteria: ready to move to Observe

Learn's target is Level 2. Do not wait for competence; that is not Learn's job.

| Signal | Level 1 (not ready) | Level 2 (ready for O) |
|---|---|---|
| Can name the gap | "My discovery is fine" | "I don't confirm the dollar impact with the buyer" |
| Vocabulary | Uses old terms or none | Uses the model's terms correctly in a sentence |
| WHY | "Leadership wants this" | States the role WHY and a personal WHY |
| Self-diagnosis | Cannot find the gap on their own call | Points to the moment on their own call where the behavior was missing |
| Knowledge check | Below threshold on concept questions | At threshold (set by you; rule of thumb 80% on concept items) |
| Emotional read | Defensive, dismissive, or overwhelmed | Uncomfortable but curious |

**Decision rules**
- If a learner passes the knowledge check but cannot find the gap on their own work, they are still Level 1 for practical purposes. Run a 1:1 gap review with their manager before Observe.
- If the room is defensive, you surfaced the gap too publicly. Switch to private evidence and manager 1:1s.
- If the room is overwhelmed, the model is too big. Cut to the minimum viable model.
- If learners are already at Level 3 (baseline says so), skip to Observe for calibration, then Feedback. Do not make them sit through Learn.

## 5. Instruments and templates

### 5.1 Activation session agenda (90 minutes)

| Min | Block | What happens | Instruction event |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Cold open | Play one 90-second clip from a real (anonymized) lost deal at the moment the behavior was missing. Ask: "What happened here?" | Gain attention |
| 5-12 | Business WHY | Senior leader, live, no slides. What is changing and why now. | Objectives |
| 12-20 | Pre-work debrief | Show the team's anonymized pretest distribution. Normalize. | Recall prior knowledge |
| 20-40 | The model | One-page model, components, the standard. Stop at the minimum viable model. | Present content |
| 40-55 | Their own evidence | Each rep reviews one of their own calls privately against the model. Writes the moment where the gap shows. | Guide learning |
| 55-70 | Pairs | Pairs share one gap each (their choice of which). Partner asks: "What would good have sounded like?" | Elicit performance |
| 70-80 | Role WHY | Managers in breakouts: what changes in your week, what support you get, what is measured | Motivation |
| 80-87 | Personal WHY card | Each rep writes the card below | Retention |
| 87-90 | What happens next | Show the path: Observe this week, Practice next week. Make clear Practice is not graded. | Transfer |

### 5.2 Personal WHY card

```
Name: ____________   Skill: ____________   Date: ________
The gap I found on my own work: ______________________________
What it costs me today (deals, time, commission, stress): _____
What changes for me if I close it: ___________________________
The first place I will use it: _______________________________
What would make me quit on this halfway: _____________________
```

The last line is for the manager. It predicts the mid-program stall (see enablement-momentum).

### 5.3 Pretest item bank (template)

Write 5-8 items. Mix the three types.

| Type | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | "Which of these is an example of [component]?" | "Which question quantifies impact?" (4 options) |
| Scenario | "The buyer says [X]. What is the best next question and why?" | "The buyer says 'reporting takes too long.' What do you ask next?" |
| Self-placement | "How would you describe your current use of [skill]?" | Rapid Level Classifier answers from enablement-assess |

### 5.4 Learn content audit checklist

- [ ] The first 5 minutes are about the learner's problem, not the agenda or the company.
- [ ] Each learner sees their own gap privately before any group discussion.
- [ ] The core model fits on one page.
- [ ] Every component has a named term.
- [ ] At least half the session time is the learner doing something.
- [ ] Learners' own work is used as content.
- [ ] Business, role, and personal WHY are each delivered by the right person.
- [ ] The session ends by showing what comes next (O and P), not with a quiz.
- [ ] Nothing in the session is a public ranking.
- [ ] A one-page reference exists for use during Practice and Apply.

**Scoring**: 9-10 = strong. 6-8 = workable, fix gaps before launch. Below 6 = this is an information dump.

### 5.5 Manager huddle script (15 minutes, day after kickoff)

1. "What did you notice about your own discovery yesterday?" (3 min)
2. "Here is what this changes for our team specifically." (4 min)
3. "Here is what I will and will not measure in the next four weeks." (3 min)
4. "What is going to get in the way?" Write answers down. (5 min)

## 6. Learn in the AI era

| Shift | Implication for Learn |
|---|---|
| Content is cheap to generate | The bottleneck is no longer producing modules. It is choosing the minimum viable model and the WHY. AI can draft; a human decides what to cut. |
| AI can deliver content on demand | Learn becomes less of an event. A rep can ask an assistant grounded in your approved content "what is the D3 standard?" at the moment of need. |
| AI produces baseline data at scale | Call-review tools can score every recent call against the new model before kickoff. Gap surfacing becomes evidence-based rather than self-report. |
| The skill shifts from producing to judging | When AI drafts the email, summary, or next step, the rep's new skill is judging the output. Learn must teach the standard well enough that reps can tell when the machine is wrong. |

**The LOPAFT division of labor at L**: AI delivers content; a human provides the WHY. Do not hand the WHY to an AI-generated video. WHY is credible only from someone whose opinion the rep cares about.

**AI-specific Learn rules**
- **Ground the assistant.** If you deploy an AI assistant for Learn content, restrict it to your approved material. An assistant that blends your method with generic internet advice teaches a different method.
- **Teach the tool and the skill separately.** For a combined rollout (new method plus AI tool), Learn the method first. A rep who does not know the standard cannot judge the tool's score against it.
- **Manual first.** Before reps use AI on a task they must later judge, they should do the task by hand enough times to see what good looks like. Learn should set this expectation explicitly.
- **Name the failure modes of the tool.** Part of Learn for any AI tool is "here is how it gets things wrong": misattributed speakers, missed nuance, confident scores on thin evidence.
- **Use AI baseline data privately.** An AI-scored baseline shown on a public leaderboard turns gap surfacing into surveillance. Same rule as human scores: private evidence, team-level distribution.

## 7. Worked example (illustrative)

> **Program scenario (illustrative, used in all six step files).** A 12-AE mid-market team is rolling out a new discovery method and an AI call-review tool at the same time. Two frontline managers (6 AEs each), one enablement lead, one RevOps analyst. The method has five scored behaviors: **D1** Hypothesis opener, **D2** Layered problem questions, **D3** Quantified impact confirmed by the buyer, **D4** Buying process mapped, **D5** Dated next step agreed live. The AI tool scores every recorded call against D1-D5 and drafts a call review. The AEs' second skill is judging that review: catching wrong or missing scores before acting on it. Traffic light: red. Program: 10 weeks plus a day-90 check. The week-0 baseline (enablement-assess) placed most AEs at Level 1 or 2 on D3 and D4, and at Level 1 on judging AI output.

**Where Learn sits in the program**

| Week | Step focus |
|---|---|
| 0 | Baseline: managers score 3 recorded calls per AE; Rapid Level Classifier |
| **1** | **L: pre-work, 90-min activation session, manager huddles.** O: library opens |
| 2 | O: calibration session. P: D1 and D2 isolation |
| 3 | P: D3 isolation. A: D1-D2 live |
| 4 | P: D4 isolation. A: adds D3. WHY re-activation event |
| 5 | P: integrated full call. A: full method on all first discovery calls |
| 6-8 | A plus weekly F. T begins for AEs at Level 3 |
| 9 | F: spaced retrieval checks. T: teach-backs |
| 10 | Certification: one live call plus one teach-back |
| Day 90 | Delayed check and Level 4 review |

**Learn decisions**

1. **Minimum viable model.** The enablement lead cut the methodology deck from 48 slides to a one-page model: D1-D5 with one sentence and one example question each. Advanced variants (multi-threaded discovery, partner-sourced deals) were moved to a second spiral after day 90.
2. **Pre-work.** Each AE got their own AI-scored baseline call privately, plus 6 pretest scenarios. No one saw anyone else's scores.
3. **Cold open.** A 90-second anonymized clip from a lost deal: the buyer said "this is costing us a lot," and the AE moved straight to the demo. The room was asked: "What happened here?"
4. **Gap surfacing.** In minute 40, each AE found the moment on their own baseline call where D3 was missing. The team distribution was shown as "most of us are at Level 1 or 2 on D3 and D4."
5. **Two Learn tracks, sequenced.** The method was taught on day 1. The AI review tool was introduced on day 3 in a 30-minute session that included three examples of the tool scoring a call wrong. AEs were told: "For the first two weeks, score your own call before you open the AI review."
6. **WHY stack.** The VP of Sales gave the business WHY (forecast slippage at proposal stage). Managers gave the role WHY in huddles (fewer stalled deals in your own pipeline, less late-stage discounting). Each AE wrote a personal WHY card.

**Exit check at end of week 1**

| Signal | Result | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 10 of 12 could name their own D3 or D4 gap in a sentence | Level 2 reached | Move to Observe |
| 2 AEs said "my discovery is fine" and could not locate the gap | Still Level 1 | Manager 1:1 gap review using their own call before the calibration session |
| 1 senior AE already performed D3 consistently on baseline | Level 3 on D3 | Skips D3 Practice; joins O as a calibration anchor and later T |

## 8. Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The three-day event | Learning happens; behavior does not change | Treat Learn as one step of six. Budget most time for P, A, F. |
| Agenda-first opening | Adults disengage before they know why it matters | Open with the cost of the current behavior |
| Public baseline reveal | Raises the affective filter; learners defend instead of learn | Private evidence, team-level distribution only |
| The complete model | Cognitive overload; nothing is retained | Minimum viable model; save the rest for later spirals |
| Tool tour as training | Teaches buttons, not behavior | Teach the standard first, then the tool as a way to meet it |
| WHY by memo | Business WHY from a slide has no credibility | Senior leader live, managers for role WHY, learner writes personal WHY |
| Teaching method and AI tool in one session | Learners cannot judge the tool against a standard they have not learned | Sequence: method first, tool second |
| Ending at Learn | Most education stops at L | Schedule O and P before the kickoff happens |
| Using AI to generate the whole curriculum unedited | Generic content, wrong emphasis, no local evidence | AI drafts; a human chooses the model, the examples, and the cuts |
