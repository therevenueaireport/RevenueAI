# LOPAFT Framework & Four Levels of Competency

LOPAFT framework by Jonathan Kvarfordt: an operating system for learning and mastery. This skill is the canonical reference for explaining it. Get it right every time.

To apply the framework to designing or auditing a real program, use lopaft-framework.

---

## The Four Levels of Competency

The diagnostic map. These tell you WHERE someone is. The framework uses a driving analogy throughout.

### Level 1 — Unconscious Incompetence
**"You don't know that you don't know."**

The 2-year-old in the backseat with a sucker. Completely oblivious that driving is even a skill. In a professional or academic context, this is the person who doesn't even know the gap exists — they haven't been exposed to enough to recognize what they're missing.

This is the hardest person to teach because they don't feel the pull to learn yet. There's no perceived problem.

### Level 2 — Conscious Incompetence
**"You become aware that you don't know something."**

The 8-9 year old who notices, "wait — they're doing something I can't do." Awareness is now present.

This is the inflection point. Getting someone to Level 2 is itself a form of teaching. In the AI context, this is the moment a student or professional realizes, "I've been missing something with how I use AI."

The job here is to surface the gap without overwhelming them — which is why LOPAFT starts with knowledge transfer, not practice.

### Level 3 — Conscious Competence
**"You know that you know — but you have to actively think about it."**

The 15-year-old with hands at 10 and 2, fully focused. They have the skill but it requires deliberate cognitive effort. In professional settings this is someone who can execute a new behavior but it's effortful, slow, or inconsistent without scaffolding.

This is where the LOPAFT cycle is most active — they're in the loop of learning, observing, practicing, applying, and getting feedback.

### Level 4 — Unconscious Competence
**"You don't know that you know — it's just instinct."**

The 25-year-old doing makeup, eating, and driving with a knee on the wheel. The skill has been internalized so completely it no longer requires conscious bandwidth.

This is mastery. The skill has moved from cognitive load to natural behavior. The explicit goal of the framework — in coaching and in AI curriculum alike — is getting people here.

## LOPAFT: The Operational Engine

The Four Levels tell you the terrain. LOPAFT is the vehicle. It's the 6-step cycle that bridges the gap from Level 1 all the way to Level 4.

### L — Learn (Knowledge Transfer)

Raw content delivery. Concepts, context, vocabulary. The framework is direct that many educators and enablement professionals stop here and then wonder why transformation doesn't happen. It's necessary but radically insufficient on its own.

In AI terms: explaining what a prompt is, why context matters, how models work probabilistically.

**Competency movement:** Moves someone from Unconscious Incompetence → Conscious Incompetence.

### O — Observe (What Good and Bad Look Like)

**This is the step most commonly skipped.** Before someone can practice, they need a reference point — a concrete example of excellent vs. poor execution. "Here's a good prompt, here's a bad prompt."

Without the O, practice becomes aimless. People try things but have no benchmark to orient against. This is where modeling and demonstration live. Having students contrast a custom-configured AI assistant with a raw, unconfigured one is a strong O-step exercise.

**Competency movement:** Deepens Conscious Incompetence — now they know what good looks like and can feel the gap.

### P — Practice (Safe Space, No Stakes)

Deliberate, low-consequence repetition. The framework explicitly frames this as "not a test, not your job depending on it." The effective filter concept (from language education) maps directly here — high anxiety (fight or flight) kills learning.

A group exercise where nothing is graded, a sandbox AI assistant they can break, a role-play scenario — all serve the P function.

**Competency movement:** Moves from Conscious Incompetence → Conscious Competence.

### A — Apply (Real-World Context)

Taking the practiced skill into an actual work or life situation. Not a simulation, not a classroom exercise — real stakes, real output. Using the finance tutor to prep for an actual exam. Applying AI to a live sales analysis or pipeline review.

This is where Conscious Competence gets stress-tested and either holds or reveals new gaps.

**Competency movement:** Stress-tests and solidifies Conscious Competence.

### F — Feedback (Calibration Mechanism)

A structured way to answer: "Am I moving toward good or toward bad?" The framework frames this as directional — you need to know which way you're heading, not just whether you "did it."

Can come from a teacher, a peer, or even AI itself. Without F, application degrades into reinforcing bad habits.

**Competency movement:** Accelerates the path from Conscious Competence → Unconscious Competence.

### T — Teach (The Multiplier)

The final and most powerful step. LOPAFT reframes teaching — it doesn't mean standing at a whiteboard. It means articulating what you learned: "Tell your partner what went well and what didn't."

Teaching forces retrieval, forces synthesis, and reveals gaps you didn't know existed. It's metacognitive by nature. When students explain it to someone else, they move from Conscious Competence into the early stages of Unconscious Competence.

**Competency movement:** Bridges Conscious Competence → Unconscious Competence (mastery).

## How They Interlock

The genius of the system is the loop. As the framework puts it: "It becomes a cycle over and over and over again."

As the skill scaffolds upward in complexity, you re-enter LOPAFT at the next layer. You don't go through it once and graduate to Level 4 — you spiral through it repeatedly, each cycle at a higher level of mastery.

**The Four Levels tell you the terrain. LOPAFT is the vehicle. The goal is always Level 4 — Unconscious Competence — but you only get there by running the LOPAFT cycle consistently, with enough scaffolding between iterations that people feel safe enough to keep going without shutting down.**

## LOPAFT × Four Levels Quick Reference

| LOPAFT Stage | What Happens | Competency Level Targeted | AI Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L** — Learn | Knowledge transfer: concepts, vocabulary, mental models | Level 1 → Level 2 (awareness of gap) | AI delivers content; human provides the WHY |
| **O** — Observe | See good AND bad examples; build reference benchmarks | Deepens Level 2 (what good looks like) | Compare AI outputs; expert vs. novice prompts |
| **P** — Practice | Safe, low-stakes repetition; no judgment, no grades | Level 2 → Level 3 (can do with effort) | AI tutors using question-first coaching; unlimited practice |
| **A** — Apply | Real-world context; real stakes, real output | Stress-tests Level 3 | AI as co-pilot; student provides judgment |
| **F** — Feedback | Directional calibration: toward good or away? | Accelerates Level 3 → Level 4 | AI: instant mechanical feedback. Human: judgment |
| **T** — Teach | Articulate understanding to others; forces synthesis | Level 3 → Level 4 (mastery) | NO AI. Proves knowledge lives in the student |

## Key Principles (Non-Negotiable)

1. **Most education stops at L.** That's the problem. Not AI. Not students. The system.
2. **O is the most commonly skipped step.** Without observing quality, practice is aimless.
3. **The danger of AI shortcuts is they collapse the entire LOPAFT journey.** A student who prompts an AI assistant to "write my essay" stays at L forever.
4. **LOPAFT is a diagnostic.** When faculty feel students are unprepared, ask: Which stage are they stuck at? Different blockages need different remedies.
5. **It's a spiral, not a line.** You re-enter LOPAFT at each new complexity level. Mastery of one layer becomes the L of the next.
6. **The affective filter matters.** High anxiety kills learning. Practice (P) must be safe. Stakes come at Application (A).
7. **T is the proof point.** If you can teach it, you own it. If you can't, you don't — regardless of what AI helped you produce.

## Common Misrepresentations to Avoid

These are WRONG. Do not use these:
- O does NOT stand for "Organize" or "Outcomes"
- A does NOT stand for "Analyze" or "Assessment"
- F does NOT stand for "Finalize" or "Frameworks"
- T does NOT stand for "Test," "Track," or "Tools"
- The Four Levels are NOT Novice/Intermediate/Advanced/Expert
- The Four Levels are NOT the levels of the revised taxonomy of learning objectives

The correct acronym is: **Learn, Observe, Practice, Apply, Feedback, Teach.**
The correct levels are: **Unconscious Incompetence, Conscious Incompetence, Conscious Competence, Unconscious Competence.**
