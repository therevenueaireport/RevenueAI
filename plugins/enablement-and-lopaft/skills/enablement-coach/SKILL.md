# Enablement Coach — Coach the Coach Skill

The best-designed program in the world, delivered by a coach who can't execute it, produces the same result as a bad program: nothing changes.

Coach the Coach is not a nice-to-have. It is the execution layer of every enablement initiative. Programs that move win rates work because a manager is given one skill to coach and the support to coach it well. Remove the coaching quality and you remove the outcome.

This skill operates in two modes:
- **Design mode**: Build manager capability before a program launches
- **Audit mode**: Diagnose and fix coaching quality in a program that is underperforming

---

## What Makes a Coach Effective in a LOPAFT Program

A LOPAFT coach is not a trainer. They are not a subject matter expert who delivers content. They are a behavior-change architect who:

1. **Observes with precision** — can identify the specific moment in an execution where the skill succeeded or failed
2. **Diagnoses with accuracy** — can distinguish the root cause of a performance gap from its surface symptom
3. **Delivers developmental feedback** — gives one specific adjustment, not a list of everything wrong
4. **Sequences coaching** — knows which skill to focus on and holds the focus (one thing at a time)
5. **Manages the affective filter** — creates safety that enables real performance, not performance-anxiety performance
6. **Runs spaced repetition** — maintains a cadence of reinforcement that matches the Forgetting Curve
7. **Self-assesses their own coaching** — knows what a good coaching session looks like and can evaluate their own

## The Coach Assessment: Where Is This Manager?

Before designing a Coach the Coach program, assess where each manager sits on the Four Levels for coaching itself.

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| Level 1 | Doesn't know that specific behavioral coaching is a distinct skill. "I give feedback all the time." |
| Level 2 | Aware that their feedback isn't landing. "I tell them the same thing every week and nothing changes." |
| Level 3 | Can execute the coaching structure when deliberate. Feedback is behavioral and specific, but it takes effort. |
| Level 4 | Coaching is natural. Managers automatically isolate skill, observe with precision, deliver one-adjustment feedback. |

**Quick diagnostic question**: Ask the manager to describe the last time they coached a rep on a specific skill. Listen for:
- Can they name the specific skill they were coaching?
- Can they describe a specific moment in a call/meeting where they observed the gap?
- Can they tell you the one adjustment they asked the rep to make?
- Can they tell you what they're expecting to see in the next session?

Four yes answers = likely Level 3. Fewer = Level 1-2. The coaching program starts where the manager is.

## Audit Mode Checklist — Coaching Quality Assessment

Use when a program is running and you need to assess coaching delivery quality:

**Coaching frequency:**
- [ ] Are managers holding dedicated coaching sessions (separate from 1:1s) at the designed cadence?
- [ ] Is the cadence matching the spaced repetition schedule (tighter early, spacing out as competence builds)?

**Coaching focus:**
- [ ] Are managers coaching only the skill designated for this phase (one thing at a time)?
- [ ] Is there evidence of coaching drift — managers bringing in adjacent topics, diluting focus?

**Observation quality:**
- [ ] Do managers have access to real performance artifacts (recordings, scored outputs)?
- [ ] Are coaching notes behavioral and specific, or vague and general?

**Feedback quality:**
- [ ] Does feedback contain one specific behavioral adjustment (not a list)?
- [ ] Does feedback reference the skill rubric / benchmark (tied to calibration standard)?
- [ ] Is there a feedforward commitment at the end of each session?

**Affective environment:**
- [ ] Do reps describe their coaching sessions as safe and developmental?
- [ ] Is there evidence of psychological safety in coaching conversations?

**Self-assessment:**
- [ ] Are managers self-scoring their own coaching quality?
- [ ] Are managers receiving feedback on their coaching from the program leader?

**Scoring**: 10-12 checked = strong coaching delivery. 7-9 = functional with notable gaps. Below 7 = coaching quality is likely the primary reason outcomes aren't moving.
