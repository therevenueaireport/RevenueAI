# Backwards Diagnostic: Worked Examples

> Three runs of **the Backwards Diagnostic by Jonathan Kvarfordt (Coach K)**. Acme is fictional and every number is illustrative. Back to [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

---

## Example 1: Win rate (new business)

**Situation:** Acme Software must grow new ARR from $10M to $13M this fiscal year. Leadership's first question was which AI SDR to buy.

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| 1. Outcome | New ARR $10M to $13M by fiscal year end. Owner: CRO. |
| 2. Drivers | Qualified opps: 400/yr. Avg deal: $50K. Win rate: 20% (was 26% two years ago). Cycle: 95 days. |
| 3. Lever | Scores: opps 4, deal size 3, **win rate 7**, cycle 4. Win rate has the biggest gap to Acme's own best period and is controllable. More opps at a falling win rate would mostly add cost. |
| 4. Gap | Cut by stage: win rate holds through demo, then drops sharply after the proposal stage. Cut by segment: drop is concentrated in deals over $75K. Cut by loss reason: "no decision" is the top reason in that band. |
| 5. Category | **Value** (primary). Evidence: large deals stall after proposal with "no decision"; business cases in lost deals contain no customer-specific numbers. Against Skills: top and bottom reps lose these deals at similar rates. |
| 6. Fix | A business case template built with the buyer's own numbers, required for every deal over $75K before proposal. AI at **Optimize** level: draft the business case from discovery notes for the rep to edit. AI SDR purchase parked; more top-of-funnel was not the lever. |
| Proof | Win rate on deals over $75K, against the last four quarters, reviewed at end of next quarter. |

**What the diagnostic prevented:** buying outbound volume to feed a funnel that was leaking at the proposal stage.

## Example 2: Ramp time (enablement)

**Situation:** Acme's sales leader wants new AEs productive faster. Proposal on the table: an AI roleplay tool.

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| 1. Outcome | New-hire AEs reach full productivity (75% of quota run rate, Acme's own definition) in 5 months instead of 8, for the next two hiring classes. |
| 2. Drivers | Time to first opportunity: 7 weeks. Time to first close: 5.5 months. First-90-day activity: on par with tenured reps. Conversion of first-quarter opps: half of tenured reps. |
| 3. Lever | **Conversion of new-hire opps** scores highest. Activity is already fine; time to first opp is reasonable. |
| 4. Gap | Cut by stage: new hires lose most deals at technical evaluation. Cut by rep: two new hires who shadowed a solutions engineer early convert near the tenured rate. |
| 5. Category | **Process** (primary), **Skills** (secondary). Evidence: onboarding has no structured time with solutions engineers; new hires run technical evaluations alone. The two who shadowed did fine, which points to exposure, not ability. |
| 6. Fix | Redesign onboarding: every new hire co-runs three technical evaluations with a solutions engineer in month two. Then add the AI roleplay tool at **Optimize** level for technical objection practice, in a safe space to fail. Process first, tool second. |
| Proof | Months to 75% quota run rate for the next class, against the last two classes. |

**What the diagnostic prevented:** treating a process gap (no exposure) as a skills gap and buying practice software for a step new hires were never shown.

## Example 3: Net revenue retention (customer success)

**Situation:** Acme's NRR fell from 112% to 101%. The CS leader wants an AI health-scoring platform.

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| 1. Outcome | NRR back to 110% on the cohort renewing in the next four quarters. Owner: VP Customer Success. |
| 2. Drivers | Gross retention: 90% (was 92%). Expansion rate: 11% (was 20%). Expansion deal size: flat. |
| 3. Lever | **Expansion rate** scores highest. Gross retention dipped slightly; expansion halved. |
| 4. Gap | Cut by segment: expansion collapsed in accounts under 200 seats. Cut by owner: those accounts moved from named CSMs to a pooled model last year. Cut by motion: no one is assigned to run expansion conversations in pooled accounts. |
| 5. Category | **Process** (primary). Evidence: the drop starts the quarter the coverage model changed; nobody owns the expansion step for those accounts. Against Fit: same product, same customer profile as before. |
| 6. Fix | Assign expansion ownership for pooled accounts to a named team with a usage-triggered play. AI at **Amplify** level: usage signals route expansion-ready accounts to that team with a drafted account summary. A full health-scoring platform waits until the play proves itself. |
| Proof | Expansion rate in accounts under 200 seats, quarterly, against the prior four quarters. |

**What the diagnostic prevented:** buying a platform to score health in accounts where the real problem was that no human owned the expansion conversation.

---

## Pattern across all three

| Example | First proposal | Real category | AI role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Win rate | AI SDR | Value | Optimize: draft business cases |
| Ramp time | AI roleplay | Process, then Skills | Optimize: practice, after process fix |
| NRR | Health-scoring platform | Process | Amplify: route signals to a named owner |

In each case AI still had a role. It just arrived after the outcome, the lever, and the gap were known.
