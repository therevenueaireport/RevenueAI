# Build a Campaign Brief

> From the [Revenue AI Report skill library](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/skills). Level: Start Here · Task: Build Campaigns · Setup: About 30 minutes · For: Marketing, Executive and Founder

**Outcome:** A one-page campaign brief with audience, message, offer, channels, and the number that defines success.

## When to use
- Kicking off any campaign with more than one channel or one person involved.
- When a campaign request arrives as a tactic instead of a goal.

## Inputs you need
- The goal: pipeline, meetings, or a named-account outcome
- The audience segment and one insight about them
- Budget range and timing

## Quick prompt
Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or any assistant. Replace the [bracketed] parts.

```text
You are a B2B campaign strategist. Draft a one-page campaign brief. Goal: [GOAL]. Audience: [SEGMENT and one insight]. Budget and timing: [paste]. Include: the single message, the offer, three channels ranked by fit for this audience, the one number that defines success, and what we will stop doing to fund this. Also list the three assumptions most likely to be wrong. No jargon. If the goal cannot be measured, say so and propose one that can.
```

## Process
1. Write the goal as a number with a date, or reject the request until it has one.
2. Generate the brief. Cut every channel that does not reach the named audience.
3. Pressure-test the success number with finance or RevOps.
4. Name what stops. A campaign without a stop list is a wish.
5. Review against the brief at the midpoint, not only at the end.

## Decision rules
- One message. If the brief needs two messages, it is two campaigns.
- A channel stays only if you can name the audience's behavior there.
- If the success number moved less than halfway by the midpoint review, change the offer, not the goal.

## Output
- One page: goal, audience, message, offer, channels, success number, stop list.
- Three named assumptions to watch.

## Quality checks
- The goal is a number with a date.
- The message is about the audience's problem.
- The stop list is real.

## Limitations
- The AI cannot know your channel economics. Validate channel fit with your own cost data.
- A brief does not fix weak audience insight. Bad input produces a tidy, wrong brief.

## Worked example
**Input:** Goal: 40 qualified meetings with RevOps leaders at 500+ person SaaS firms in Q4. Insight: they are being asked to govern AI tools they did not choose. Budget $60k.

**Output:** Message: you did not choose these AI tools, but you own the risk. Offer: a 20-minute governance teardown with a written kill-criteria checklist. Channels: LinkedIn to named accounts, a sponsored operator newsletter, one webinar with a practitioner guest. Success: 40 meetings; kill the webinar if it has not produced 8 by week four.

## Review checklist
- [ ] One message?
- [ ] Success number agreed with the budget owner?
- [ ] Stop list attached?
