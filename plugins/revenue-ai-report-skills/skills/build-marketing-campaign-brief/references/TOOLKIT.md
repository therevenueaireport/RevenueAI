# Campaign Brief Toolkit: Intake, Goal Math, Channel Fit, Stop List, and Midpoint Review

**Use this file when:**
- A campaign request just arrived as a tactic ("we need a webinar") and you need to turn it into a goal.
- You have a draft brief from an AI assistant and need to test the goal, message, channels, and success number before anyone spends money.
- You need to agree the success number with finance or RevOps and want the math on one page.
- You are at the campaign midpoint and need to decide: keep going, change the offer, or kill a channel.

The SKILL.md holds the process and the decision rules. This file holds the instruments.

---

## 1. The Sequence

| Step | What you do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Run the intake questions on the request | Goal as a number with a date, or a rejected request | 15 min |
| 2 | Back-solve the goal into a funnel | Goal math sheet | 20 min |
| 3 | Test the audience insight | One insight that passes the test, or a research task | 15 min |
| 4 | Write the single message and the offer | Message and offer, tested | 30 min |
| 5 | Score channels for fit | Three channels ranked, the rest cut | 20 min |
| 6 | Pressure-test the success number with finance or RevOps | Agreed number and kill criteria | 30 min meeting |
| 7 | Write the stop list | What stops, what it frees, who agreed | 15 min |
| 8 | Fill the one-page brief | Brief (Section 8) | 30 min |
| 9 | Run the midpoint review | Keep / change offer / cut channel decision | 30 min |

## 2. Step 1: Intake Questions (Turn a Tactic into a Goal)

When the request arrives as "we need an event," "let's do a webinar," or "can we run ads on X," ask these before writing anything.

| # | Question | What a usable answer looks like | If the answer is weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What business result should be different when this is over? | "Pipeline from mid-market RevOps buyers" | Ask what the requester will report upward |
| 2 | How many, by when? | "40 qualified meetings by 15 December" | Propose a number from Section 3 and ask them to accept or change it |
| 3 | Who exactly? | A segment a list could be pulled for | Narrow until a list can be built |
| 4 | What do we know about them that a competitor does not say? | One specific insight from calls, wins, or customer interviews | Treat as a research task before launch |
| 5 | What budget and people are available? | A range and named owners | Brief cannot be finalized |
| 6 | What would make you call this a failure? | A number below which they would stop | This becomes the kill criterion |
| 7 | Why this tactic? | A reason tied to the audience's behavior | Keep the goal, drop the tactic until Step 5 earns it |

**Decision rule (from the SKILL.md):** if question 2 has no answer after the conversation, reject the request until it has one. Write back with a proposed number so the rejection is helpful, not a wall.

### Goal types and the one number for each

| Goal type | The one success number | Usually the wrong number |
|---|---|---|
| Pipeline | Qualified pipeline value created by a date | Leads, impressions, registrations |
| Meetings | Qualified meetings held (not booked) by a date | Meetings booked, form fills |
| Named-account outcome | Number of target accounts with a meeting or opportunity by a date | Account "engagement score" |
| Expansion | Expansion opportunities opened in the install base by a date | Product usage clicks |
| Category or awareness | A measurable proxy agreed with the budget owner: branded search, direct traffic, or recall in buyer conversations | Reach, followers |

If the goal is awareness and no proxy can be agreed, say so in the brief. The Quick prompt tells the AI to do the same.

## 3. Step 2: Goal Math (Back-Solve the Funnel)

Use your own conversion rates from the last two to four quarters, pulled from your CRM and marketing automation system. Never use an industry benchmark the AI suggests.

```
GOAL MATH SHEET
Goal:                         [e.g., 40 qualified meetings held] by [date]
Meeting held rate (booked -> held, your data):          [%]   -> meetings to book:      [n]
Response-to-booking rate (your data, this channel type): [%]   -> responses needed:      [n]
Response rate (your data, this audience):                [%]   -> people to reach:       [n]
Addressable people in the named segment (list count):           [n]
Reach needed as share of the segment:                            [%]
Budget / meetings held = cost per meeting:                       [$]
Your historical cost per qualified meeting (if known):           [$]
```

**Reading the sheet:**

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Reach needed is more than the segment can supply | Goal is not reachable with this audience | Widen the audience, extend the date, or lower the goal. Pick one and write it down |
| Cost per meeting is far below your historical cost | Plan is optimistic | Find which rate is assumed higher than history and mark it as an assumption to watch |
| Cost per meeting is far above your historical cost | Expensive campaign | Justify by deal size or strategic value, or shrink scope |
| You do not have a conversion rate for a step | The goal rests on a guess | Use the most conservative rate you have and list it as assumption 1 |

## 4. Step 3: Audience Insight Test

The SKILL.md limitation: a brief does not fix weak insight. Test it before writing the message.

| Test | Pass | Fail example |
|---|---|---|
| Specific to this segment | Would not be true of every B2B buyer | "They are busy" |
| Comes from evidence | Traceable to calls, win/loss notes, customer interviews, or support tickets | "We think they care about AI" |
| Names a tension | Something they must do but cannot easily do | "They want efficiency" |
| Not already said by every competitor | Competitor sites do not lead with it | "Do more with less" |
| Would make one of them nod | A customer would say "yes, that is my week" | Any sentence a customer would not repeat |

**Where to find a real insight fast:** the last ten call recordings with this segment, the last ten closed-lost notes, recent support tickets, and two 15-minute calls with current customers in the segment.

## 5. Step 4: Message and Offer

### Message test

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| One message | If you need "and" to join two ideas, it is two campaigns (SKILL.md decision rule) |
| About their problem | Subject is the audience, not your product |
| Under 15 words | Rule of thumb. Longer usually hides a second message |
| Sounds like a person wrote it | Buyers discount content that reads as if nobody wrote it. Read it aloud |
| A competitor could not sign it | If it fits their logo, it is not yours |

### Offer ladder

Pick the offer that matches the buyer's readiness and the goal.

| Offer type | Asks of buyer | Fits goal | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Useful asset (checklist, template, benchmark from your own data) | Low | Reach, early pipeline | Downloads without intent |
| Working session or teardown (short, specific, written output) | Medium | Meetings | Must deliver real value, not a disguised demo |
| Peer session (small roundtable) | Medium | Named accounts | Attendance drop-off; over-invite |
| Assessment against their own data | Medium-high | Pipeline | Needs delivery capacity |
| Pilot or trial with a defined success test | High | Pipeline, late stage | Only for accounts already in motion |

**Decision rule:** the offer must deliver something useful even if the buyer never meets sales. If it does not, it is a demo request in disguise and response will reflect that.

## 6. Step 5: Channel Fit Scoring

Score every candidate channel. Cut any channel scoring 0 on "named behavior" regardless of total (SKILL.md rule: a channel stays only if you can name the audience's behavior there).

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Named behavior | Cannot say what this audience does there | General presence | Can name the specific behavior (reads X weekly, attends Y, asks peers in Z) |
| Reach into the named segment | Untargetable | Partial targeting | Can target the named list or role |
| Cost evidence | No cost data | Cost data from another audience | Your own cost per meeting or opportunity for this audience |
| Fit with the offer | Offer does not work there | Workable | Offer is native to the channel |
| Speed to signal | Signal after the campaign ends | By the end | By the midpoint |

**Rule of thumb:** keep the top three channels with a total of 6 or more. If fewer than three qualify, run fewer channels. Two strong channels beat three with one weak.

## 7. Steps 6-7: Success Number Pressure Test and Stop List

### Questions for finance or RevOps (30-minute meeting)

1. Is the goal math (Section 3) using our real conversion rates? Which rate would you change?
2. Which number will we both report at the end, from which system, with what definition of "qualified"?
3. What cost per meeting or per pipeline dollar would make this a bad use of money?
4. What is the kill criterion per channel at the midpoint?
5. Does the stop list free the budget and hours this needs?

### Stop list template

| What stops | Budget or hours freed | Owner who agreed | Date it stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| [e.g., monthly generic newsletter] | [$ / hours] | [name/role] | [date] |

**A stop list is real when:** each item has an owner who agreed, the freed resources cover a meaningful part of the campaign's cost, and the item actually stops on the date. A stop list of things nobody was doing anyway is a wish (SKILL.md).

## 8. The One-Page Brief Template

```
CAMPAIGN BRIEF: [Name]
Owner: [ ]   Budget owner: [ ]   Dates: [start] to [end]   Midpoint review: [date]

GOAL (number with a date)
  [e.g., 40 qualified meetings held with RevOps leaders at 500+ person SaaS firms by 15 Dec]
  Definition of "qualified": [agreed with RevOps]
  Reported from: [system/report]

AUDIENCE
  Segment: [list definition]   List size: [n]
  Insight: [one sentence]      Evidence: [source]

MESSAGE (one)
  [one sentence]

OFFER
  [what they get, format, time required]   Useful without a sales meeting? [Y/N]

CHANNELS (ranked by fit)
  1. [channel] Behavior there: [ ]   Score: [ ]   Midpoint kill criterion: [ ]
  2. [channel] Behavior there: [ ]   Score: [ ]   Midpoint kill criterion: [ ]
  3. [channel] Behavior there: [ ]   Score: [ ]   Midpoint kill criterion: [ ]

SUCCESS NUMBER
  [n] by [date]   Agreed with: [finance/RevOps name]   Goal math attached: [Y/N]

STOP LIST
  [item] frees [ ] owner [ ]

ASSUMPTIONS TO WATCH (three most likely to be wrong)
  1. [assumption]  How we will know by midpoint: [ ]
  2.
  3.
```

## 9. Step 9: Midpoint Review

Hold it at the halfway date. Use one page: progress against the number, per channel, with a stated cause for every material change.

| Situation at midpoint | Decision (SKILL.md rule and extensions) |
|---|---|
| Success number moved less than halfway | Change the offer, not the goal |
| One channel below its kill criterion, others on track | Cut the channel, move budget to the best performer |
| All channels weak, offer response weak | Offer problem. Change the offer |
| Responses good, meetings held weak | Handoff or qualification problem. Check speed to follow-up and the qualified definition |
| Meetings good, quality poor (sales rejects them) | Audience or qualification drift. Tighten the list |
| Ahead of plan | Check the qualified definition has not loosened, then consider extending reach |
| An assumption to watch proved wrong | Update the goal math and tell the budget owner now, not at the end |

**Rule:** every number in the review needs a proposed cause and a way to check it. Unexplained swings train the team to ignore reviews.

## 10. Red Flags

| Red flag | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Goal is "awareness" with no proxy | Nobody will be able to say if it worked | Agree a proxy or label the spend as unmeasured |
| Brief lists five or more channels | Spreading budget to avoid choosing | Score and keep three or fewer |
| Message has "and" in it | Two campaigns | Split or choose |
| Success number is registrations or leads | Activity, not outcome; this is how campaigns slide into [Optimization Theater, a concept from The Revenue AI Report](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/frameworks/optimization-theater): busy dashboards, no revenue | Move to meetings held or pipeline |
| No kill criterion per channel | Nothing will ever be stopped | Add one per channel |
| Stop list is empty or vague | Campaign is funded by overtime | Name items, owners, dates |
| Offer only works if they take a demo | Disguised sales ask | Rework the offer ladder choice |
| Content reads as machine-written | Buyers discount it on sight | Human edit with a named author voice |

## 11. Worked Example: Acme

*Fictional and illustrative. All numbers are made up for the example.*

**Company:** Acme, a 400-person B2B software company selling a revenue data quality product. The CMO receives a request from sales leadership: "We need a webinar in Q4."

**Step 1, intake:** the intake questions turn the request into a goal. Sales leadership wants more first meetings with RevOps leaders at 500 to 2,000 person SaaS companies. They will call it a failure below 25. Goal set: 40 qualified meetings held by 15 December. Budget range: $50-60k. Owners: one campaign manager, one SDR pod.

**Step 2, goal math (Acme's own last-three-quarter rates):**

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Meeting held rate | 80% -> 50 meetings to book |
| Response-to-booking rate (outbound plus content offers) | 25% -> 200 positive responses needed |
| Response rate for this audience | 4% -> 5,000 people to reach |
| Named segment size (list count) | 6,500 RevOps and revenue leaders |
| Reach needed | About 77% of segment |
| Cost per meeting at $55k | About $1,375 |
| Historical cost per qualified meeting | About $1,200 |

**Reasoning:** reaching 77 percent of the segment is aggressive. The team listed the 4 percent response rate as assumption 1, because it came from a different offer. Cost per meeting is close to history, so budget is plausible.

**Step 3, insight:** from ten recent call recordings: RevOps leaders are being asked to govern AI tools other teams bought, and they own the data risk. Passes the test: specific, evidenced, a tension, and competitors lead with "clean data" instead.

**Step 4, message and offer:** message: "You did not choose these AI tools, but you own the data risk." Offer: a 20-minute data-risk teardown with a written checklist they keep. Useful without a sales meeting: yes.

**Step 5, channel scores:**

| Channel | Behavior | Reach | Cost evidence | Offer fit | Speed | Total | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Named-account outreach on a professional social network plus email | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 10 | Keep |
| Sponsored issue of an operator newsletter the segment reads | 2 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 7 | Keep |
| Webinar with a practitioner guest (the original request) | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 7 | Keep, with strict kill criterion |
| Paid search | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 5 | Cut (no named behavior for this segment) |
| Trade show booth | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 2 | Cut |

**Step 6, pressure test:** RevOps agreed "qualified" means target persona at an in-segment account, meeting held, and next step booked. Finance set a ceiling of $2,000 per qualified meeting. Kill criteria: webinar cut if it has not produced 8 meetings booked by week four; newsletter cut if under 5 by week four.

**Step 7, stop list:** monthly generic newsletter paused for the quarter ($8k and 20 hours per month freed, agreed by the content lead); one low-performing paid social program ended ($12k, agreed by the demand lead).

**Assumptions to watch:**
1. 4 percent response rate holds with the teardown offer. Check: week-two response rate.
2. SDRs can deliver 50 teardowns in the window. Check: capacity plan by week one.
3. The webinar draws the target persona, not juniors. Check: registrant titles by week three.

**Midpoint (week six):** 18 meetings held, below halfway. Outreach slightly behind but strongest, newsletter at 6 booked, webinar at 3 booked. The team should have cut the webinar at week four; it was missed because no week-four check was on the calendar. Decisions: cut the webinar (below its kill criterion of 8) and move its remaining budget to outreach; change the offer for the non-responders to a shorter written checklist with an optional call. The goal stayed at 40.

## 12. AI Assist

### Split the work

| Task | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | Draft intake questions and a proposed goal | Run the conversation and get the number agreed |
| Goal math | Do the arithmetic from your rates | Supply the rates from your systems |
| Insight | Summarize call notes and loss notes into candidate insights | Choose the one with evidence |
| Message and offer | Generate options | Pick one, read aloud, edit into a human voice |
| Channel fit | Draft the scoring table | Supply cost data and named behaviors |
| Stop list | Suggest candidates | Get owners to agree |
| Midpoint | Summarize channel exports into one page | Make the decision |

### Follow-up prompts to use after the Quick prompt

1. "Here are our conversion rates by funnel step for the last three quarters: [paste]. Back-solve the goal into reach, responses, and meetings booked, and say what share of the segment we would need to reach."
2. "Test this audience insight: is it specific to the segment, does it name a tension, and would a competitor say the same thing? Here is the evidence it came from: [paste notes]."
3. "Write ten versions of the single message, each under 15 words, each about the audience's problem rather than our product. Flag any that contain two ideas."
4. "For each proposed channel, state the specific behavior of this audience there. If you cannot name one from the inputs I gave you, mark the channel as unsupported."
5. "Propose a midpoint kill criterion for each channel, expressed as a number by a week."
6. "List the three assumptions in this brief most likely to be wrong, and the earliest week we could see evidence for each."
7. "Midpoint review: here are the channel exports [paste]. Report progress against the goal, per channel, with a proposed cause and a way to check each change. No adjectives without a number."

### Errors this job invites, and how to catch them

| AI error | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Invented benchmarks (response rates, cost per meeting) | Numbers you did not supply | Delete and replace with your own rates, or mark as unknown |
| Channel lists that ignore the audience | Channels with no named behavior | Prompt 4 |
| Two messages blended into one sentence | "And," "while," or a long sentence | Prompt 3 |
| Generic insight | Would be true of any buyer | Prompt 2 |
| Stop list with nothing that costs anything | Items with no budget or hours attached | Require a freed amount per item |
| Polished copy that sounds machine-made | Reads smooth but says nothing specific | Human rewrite with a concrete detail from the evidence |

## 13. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Accepting the tactic as the goal | Brief starts with the channel | Run intake first |
| Using vanity metrics as the success number | Registrations, impressions, leads | Use meetings held or pipeline |
| No agreed definition of "qualified" | Marketing and sales report different numbers at the end | Agree it in Step 6 |
| Changing the goal at midpoint | Goal line edited after launch | Change the offer, not the goal |
| Reviewing only at the end | No midpoint date on the brief | Put the date on the page |
| Brief longer than one page | Nobody reads it twice | Cut to the template |

---

## 14. The 5-Minute Version

1. Write the goal as a number with a date. If you cannot, stop.
2. Write the one insight and where it came from.
3. Run the Quick prompt.
4. Delete every channel where you cannot name the audience's behavior.
5. Add one kill criterion per channel and one item to the stop list. Send to the budget owner.

## 15. The Deep Version

For campaigns with large budgets, a new segment, or board visibility.

| Addition | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Full goal math with three quarters of your own conversion data per channel | A success number finance will defend |
| Insight research: ten call recordings, ten loss notes, five customer calls | An insight with evidence, not opinion |
| Message test with five target buyers before launch | Catches the message that only makes sense internally |
| Holdout group (a slice of the named list receives nothing) | Shows what the campaign actually caused |
| Weekly one-page recap with stated causes | Midpoint decisions are not a surprise |
| Post-campaign review against the three assumptions | Next brief starts from better rates |
