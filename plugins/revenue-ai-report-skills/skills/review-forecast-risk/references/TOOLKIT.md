# Review Forecast Risk: Toolkit

**Use this file when:**
- You are about to commit a number upward and want to know which deals it actually rests on.
- The board, the CEO, or the CFO will see the number this week.
- Last period's forecast missed by more than ten percent and you need to show what changed.
- You run a forecast call and want managers inspecting the same evidence the same way.

The SKILL.md holds the logic. This file holds the instruments: evidence tiers, the stress-test sheet, how to rank the deciding deals, how to build the honest range, the board sentence, the accuracy log, and a worked example.

---

## 1. The sequence, with time boxes

| Step (from SKILL.md) | What you actually do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. List commit deals with why each closes | Export commit and best case. One line per deal in the rep's words | Commit list | 5 min |
| 2. Run the stress test | Fill the stress-test sheet (Section 3). Read the evidence-missing column first | Per-deal risk notes | 10-15 min |
| 3. Send managers for missing evidence | One specific evidence request per deciding deal, with a deadline | Evidence requests | 5 min (plus 24-48 hours) |
| 4. Set the commit at what survives | Apply the commit rule and range method (Sections 5-6) | Commit, range, board sentence | 5 min |
| 5. Record the call | One row in the accuracy log (Section 8) | Logged call | 2 min |

**The buyer's-words rule:** a deal stays in commit only if the buyer has confirmed the process and the date in their own words. A rep's summary of what the buyer said is not the buyer's words.

## 2. Evidence tiers

Classify the "why it closes" line for every deal. This is the single most useful thing in the review.

| Tier | Evidence | Examples | Commit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Written by the buyer** | Buyer stated the remaining steps and the date in writing | Email from the buyer listing approvals and signature date; mutual plan the buyer edited; procurement confirmation | Yes |
| **2. Said by the buyer, on record** | Buyer said it on a recorded call or in a meeting with a second person from your side, but not in writing; or written, with one step outside the buyer's control (their legal, their board) | Call recording where the economic buyer names the date; written confirmation that the contract is in their legal queue | Yes, flagged. Counts toward the deciding-deal evidence check |
| **3. Reported by the rep** | Rep's account of what the champion said, no buyer artifact | "Champion says paper process started"; "they told me end of month" | No. Best case until upgraded |
| **4. Hope** | Rep's belief with no buyer statement | "They love it"; "budget is there"; "it's a no-brainer"; "end of quarter push" | No. Name it a hope, per the Quick prompt |

**Tier 3 to Tier 1 is usually one email.** Ask the champion to confirm the remaining steps and date in writing. If they will not, that is information.

## 3. Stress-test sheet

One row per commit deal.

| Deal | Amount | Close date | Why it closes (rep's words) | Tier | What must be true to close on time | Evidence missing | Risk type | Ask (who gets what, by when) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | 1-4 | | | | |

### Risk types

| Risk type | What it looks like | Typical ask |
|---|---|---|
| Process unknown | No one has named the approval steps | Buyer email listing remaining approvals |
| Date unconfirmed | Close date set by the rep or the quarter-end | Buyer confirmation of date in writing |
| External queue | Waiting on their legal, procurement, security review, or board | Position in queue and expected turnaround, in writing |
| Single-threaded | Only one contact engaged | Meeting with economic buyer before close date |
| Paper not started | Contract not yet sent or not in their system | Confirmation that procurement has the paper |
| Amount at risk | Discount, scope, or term under negotiation | Agreed pricing in writing |
| Date slipped before | Close date moved one or more times | Reason for each move; new date in buyer's words |
| Competitive or no-decision | Alternatives or status quo still live | Buyer statement that the decision is made |

### What-must-be-true checklist (use for every deal in the deciding five)

| Question | Must be yes for commit |
|---|---|
| Is the economic buyer known by name and engaged? | Yes |
| Has the buyer named every remaining step (legal, security, procurement, signature)? | Yes |
| Does the buyer's date allow time for those steps at their usual pace? | Yes |
| Is the paper in their system? | Yes, or a dated step to get it there |
| Is pricing agreed? | Yes |
| Has the date slipped fewer than two times? | Yes; two slips moves it to best case unless Tier 1 evidence is new |

## 4. Ranking the five deciding deals

The deciding deals are the ones whose outcome changes whether the number lands. Rank by size of the swing, not by size alone.

| Factor | Weight in your judgment |
|---|---|
| Amount relative to the gap between commit and target | Highest: a deal larger than the gap decides the number alone |
| Evidence tier | Tier 3 or 4 in commit pushes a deal up the list |
| Risk outside your control (external queue, board approval) | Pushes up |
| Close date in the last two weeks of the period | Pushes up: no time to recover a slip |
| Slipped before | Pushes up |

**Rule of thumb:** if your five largest deals are all Tier 1, your deciding deals are the five largest. Otherwise, a smaller Tier 3 deal can outrank a larger Tier 1 deal.

## 5. Decision table

| If | Then | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer has not confirmed process and date in their words | Move to best case | SKILL.md rule |
| Two or more of the five deciding deals lack evidence | Take the range to the board, not a single commit | SKILL.md rule |
| Forecast missed two periods running | Change the process (definitions, inspection, evidence standard) before the next commit | SKILL.md rule |
| A deciding deal's evidence request is unanswered by the deadline | Treat as Tier 3; move to best case | Judgment |
| Close date moved twice | Best case unless new Tier 1 evidence | Judgment |
| Commit after stress test is below target | Report the gap and the best-case deals that could close it; do not pull best case into commit | Judgment |
| Rep pushes back on a downgrade | Ask for the buyer's words. If they arrive, upgrade | Judgment |
| Last period's forecast missed by more than ten percent | Widen the range and say why in the board sentence | Judgment |

### Red flags

| Red flag | What it means |
|---|---|
| Close dates clustered on the last day of the period | Dates set by the calendar, not the buyer |
| "Why it closes" lines read alike across deals | Reps are filling a field, not describing a deal |
| Commit equals target exactly | The number was set backward from the target |
| Large deal entered commit in the last two weeks | Either a real acceleration (rare) or pressure to fill the gap |
| No deal ever moves from commit to best case in review | The review is not inspecting anything |
| Amount fields mix contract value, annual value, and list price | The roll-up adds different units |

## 6. Building the honest range

Three numbers. Each has a stated method so the board can see how it was built.

| Number | Method |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Commit, minus each deciding deal whose remaining risk is outside your control (external queue, their board) |
| **Commit** | Every deal with Tier 1 or Tier 2 evidence. Nothing at Tier 3 or 4 |
| **High** | Commit, plus best-case deals with a dated, credible path, minus a slip allowance based on last period's accuracy |

**Slip allowance (rule of thumb):** if last period missed, assume at least one non-deciding commit deal slips even in the high case. Use your own history: what share of commit slipped last period and the period before?

### The board sentence

Template:

```
We are committing [$X], with a range of [$low] to [$high]. The range depends on
[N] deals: [one line each on the open risk]. We will know on [both/all] by [date].
[If last period missed:] Last period we missed by [x]%; this period we [what changed].
```

Rules: one sentence for the number, one for what drives the range, one for when you will know. No adjectives about confidence.

## 7. Worked example

**Situation (illustrative):** Acme, a fictional 400-person B2B software company. Quarter target $1.3M. Rep-submitted commit: $1.4M across 9 deals. Last quarter's forecast missed by 14%. Board meeting next week.

**Stress-test sheet:**

| Deal | Amount | Why it closes (rep) | Tier | Evidence missing | Risk type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | $300k | "Champion says paper process started" | 3 | Proof procurement has the paper | Paper not started? |
| B | $220k | "Waiting on legal since the 12th" | 2 | Their legal's position in queue and turnaround | External queue |
| C | $180k | Buyer email: approvals done, signature on the 24th | 1 | None | None |
| D | $160k | Mutual plan edited by buyer, date confirmed | 1 | None | None |
| E | $150k | Economic buyer named date on recorded call | 2 | Written confirmation | Date unconfirmed in writing |
| F | $130k | Buyer confirmed date on call with our VP present | 2 | Written confirmation | Single-threaded |
| G | $100k | Buyer email with date; new procurement step added last week | 2 | Turnaround of new step | External queue (small) |
| H | $90k | Expansion; buyer email with PO date | 1 | None | None |
| I | $70k | Buyer email with date | 1 | None | None |

**Deciding five, ranked:**
1. A, $300k: largest, Tier 3. Larger than the gap between the stress-tested commit and target on its own.
2. B, $220k: external queue, no date from their legal.
3. E, $150k: verbal only.
4. F, $130k: verbal, single contact.
5. C, $180k: Tier 1, but large enough that a slip would matter.

**Evidence check:** A and B both lack evidence. Two of five triggers the rule: the range goes to the board.

**Commit:** $1.4M minus A ($300k, Tier 3) = **$1.1M**. Below the $1.3M target. A sits in best case.

**Range:**
- Low: $1.1M minus B ($220k, external queue) = $0.88M, rounded to **$0.9M**.
- High: $1.1M plus A ($300k) if procurement confirms, minus a slip allowance of one non-deciding deal (G, $100k, given last quarter's miss) = **$1.3M**.

**Evidence requests sent:**
- A: AE asks the champion to forward the procurement confirmation or the PO request. Manager follows up in 24 hours. No email by Thursday: stays in best case.
- B: AE asks the buyer for their legal's position in line and expected turnaround, in writing.
- E, F: AE sends a recap email asking the buyer to confirm the remaining steps and date.

**Board sentence:** "We are committing $1.1M, with a range of $0.9M to $1.3M. The range depends on two deals: one in the customer's legal queue and one where we are confirming procurement has the paper. We will know on both by the 20th. Last quarter we missed by 14%; this quarter no deal is in commit without the buyer's own confirmation of process and date."

**Reasoning that mattered:** the rep total of $1.4M was above target, which is what made it dangerous. The stress test showed the number rested on one Tier 3 deal. Committing $1.4M would have repeated last quarter's miss with a bigger number.

## 8. The accuracy log

| Period | Rep commit | Stress-tested commit | Range | Actual | Error vs. stress-tested commit | Deals that moved | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | [ ] | [ ] | [low-high] | [ ] | [%] | [names] | [tier at review, reason] |

**Quarterly questions:**

| Question | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Did actual land inside the range? | Whether the range method is honest |
| What tier were the deals that slipped? | If Tier 1 deals slip, your evidence standard is too loose |
| Which risk type caused most slips? | Where to change the process |
| Was the stress-tested commit closer than the rep commit? | Whether the review is adding value |

## 9. AI assist: who does what

| Task | AI | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Classify each "why it closes" line into a tier | Yes | Check every Tier 1 and 2 against the actual artifact |
| Fill what-must-be-true and evidence missing | Yes | Add what you know from the deal |
| Rank the deciding five | Draft | Decide |
| Build the range | Calculate | Choose the slip allowance from your history |
| Verify a champion told the truth | No | Get the buyer's words |
| Write the board sentence | Draft | Own it in the room |

### Follow-up prompts after the Quick prompt

1. **Tiering:** "Classify every deal's why-it-closes line using these tiers [paste Section 2]. Quote the exact phrase that put it in that tier. Any line with no buyer statement is Tier 3 or 4."
2. **Hope detector:** "List every phrase in the why-it-closes lines that describes the rep's belief rather than a buyer's statement or action."
3. **Deciding five:** "Our target is [$]. Rank the deals that decide whether we hit it, by size of swing, not size alone. For each, give one evidence request: who asks whom for what, by when."
4. **Range math:** "Build low, commit, and high using this method [paste Section 6]. Show the arithmetic line by line."
5. **Board sentence:** "Write the board sentence in this template [paste]. No adjectives about confidence. Under 70 words."
6. **Process check:** "We missed last period by [x]% and the period before by [y]%. What process change, not deal change, would most likely have caught it?"

### Errors this job invites

| Error | How to spot it | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Treating rep summaries as buyer evidence | Tier 1 given to "champion says" lines | Prompt 1; demand the quoted phrase |
| Smoothing | "Generally solid commit with some risk" | Ask for the per-deal sheet, not a summary |
| Arithmetic errors in the range | Totals that do not reconcile with deal amounts | Prompt 4; re-add yourself |
| Pulling best case into commit to hit target | Commit equals target | Commit is evidence-built; the gap is reported, not filled |
| Ranking by amount alone | Deciding five are just the five largest, despite Tier 3 smaller deals | Prompt 3 |

## 10. Common mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Reviewing only the deals at risk | Tier 1 deals never checked | Check the artifact behind every Tier 1 |
| Evidence requests with no deadline | Requests open at the forecast call | 24-48 hour deadline; unanswered = Tier 3 |
| Giving the board a point number when evidence is thin | Two of five deciding deals unevidenced, single number sent | Range goes to the board |
| Changing deals but not process after repeat misses | Same miss pattern two periods running | Change the evidence standard or stage definitions |
| No record of the call | Nobody can say how accurate last quarter's review was | Log every call |

## 11. The 5-minute version

1. List the three largest commit deals. (1 min)
2. For each: is there a buyer email confirming process and date? (2 min)
3. Any without one: move to best case. (1 min)
4. Tell the board the commit that remains, plus "and [deal] is the swing." (1 min)

## 12. The deep version

For a board quarter, a funding event, or after two misses.

| Add | How |
|---|---|
| Artifact audit | Open the actual email, recording, or mutual plan behind every Tier 1 and 2 claim |
| Slip history per deal | Count close-date moves and stage regressions in the period |
| Rep-level accuracy | Each rep's commit vs. actual over the last four periods; weight their commit accordingly |
| Call evidence | Search recorded calls on deciding deals for the economic buyer's own statements on process and date |
| Process change | If two misses: redefine forecast categories by evidence tier and train managers on the sheet |
| Pre-mortem | Ask: "It is the last day of the period and we missed. Which deal did it?" Then get evidence on that deal |
