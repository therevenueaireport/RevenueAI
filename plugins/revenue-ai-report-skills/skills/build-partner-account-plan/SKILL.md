# Build a Partner Account Plan

> From the [Revenue AI Report skill library](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/skills). Level: Intermediate · Task: Grow Accounts · Setup: About 30 minutes · For: Partnerships, Sales Leader

**Outcome:** A one-page plan for a partnership: the shared customer, what each side gives and gets, and the first joint play.

## When to use
- Starting a partnership that has a signed agreement but no plan.
- Quarterly reviews of partners that signed and went quiet.

## Inputs you need
- The partner's business and who they serve
- Three shared or target accounts
- What you can offer: integration, services, co-selling, content

## Quick prompt
Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or any assistant. Replace the [bracketed] parts.

```text
You are a partnerships lead. Here is a partner: [paste their business and customers]. Shared or target accounts: [paste]. What we can offer: [paste]. Draft a one-page partner plan: the shared customer profile we both win with, what each side gives and gets, the first joint play with a named account, how we will measure the partnership at 90 days, and the most likely reason it produces nothing. Keep it to what both sides will actually do.
```

## Process
1. Define the shared customer. If you cannot, the partnership is a logo swap.
2. Write gives and gets for both sides. Asymmetry kills partnerships.
3. Name one account and one play. Do it before building anything bigger.
4. Set the 90-day measure: shared pipeline, not meetings held.
5. Review at 90 days and either scale or end it honestly.

## Decision rules
- No shared customer profile means no partnership work yet.
- If one side gives more than it gets for two quarters, renegotiate or exit.
- Measure shared pipeline and revenue. Meeting counts are activity, not partnership health.

## Output
- One page: shared customer, gives and gets, first joint play, 90-day measure, failure risk.

## Quality checks
- Gives and gets balance across both sides.
- The first play names a real account.
- The measure is pipeline or revenue, not meetings.

## Limitations
- The plan cannot create partner commitment. A plan without an engaged counterpart is fiction.
- Attribution between partners stays hard. Agree on the counting rule early.

## Worked example
**Input:** Partner: a RevOps consultancy serving the same mid-market SaaS firms. Shared accounts: 4. We offer the platform; they offer implementation.

**Output:** Shared customer: 300 to 1,500 person SaaS firms rebuilding revenue process after a leadership change. We give platform and co-marketing; they give implementation and their client introductions. First play: joint audit offer to named account Brindle Co. 90-day measure: 3 shared opportunities sourced. Failure risk: their consultants bill by the hour and deprioritize anything that does not generate services work this quarter.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Shared customer defined?
- [ ] Gives and gets balanced?
- [ ] A named account in the first play?
