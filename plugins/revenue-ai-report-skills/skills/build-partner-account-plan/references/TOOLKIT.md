# Partner Plan Toolkit: Shared Customer Test, Gives and Gets Ledger, First Play, and 90-Day Review

**Use this file when:**
- A partnership agreement is signed and nobody has written down what either side will do next.
- A partner signed, made introductions for a month, and went quiet, and you need to decide whether to revive or end it.
- You have an AI-drafted partner plan and need to test whether it is real or a logo swap.
- You need a counting rule for partner pipeline before the first argument about attribution.

The SKILL.md holds the process and the decision rules. This file holds the instruments.

---

## 1. The Sequence

| Step | What you do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pull the account overlap and partner facts | Overlap list and partner fact sheet | 1 hour |
| 2 | Run the shared customer test | Shared customer profile, or "not yet" | 30 min |
| 3 | Fill the gives and gets ledger, both sides | Balanced ledger | 30 min |
| 4 | Pick one account and one play | First play card | 30 min |
| 5 | Agree the counting rule and the 90-day measure | Written counting rule | 20 min, with the partner |
| 6 | Fill the one-page plan and review it with the partner | Plan both sides signed off | 45 min joint session |
| 7 | Run the 30-day check and the 90-day review | Scale / fix / end decision | 30 min each |

**The counterpart rule:** the plan is written with a named person on the partner side, not about them. A plan without an engaged counterpart is fiction (SKILL.md limitation).

## 2. Step 1: Data Checklist

| What to pull | Source (category) | How far back | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your customers and open opportunities in the partner's market | CRM | Current, plus 12 months closed | Basis for overlap |
| The partner's customers or target list (shared under the agreement, or matched by an account-mapping tool) | Partner, or a neutral account-mapping tool | Current | Overlap without sharing full lists |
| Deals where the partner was involved, even informally | CRM partner field, opportunity notes, ask reps | 12-24 months | Shows whether co-selling already happens |
| Integration usage among shared customers | Product analytics | Last 2 quarters | Evidence of joint value |
| Partner's revenue model (services hours, licenses, resale margin, referral fee) | Partner conversation, agreement | Current | Predicts what they will actually prioritize |
| Partner's sales team size and who covers your segment | Partner conversation | Current | Capacity for a joint play |
| Agreement terms: referral fees, deal registration, exclusivity | Contract | Current | Constraints on the play and the counting rule |

**Questions to ask the partner counterpart:**
1. Which of your clients would be better off if they also used us? Why those?
2. How does your team get paid, and what does a good quarter look like for them?
3. What would make your people bring us into a deal without being asked?
4. What has gone wrong with other partnerships you have had like this one?
5. Who on your side will own this, with how many hours a month?
6. What would you need to see at 90 days to keep investing?

## 3. Step 2: Shared Customer Test

The SKILL.md rule: no shared customer profile means no partnership work yet. Run all five checks.

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Same buyer | Both sides sell to the same role or a role that sits next to it | Different buyers in the same company |
| Same trigger | A shared event makes both offerings urgent (new leader, system change, growth stage) | Each side's trigger is unrelated |
| Real overlap | At least a handful of shared customers or open accounts (rule of thumb: 3+) | Zero overlap and no shared target list |
| Joint value | The customer gets something from the combination they cannot get from either alone | Two products in the same account with no connection |
| Both win in the same deal | Each side gains revenue when the joint play works | One side's win is the other's distraction |

**Reading the test:**

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 5 of 5 | Real shared customer | Write the profile, continue |
| 3-4 of 5 | Plausible | Continue, but put the failed checks in the risk section |
| 0-2 of 5 | Logo swap | Stop partnership work. Agree a narrow test or end politely |

### Shared customer profile template

```
SHARED CUSTOMER
Size:            [band]
Segment:         [industry, region]
Buyer roles:     Ours: [role]   Theirs: [role]
Shared trigger:  [event]
Joint value:     [what the combination does that neither does alone, one sentence]
Overlap today:   [n] shared customers, [n] shared open accounts
```

## 4. Step 3: Gives and Gets Ledger

Asymmetry kills partnerships (SKILL.md). Make the ledger explicit and score each item by what it costs the giver and what it is worth to the receiver.

| Item | Given by | Received by | Cost to giver (L/M/H) | Value to receiver (L/M/H) | Owner | By when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [e.g., introductions to 3 clients] | Partner | Us | M | H | [name] | [date] |

**Common gives, by side:**

| You can give | Partner can give |
|---|---|
| Integration or technical support | Implementation or services capacity |
| Referral fee or margin | Introductions to their clients |
| Leads that need services you do not offer | Credibility with a buyer you cannot reach |
| Co-marketing budget and content | Practitioner voice for content and events |
| Enablement for their team | Field insight on what clients actually struggle with |
| Deal registration protection | Access to their sales team's pipeline reviews |

**Balance rule of thumb:** each side should give at least one item that is medium or high cost to it and medium or high value to the other. If all of one side's gives are low cost, that side is not invested.

**The two-quarter rule (SKILL.md):** if one side gives more than it gets for two quarters, renegotiate or exit. Track the ledger each quarter, not only at signing.

## 5. Step 4: The First Play Card

One named account, one play, before anything bigger.

```
FIRST PLAY
Account:          [real, named account from the overlap list]
Why this account: [shared trigger present? relationship on either side?]
Who has the relationship: [our contact / their contact]
The play:         [e.g., joint audit offer, co-led workshop, introduction to economic buyer]
Partner does:     [specific action, owner, date]
We do:            [specific action, owner, date]
What the customer gets: [in their terms]
Success:          [opportunity created with value, by date]
If it stalls:     [what we try next, by when]
```

### Play menu

| Play | Best when | Partner does | You do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joint assessment or audit | Shared trigger is present, buyer needs diagnosis | Leads the assessment | Provides data, tooling, follow-up |
| Introduction to an existing client | Partner has trust in the account | Makes the introduction with context | Prepares a specific reason to meet |
| Co-sell on an open deal | You have an open opportunity in a partner client | Joins a call as the implementation voice | Leads the deal |
| Reverse introduction | You have a customer who needs services | Scopes services | Introduces and endorses |
| Joint workshop for a small group | Several shared targets with the same trigger | Co-presents practitioner content | Hosts, invites, follows up |

**Choosing the first account (rule of thumb):** pick the account where both sides have a relationship and the shared trigger is visible now. Do not pick the biggest logo; pick the one most likely to produce an opportunity inside 90 days.

## 6. Step 5: Counting Rule and the 90-Day Measure

Attribution between partners stays hard (SKILL.md). Agree the rule in writing before the first deal. The partnerships failure named in [the Eight Seats, a framework from The Revenue AI Report](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/frameworks/eight-seats) is influenced revenue claimed twice because no rule separates partner lift from direct. The counting rule below is the guard against it.

| Term | Definition to agree |
|---|---|
| Partner-sourced | Partner introduced an account with no open opportunity on our side, and an opportunity was created within [n] days |
| Partner-influenced | Partner joined at least one customer-facing meeting or delivered work on an opportunity that already existed |
| Shared pipeline | Value of open opportunities that are sourced or influenced, counted once, in our CRM |
| Where it is recorded | CRM partner field, filled by the opportunity owner at the stage where the partner engages |
| Disputes | Decided by [named owners on both sides] within [n] days |

**The 90-day measure:** shared pipeline created (count and value) and, if any deals close, shared revenue. Meetings held are activity, not partnership health (SKILL.md rule). Set the number with the partner: rule of thumb, start small enough that the first play alone could hit it.

## 8. 30-Day Check and 90-Day Review

### 30-day check (15-30 minutes, both owners)

| Question | Healthy answer |
|---|---|
| Did each side do what it committed for the first play? | Yes, on dates |
| Has the named account had a real conversation? | Yes, with a next step |
| Has the counting rule been used on any opportunity? | Yes, or no opportunity yet with a reason |
| Is the partner owner still the owner? | Yes |

### 90-day decision table

| Situation at 90 days | Decision |
|---|---|
| Measure hit, both sides delivered gives | Scale: add a second play and more accounts |
| Measure missed, both sides delivered gives | Wrong play or wrong account. Change the play, keep the partner, set a new 90 days |
| Measure missed, one side did not deliver | Ledger problem. Renegotiate gives or reduce scope |
| One side has given more than it got for two quarters | Renegotiate or exit (SKILL.md rule) |
| Partner owner changed or disappeared | Find a new owner within 30 days or pause |
| No shared pipeline and no activity | End it honestly. A quiet partnership costs attention |

**Ending honestly:** write a short note to the partner saying what you tried, what happened, and that you will revisit if a shared account appears. Close the plan in your system so it stops appearing in reviews.

## 9. Red Flags

| Red flag | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Plan has no named account | Nobody wants to spend a relationship | Do not proceed until one is named |
| Partner's revenue model rewards something else this quarter | Their people will deprioritize your play | Design a play that also produces their kind of revenue |
| All gives on one side are "co-marketing" | Low-cost gives, low commitment | Ask for one medium or high cost give |
| Measure is "number of meetings" or "joint webinars" | Activity, not health | Move to shared pipeline |
| Only executives are engaged, no field people | Partnership exists on paper only | Get a field owner on each side |
| You cannot get their client list or an overlap match | Low trust or contract limits | Use a neutral account-mapping approach or a narrow target list |
| Partner proposes building something big first (joint product, big event) | Avoiding the hard first account | Run one play first |

## 10. Worked Example: Acme

*Fictional and illustrative. Names and numbers are made up for the example.*

**Company:** Acme, a 400-person B2B software company selling a revenue planning platform. Partner: "Partner P," a 60-person RevOps consultancy serving mid-market SaaS firms. Agreement signed four months ago; two introductions, nothing since.

**Step 1, facts:** account matching shows 4 shared customers and 11 shared open targets. Partner P earns revenue from project hours. Their consultants are measured on utilization. They have 8 consultants covering Acme's segment.

**Step 2, shared customer test:**

| Check | Result | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Same buyer | Pass | Both sell to the head of RevOps |
| Same trigger | Pass | New revenue leader rebuilding process |
| Real overlap | Pass | 4 shared customers, 11 shared targets |
| Joint value | Pass | Platform works better with a process redesign; redesign sticks with a platform |
| Both win in the same deal | Partial | Partner P wins only if the deal includes paid implementation |

4 of 5. Continue, with the partial check named as the main risk.

**Step 3, ledger:**

| Item | Given by | Cost to giver | Value to receiver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implementation referrals on Acme deals in segment | Acme | M | H |
| Enablement for 8 consultants | Acme | M | M |
| Introductions to 3 clients with the trigger | Partner P | M | H |
| A consultant joins the first discovery call on co-sell deals | Partner P | H (billable hours lost) | H |
| Co-written practitioner guide | Both | L | L |

Balance: both sides have medium or high cost gives. The co-written guide is low value and was moved to "later."

**Step 4, first play:** Account "Customer X," a 900-person SaaS firm, new CRO in seat three months, Partner P already running a small project there. Play: joint revenue process audit. Partner P leads the audit (paid, small scope); Acme supplies planning data analysis and a follow-up proposal. Success: Acme opportunity created by day 60.

**Step 5, counting rule:** partner-sourced if Partner P introduces an account with no open Acme opportunity and an opportunity is created within 60 days. Recorded in the CRM partner field. Disputes go to both partnership owners.

**90-day measure:** 3 shared opportunities created, at least one partner-sourced.

**Failure risk:** Partner P's consultants are measured on billable hours and will deprioritize anything that does not create services work this quarter. Early signal: introductions arrive without context or a scheduled meeting. Response: every play includes a paid scope for Partner P.

**90-day review:** 2 shared opportunities created (the audit account and one introduction). Both sides delivered their gives. Decision: measure missed by one, gives delivered, so keep the partner and change the play: add a joint workshop for four shared targets with the same trigger. New measure set for the next 90 days.

## 11. AI Assist

### Split the work

| Task | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap analysis | Match and rank shared accounts from lists you supply | Decide which lists may be shared under the agreement |
| Shared customer test | Draft the profile and test results | Confirm with reps and the partner counterpart |
| Gives and gets | Propose items and balance check | Negotiate with the partner |
| First play | Draft the play card and outreach | Choose the account; own the relationship |
| Counting rule | Draft definitions | Agree in writing with the partner |
| 90-day review | Summarize CRM data and notes | Make the scale, fix, or end decision |

### Follow-up prompts to use after the Quick prompt

1. "Run this five-check shared customer test on the plan: same buyer, same trigger, real overlap, joint value, both win in the same deal. Mark each pass, partial, or fail with the evidence from my inputs."
2. "Build a gives and gets ledger with cost to giver and value to receiver for each item. Flag any side whose gives are all low cost."
3. "The partner earns revenue by [model]. Rewrite the first play so the partner's people earn their kind of revenue from it."
4. "Rank these shared accounts for the first play by: both sides have a relationship, shared trigger visible now, likely to create an opportunity in 90 days. Explain each rank."
5. "Draft a counting rule for partner-sourced and partner-influenced pipeline, including where it is recorded and how disputes are settled."
6. "Write the most likely reason this partnership produces nothing, the earliest signal we would see, and what we would do."

### Errors this job invites, and how to catch them

| AI error | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Generic "synergies" with no shared buyer | Profile does not name a role or trigger | Prompt 1 |
| Ledger that ignores the partner's revenue model | Partner gives are all unpaid effort | Prompt 3 |
| Invented accounts or invented overlap | Accounts not in your pasted lists | Check every account name against the input |
| Activity measures (meetings, webinars) | Measure lacks pipeline or revenue | Replace with shared pipeline |
| A plan the partner never saw | Written in third person about the partner | Review with the counterpart before calling it final |

## 12. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Building a big launch before one deal | Plan starts with an event or integration roadmap | Run one named play first |
| No counting rule until the first dispute | Both sides claim the same deal | Agree it in Step 5 |
| Treating signature as success | Partnership reported as "live" with no pipeline | Report shared pipeline only |
| Keeping dead partnerships open | Partner list grows, pipeline does not | End honestly at 90 days |
| Ignoring how the partner's people get paid | Introductions dry up after the first month | Align the play with their revenue model |

---

## 13. The 5-Minute Version

1. Write the shared customer in one sentence. If you cannot, stop here.
2. Write one give and one get for each side.
3. Name one account and the one action each side takes this month.
4. Set the 90-day measure as shared opportunities created.
5. Send it to your partner counterpart and ask what they would change.

## 14. The Deep Version

For strategic partners, resale agreements, or partners in a board-level plan.

| Addition | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Full account overlap match with ranking | A target list for several plays, not one |
| Interview three of the partner's field people | Real view of what they will actually do |
| Ledger reviewed quarterly with both executive sponsors | Early warning on asymmetry |
| Partner enablement with a short certification | Their people can position you without you in the room |
| Joint pipeline review every month | Shared pipeline becomes a managed number |
| Written exit terms | Ending is a decision, not a drift |
