# Prepare a Customer QBR: Toolkit

**Use this file when:**
- An executive-facing QBR is on the calendar and the last one was a product usage tour.
- Renewal is within two quarters and you need the customer's executive to see value in their own terms.
- Something went wrong this quarter and you have to say so without losing the room.
- A new executive sponsor has arrived and this QBR is their first impression of you.

The SKILL.md holds the logic: results first, misses second, plan third, ask last. This file holds the prep schedule, the data pull, the templates, the question bank, and a full worked QBR.

---

## 1. Prep schedule

| When | What you do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| T-21 days | Confirm date, attendees, and the executive who must attend. Re-read the outcomes they bought | Attendee list, outcome list |
| T-14 days | Pull the data (Section 2). Translate each metric into customer terms (Section 3) | Results scorecard draft |
| T-10 days | Write the miss cards (Section 5). Name the one decision (Section 7) | Misses with fixes, the ask |
| T-7 days | Pre-call with your champion: test the results, the misses, and the ask | Champion's corrections |
| T-5 days | Send the agenda and pre-read (Section 6) | Agenda in their inbox |
| T-2 days | Rehearse the three hard questions out loud with your manager playing the executive | Answers that fit in 60 seconds |
| T+1 day | Send the recap with the decision recorded (Section 10) | Decision log entry |

**The champion pre-call rule:** never let the executive be the first person on the customer side to see a miss. Your champion should hear it from you a week early, in private.

## 2. Data checklist

| Data | System category | Lookback |
|---|---|---|
| The outcomes they bought, in their words | Sales handoff notes, mutual action plan, kickoff deck, CRM opportunity | Original deal |
| Baselines for those outcomes | Kickoff notes, success plan, their own reports | Pre-purchase |
| Results this quarter for each outcome | Customer's own systems where possible; your product analytics where not | This quarter plus last |
| Usage by team, role, and feature | Product analytics | This quarter plus trend since go-live |
| Support tickets, severity, time to resolve | Support desk | This quarter |
| Commitments made last QBR and their status | Last QBR recap, success plan | Last QBR |
| Stakeholder changes (new leaders, departures, reorgs) | CRM contacts, champion, their public announcements | Since last QBR |
| Renewal date, contract value, seat count, open expansion | CRM, billing | Current |
| What their executive said on calls | Call recorder, CSM notes | Last two quarters |

**Source rule:** a result the customer measured in their own system beats a result your product reported. Where you only have your own data, say "measured in our platform" on the slide.

## 3. Translate product metrics into customer terms

A usage number is never a result. Every chart gets one sentence: what it meant for their goal (SKILL.md decision rule 1).

| Product metric (what you have) | Customer result (what they care about) | The sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly active users | Coverage of the team the outcome depends on | "38 of 40 managers use it weekly, so forecast prep is consistent across regions" |
| Summaries or drafts generated | Time returned to a named role | "Managers spend about 4 fewer hours a week on prep" |
| Accounts scored | Risk caught earlier | "We flagged 6 at-risk accounts 60 days before renewal; 4 were saved" |
| Tickets deflected | Support cost or response time | "Median first response fell from 9 hours to 3" |
| Features adopted | Nothing on its own | Cut it, or tie it to one of the above |

**The "so what" formula:** [metric moved] so [role or team] can [do what], which moves [their outcome] by [amount or direction].

If you cannot complete the formula for a chart, drop the chart.

---

## 4. Results scorecard

Fill one row per outcome they bought. This is slide 2, right after the agenda.

| Outcome they bought | Baseline | Target | This quarter | Status | Evidence source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [In their words] | [Number, date] | [Number] | [Number] | On track / Behind / Ahead / Not measured | [Their system or yours] |

**Status rules (rule of thumb):**
- On track: within the agreed range for this point in the plan.
- Behind: outside the range. It also gets a miss card.
- Not measured: say so. "Not measured" is honest; a substitute metric slipped into the row is not.

---

## 5. The miss card

One card per miss. A miss without a fix is a credibility loss (SKILL.md decision rule 2).

```
MISS CARD
What we said would happen:   [The commitment or expected result]
What happened:               [Plain fact, with the number]
Why (our part):              [What we got wrong, owned plainly]
Why (shared part):           [What depended on them, stated without blame]
What we changed:             [The specific change, with its date]
Evidence the fix is working: [Early number, or "too early, read on (date)"]
What we need from you:       [If anything. Often this feeds the ask]
```

**Miss severity and placement:**

| Severity | Example | How to handle |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | One feature slower to adopt than planned | One line in the misses section |
| Material | An outcome is behind target | Its own miss card, fix shown, early evidence |
| Trust-breaking | Outage, data error, a promise broken | Raise it before the QBR with the sponsor; open the misses section with it |

**Placement rule:** misses go second, after results, never buried in the middle of a plan section (SKILL.md process step 2).

## 6. Agenda and pre-read

### The 45-minute agenda

| Minutes | Section | Owner | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Purpose and the decision we will ask for today | Your exec sponsor or CSM | No surprises in the structure |
| 3-15 | Results against the outcomes you bought | CSM | Value in their terms |
| 15-23 | What did not work and what we changed | CSM | Credibility |
| 23-35 | Next quarter plan tied to your goals | CSM plus champion | Earn the next quarter |
| 35-42 | The decision we need | Your exec sponsor | Leave with it made |
| 42-45 | Confirm owners and dates | CSM | Close the loop |

**Rule:** state the decision you will ask for in minute one. An executive who knows the ask from the start listens to the results as evidence for it.

### Pre-read email (send T-5 days)

```
Subject: [Customer] Q[n] review: agenda and one decision we will ask for

[Name],

Ahead of our review on [date], here is the agenda:
1. Results against [outcome 1] and [outcome 2]
2. What did not work this quarter and what we changed
3. Plan for next quarter
4. One decision: [the decision, in one line]

Headline: [one sentence, best honest result]. We will also cover [the main miss, named].

If there is anything you want added, reply by [date].
```

Naming the miss in the pre-read is deliberate. The executive arrives knowing it, and the room spends its time on the fix, not the shock.

## 7. The ask: one decision

| Good ask | Weak ask | Why the weak one fails |
|---|---|---|
| "Approve the renewals team rollout for January, yes or no" | "Think about expanding" | No decision, no date |
| "Name an owner for the scoring rubric by the 15th" | "We need more engagement" | No owner |
| "Give us read access to your billing data so we can measure churn impact" | "Help us with data" | No specific action |
| "Agree the Q[n] success target: 30 percent fewer escalations" | "Keep partnering with us" | Not a decision |

**The ask test (SKILL.md decision rule 3):** can the executive answer it with yes, no, or a name and a date, before the meeting ends? If not, rewrite it. If you cannot write it at all, you are not ready for the meeting.

**Politics adjustment:** if the executive has not seen value yet, ask for something small and verifying (data access, a named owner). Ask for expansion only after the results section has landed.

## 8. Executive question bank

Pick the three most likely for this account and rehearse them. Answer each in under 60 seconds: direct answer, the evidence, what happens next.

| Category | Question you should expect | Answer structure |
|---|---|---|
| Value | "What did we actually get for the money?" | One outcome, the number, the source |
| Value | "How do I know this would not have happened anyway?" | Baseline, what else changed, what you can and cannot attribute |
| Risk | "Why should my team trust what the AI says?" | Accuracy check you ran, human review step, the error you caught |
| Risk | "What happens to our data?" | Where it lives, who sees it, what is retained |
| Miss | "Why should I believe the fix will hold?" | Early evidence, date of the next read, what you will do if it fails |
| Cost | "Can we do this with fewer seats?" | Which roles drive the outcome; what breaks if they are cut |
| Comparison | "Another vendor says they do this cheaper. Why stay?" | Switching cost in their terms, results to date, what is unique to this setup |
| Roadmap | "When will you fix [thing]?" | Date if committed, honest "not planned" if not |
| Renewal | "What are you going to ask us for at renewal?" | Answer it straight; do not dodge |
| People | "Who on my team is not using it, and does it matter?" | Usage by role tied to the outcome, without naming individuals in the room |

## 9. Account situation: if X, shape the QBR like Y

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| Results are strong and measured | Short results section, longer plan, ask for the expansion step |
| Results are not measured yet | Lead with the measurement plan, ask for the data access or baseline you need |
| A material miss happened | Pre-call the sponsor, open the misses with it, ask for the one thing the fix needs |
| New executive sponsor | Re-state the original outcomes and ask whether they still hold before showing results |
| Champion has left | Ask the executive to name a new owner. That is the decision |
| Renewal within 90 days | Make the plan section about the next 12 months; preview renewal terms honestly |
| Usage high, outcome flat | Say it plainly: adoption happened, the outcome did not move; propose the workflow change |
| Executive will not attend | Hold a working review with the champion and reschedule the executive version; do not run an executive QBR without the executive |

## 10. Recap and decision log (send T+1 day)

```
Subject: [Customer] Q[n] review: decision and next steps

Decision made:   [The decision, who made it, date]
Results agreed:  [One line per outcome]
Misses and fixes:[One line each, with the next read date]
Next quarter:    [Two or three commitments, owner, date]
Next review:     [Date]
```

Keep a running decision log per account. At renewal it is the record of what the customer agreed was true.

## 11. Red flags

| Red flag | What it usually means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| The deck opens with your product roadmap | You are presenting what you shipped, not what they got | Move the roadmap to an appendix |
| Every status is green | Something is being hidden, or nothing was measured | Find the real miss; the room already knows it |
| No baseline for any outcome | Value cannot be shown | Make the measurement plan the ask |
| The executive declined the invite | They do not see enough value to spend 45 minutes | Ask the champion why, before the meeting |
| Champion asks you to leave out the miss | They are protecting themselves | Agree on framing, not on omission |
| Results only from your platform | Executive may not trust them | Get one number from their own systems |
| You cannot name the ask | Unclear what the next quarter is for | Do not hold the QBR until you can |

## 12. Worked example: Acme

*Illustrative. Acme is a fictional 400-person B2B software company. It bought your AI call-review and coaching platform for its 60-person sales team 9 months ago. Renewal is in 5 months. Acme's new CRO started 6 weeks ago; this is the CRO's first QBR with you.*

**Outcomes Acme bought (from the kickoff deck):**
1. Cut new-AE ramp: time to first closed deal from 5.5 months to 4.
2. Cut manager call-review time, then about 5 hours a week per manager.

**Data pulled.** Ramp: the cohort hired this year (8 AEs) against last year's cohort average. Manager time: a two-week time sample by Acme's enablement lead. Usage: weekly scorecard views by rep from your platform. Last QBR recap: you committed to rubric tuning.

**Results scorecard.**

| Outcome | Baseline | Target | This quarter | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to first deal, new AEs | 5.5 months | 4.0 months | 4.4 months (cohort of 8) | Behind, improving | Acme CRM |
| Manager call-review time | 5 hrs/week | 3 hrs/week | 2.5 hrs/week | Ahead | Acme time sample |

**Reasoning on results.** The ramp number moved but is behind target, and a cohort of 8 is small: one slow hire swings the average. Say both. The manager-time result is ahead, and it is measured by Acme, not by you, so lead with it.

**Miss card.**
- Said would happen: reps would use the AI scorecard weekly to self-coach.
- Happened: weekly views fell from 80 percent of reps to 35 percent by mid-quarter.
- Why (our part): the "discovery depth" rubric line was vague; the AI and Acme managers agreed on it only 58 percent of the time, so reps stopped trusting the score.
- Why (shared part): the rubric was set at kickoff without front-line manager input.
- What we changed: rewrote that line with two Acme managers in week 7, hand-scored 30 calls, agreement rose to 81 percent.
- Evidence: weekly views back to 60 percent over the last 3 weeks. Early; read again at week 4 of next quarter.

**The ask.** Approve the scorecard as part of new-hire certification for the January cohort, yes or no by the 15th. Reasoning: ramp is the outcome behind target, and making the scorecard part of certification is the change most likely to move it. It is a decision the CRO can make in the room.

**Agenda as sent.**

| Minutes | Section |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Purpose; today we will ask you to decide on January certification |
| 3-15 | Results: manager review time halved (Acme's own sample); ramp down from 5.5 to 4.4 months, short of the 4.0 target |
| 15-23 | What did not work: rep trust in the scorecard, the rubric fix, the recovery so far |
| 23-35 | Next quarter: tuned rubric live for all reps, January cohort certification, ramp read at month 4 |
| 35-42 | Decision: January certification |
| 42-45 | Owners and dates |

**Three executive questions, drafted answers.**
1. "How do I know ramp would not have improved anyway?" *Answer:* "You may be right for part of it. Last year's cohort had the same manager team and the same territories, and averaged 5.5 months. This cohort is 4.4. With 8 people we cannot rule out cohort quality. The January cohort, certified on the scorecard, is the cleaner test, which is why it is today's ask."
2. "Why should my reps trust a machine scoring their calls?" *Answer:* "They stopped trusting it this quarter, for a good reason: one rubric line was wrong. Your managers rewrote it, agreement with their scores is now 81 percent, and reps can see the quote behind every score. Managers still own the coaching call."
3. "Can we cut this to managers only and save the rep seats?" *Answer:* "You would keep the manager time saving. You would lose the self-coaching that is driving the ramp result, which is the outcome behind target. We would advise deciding after the January cohort reads."

**Pre-read headline.** "Manager review time is down by half in your own sample; new-AE ramp improved but is short of target. We will also cover why reps stopped using the scorecard mid-quarter and what we changed."

## 13. AI assist

| Work | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Turn raw usage exports into the "so what" sentences | Draft | Check each against the customer's actual goal |
| Draft miss cards from your notes | Draft | Own the "our part" line; the AI will soften it |
| Draft the agenda and pre-read | Yes | Confirm the ask is one decision |
| Predict executive questions | Draft a long list | Pick the three; you know this executive |
| Draft answers | Draft | Rehearse out loud; rewrite anything you would not say |
| Read the politics in the room | No | Yours alone |

**Follow-up prompts to use after the Quick prompt:**

1. "For every result, write it in the customer's terms using: [metric moved] so [role] can [do what], which moves [their outcome]. Flag any result you could not translate."
2. "Rewrite each miss so the first sentence states what happened with the number, and the second states what we changed and when. Remove any softening words."
3. "Here is what I know about their executive: [role, tenure, what they said on recent calls]. List ten questions they might ask, ranked by likelihood, then draft answers to the top three in under 80 words each."
4. "Test my ask: can the executive answer it with yes, no, or a name and date in the meeting? If not, give me three sharper versions."
5. "Write the pre-read email. Name the main miss in it. Keep it under 120 words."

**Check the AI's output for these errors:**

| Error this job invites | How to catch it |
|---|---|
| Invented or rounded-up results | Every number traces to your paste; nothing new appears |
| Misses softened into "learnings" or "opportunities" | Search for those words; replace with the plain fact |
| Product terms in the results section | Look for feature names on results slides |
| Bad news moved into the plan section | Check misses sit in their own section, second |
| Several asks instead of one | Count the decisions requested |
| Generic executive questions | Each question should only make sense for this account |

## 14. Common mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The usage tour | More than half the slides are usage charts | One "so what" sentence per chart or cut it |
| Results in your terms | Slide titles name features | Rewrite titles as their outcomes |
| Hiding the miss | Misses appear only in speaker notes | Own section, second |
| A list of follow-ups instead of a decision | Recap has ten action items and no decision | Rewrite to one decision with owner and date |
| Surprising the executive | First time the sponsor hears the miss is in the room | Champion pre-call at T-7 |
| Overclaiming attribution | "Our tool drove a 20 percent increase" with no baseline | Say what you can and cannot attribute |
| Reading slides aloud | Rehearsal runs long | Pre-read carries the detail; meeting carries the decision |

## 15. The 5-minute version

When the QBR is tomorrow and you have nothing:
1. Write the outcomes they bought, one line each.
2. Next to each, write the best honest number you have and its source. "Not measured" is allowed.
3. Write the one biggest miss and the one change you made.
4. Write the one decision you need, answerable with yes, no, or a name and date.
5. Send those four things as the agenda tonight.

## The deep version

For a strategic account, a renewal at risk, or a new executive sponsor:
- Run the full prep schedule from T-21.
- Get at least one result measured in the customer's own systems.
- Build a stakeholder map of everyone in the room: what each cares about, the question each is likely to ask, and who will speak for you.
- Hold two rehearsals: one with your manager as the executive, one with a peer as the skeptic from finance.
- Prepare a one-page appendix of every number with its source, for the person who asks.
- After the meeting, update the success plan and decision log, and set the next QBR date before you leave the room.
