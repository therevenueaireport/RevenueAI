# Prepare for a Discovery Call: Toolkit

**Use this file when:**
- A first or second call is booked and the buyer's problem is not yet confirmed.
- A deal is stalling and you suspect discovery was skipped or done as a demo.
- You manage reps and want every discovery plan built and scored the same way.
- An AI assistant gave you a call plan and you want to know if it is any good before you dial.

The SKILL.md holds the logic: hypotheses, test questions, kill answers, one buying-process question, one must-learn item, five questions maximum. This file holds the prep checklist, the hypothesis builder, the question bank, the call plan and scorecard templates, and a full worked example.

---

## 1. Fifteen-minute prep sequence

| Minutes | What you do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0-4 | Check what is already known (Section 2) | Known vs assumed sheet |
| 4-8 | Build three hypotheses with kill answers (Section 3) | Hypothesis cards |
| 8-11 | Choose test questions and the buying-process question (Section 4) | Five questions |
| 11-13 | Name the must-learn item (Section 5) | One line |
| 13-15 | Write the call plan and the close (Section 6) | One-page plan |

## 2. Known vs assumed

Write every fact you have, its source, and whether it is known or assumed. This is SKILL.md process step 1.

| Item | Known or assumed | Source | Matters because |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the company sells, to whom | | Website | Frames the problem |
| Size, growth, recent funding or layoffs | | Press releases, filings, professional social network | Signals budget and pressure |
| Attendee role, tenure, prior employer type | | Professional social profile | Shapes their priorities and history |
| How the meeting was booked | | CRM, form fields, SDR notes | Inbound, outbound, referral each mean different intent |
| What they wrote on the form or replied to | | Form fields, email thread | Their own words about the problem |
| Prior contact with your company | | CRM activity history, call recorder | Avoid re-asking; build on it |
| Open roles in the function you serve | | Their careers page | Hiring often signals the problem or the plan |
| Tools they likely run today | | Job postings, careers page, attendee profile | Current approach and switching cost |

**Research checklist rule:** check these sources before you write a single question. Any question these sources answer is cut (SKILL.md process step 2).

**Time-box rule (rule of thumb):** 5 minutes of research for a first call with a single attendee; up to 20 for an executive or a large account. Past that, you are preparing a pitch, not discovery.

## 3. The hypothesis builder

### Formula

```
HYPOTHESIS CARD
Because [observed trigger or evidence],
[attendee's role or team] is likely struggling with [specific problem],
which costs them [impact on a metric they own].
Confirm answer:  [What they would say if this is true]
Kill answer:     [What they would say if this is false, written before the call]
Evidence for it: [Source from Section 2]
```

### Hypothesis quality score

Score each card 0-2 per row. 0 = no, 1 = partly, 2 = yes.

| Test | 0 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Specific | "They want to grow revenue" | "Their new board target needs 40 percent more pipeline than last year" |
| Based on evidence | No source | A trigger from Section 2 |
| Falsifiable | No kill answer exists | A kill answer is written and plausible |
| Owned by the attendee | Problem belongs to someone else | Problem sits on a metric this person owns |
| Solvable by you | Your product cannot touch it | Your product addresses it directly |

**Rule of thumb:** 8-10 keep; 5-7 sharpen before the call; 0-4 rewrite. A card with no kill answer is a bias, not a hypothesis (SKILL.md decision rule 1), whatever else it scores.

### Hypothesis sources: where good ones come from

| Trigger | Likely hypothesis direction |
|---|---|
| New funding round | New growth target the current process cannot hit |
| New executive in the attendee's chain | Mandate to change something within the first two quarters |
| Layoffs or hiring freeze | Same targets with fewer people; efficiency pressure |
| Hiring many reps or CSMs | Ramp, consistency, or coverage problem coming |
| Hiring for the role your product supports | Gap they are trying to fill with people |
| Inbound from pricing or comparison page | Active evaluation, likely with alternatives |
| Attendee used a competitor before | Expectations set by that tool, good or bad |
| Merger or new product line | Process and data fragmentation |

## 4. The question bank

Pick five in total: test questions for your hypotheses, plus exactly one buying-process question. All open. None answerable from the website.

### Problem and impact

| Purpose | Question |
|---|---|
| Open the problem | "What prompted you to take this meeting now rather than six months ago?" |
| Test a trigger | "What did the board or leadership ask your team to prove this year?" |
| Size the impact | "When this goes wrong, what does it cost you, and how do you know?" |
| Find the metric | "Which number would tell you this problem is solved?" |
| Find the current approach | "Walk me through how this works today, step by step, the last time it happened." |
| Test priority | "Where does this sit against the other two or three things on your plate this quarter?" |
| Test ownership | "Who feels this problem most, and who gets blamed for it?" |

### Buying process (pick one, ask it every time)

| Question | What it uncovers |
|---|---|
| "How did the last similar purchase get approved, step by step?" | Real process, from a real example |
| "Who else would need to agree before anything changed here?" | Approvers and blockers |
| "When you bought [category] before, what went well and what would you do differently?" | Past scars and evaluation habits |
| "If this were a priority, what would have to happen by when?" | Timeline tied to events, not hopes |

### Rewrite closed or leading questions

| Weak (closed, leading, or public) | Strong (open, neutral, private) |
|---|---|
| "Is pipeline a challenge for you?" | "How is pipeline tracking against what the board expects?" |
| "Would faster forecasting help?" | "What happens in the week before a forecast call today?" |
| "You raised a round last month, right?" | Cut it. It is public. |
| "Do you have budget?" | "How have projects like this been funded in the past?" |
| "Our tool cuts prep time by half. Is that interesting?" | Cut it. It is a pitch. |
| "Are you the decision maker?" | "How would a decision like this get made here?" |

## 5. The must-learn item

The single thing you must know before the call ends. If you learn nothing else, this decides the next step.

| Deal situation | Typical must-learn |
|---|---|
| Inbound, unknown intent | Is there a problem they will spend on this half-year? |
| Outbound, cold | Does the problem you hypothesized exist here at all? |
| Senior attendee | What they personally need to show, and by when |
| Junior attendee | Who decides, and whether this person can bring them |
| Stalled deal, discovery skipped | What changed since they first engaged, and whether it still matters |
| Past buyer of a competitor | Why that purchase did or did not work |

**Rule:** if you have not learned the must-learn item by minute 20, stop the planned questions and ask it directly.

## 6. Call plan template

```
DISCOVERY CALL PLAN
Account:          [ ]          Attendee(s): [Name placeholder, role]
Booked via:       [Inbound / outbound / referral, and what they said]
Call length:      [30 min]

Must-learn:       [One line]

H1: [Hypothesis]      Test Q: [ ]   Confirm: [ ]   Kill: [ ]
H2: [Hypothesis]      Test Q: [ ]   Confirm: [ ]   Kill: [ ]
H3: [Hypothesis]      Test Q: [ ]   Confirm: [ ]   Kill: [ ]
Buying-process Q:     [ ]

Opening (2 min):  [Why we are here, proposed agenda, what they want from the call]
Close (5 min):    [Summarize what you heard; propose the next step tied to what you learned]
Next step if confirmed: [ ]
Next step if killed:    [ ]
```

### Thirty-minute shape

| Minutes | Segment | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Opening | Ask what they want from the call; adjust |
| 2-22 | Five questions, follow-ups | Listen for confirm and kill answers; follow the energy, not the list |
| 22-25 | Must-learn check | Ask it directly if not yet answered |
| 25-30 | Summary and next step | Read back what you heard; ask what you got wrong |

## 7. Post-call scorecard

Fill within 30 minutes of hanging up (SKILL.md process step 5).

| Hypothesis | Status | Evidence (their words) | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Confirmed / Killed / Open | [Quote] | |
| H2 | | | |
| H3 | | | |
| Buying process | Clear / Partial / Unknown | [Quote] | |
| Must-learn | Learned / Not learned | [Quote] | |

**Status rules:**
- Confirmed: the attendee described the problem in their own words, with an example or a number.
- Killed: you heard the kill answer, or the attendee said the problem is not theirs.
- Open: you heard interest but no example, number, or owner. Open is not confirmed.

### Next step decision table

| Scorecard pattern | Next step |
|---|---|
| At least one confirmed, buying process clear | Propose the next meeting with the decision makers named; no demo before this |
| At least one confirmed, buying process unknown | Book someone who can describe approval before proposing anything (SKILL.md decision rule 2) |
| All open | One more discovery call with a sharper hypothesis, or a peer of the attendee |
| All killed | Disqualify fast and say why (SKILL.md decision rule 3). Offer to reconnect if a named trigger happens |
| Confirmed, but the problem belongs to another team | Ask for an introduction to the owner |
| Must-learn not learned | Do not advance the stage in the CRM |

## 8. Red flags on the call

| Red flag | What it usually means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| "Just exploring options" with no trigger | No active problem or budget | Ask what would make it a priority; disqualify if nothing |
| Cannot describe how purchases get approved | Not close to the decision | Ask who could; book them |
| Asks for a demo in minute 3 | Comparing vendors on features, or a researcher | Agree to show it after two questions; ask what they want to see and why |
| Every hypothesis "kind of" true | Politeness, not confirmation | Ask for the last time it happened |
| Pricing question before any problem talk | Price-shopping or a budget ceiling exists | Ask what they compare against and what budget range is in play |
| Attendee says the boss "loves this idea" | Champion without authority | Ask what the boss would need to see |
| Answers in "we should" not "we do" | Aspiration, not current pain | Ask what it costs them today |

## 9. Worked example

*Illustrative. You are an AE at Acme, a fictional 400-person B2B software company selling an AI forecasting platform. The prospect is "Prospect Co", a fictional 700-person payments software company. The attendee is their Director of Revenue Operations, who requested a demo through your pricing page.*

**Known vs assumed (abridged).**

| Item | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hired a new CRO 3 months ago | Known | Press release |
| Posting for 2 revenue analysts | Known | Careers page |
| Forecast is built in spreadsheets | Assumed | Attendee profile lists spreadsheet modeling skills |
| Came through the pricing page | Known | CRM form source |
| Attendee used a forecasting tool at a prior company | Assumed | Profile mentions "forecasting platform rollout" |

**Hypotheses.**

H1. Because a new CRO arrived 3 months ago, RevOps is likely being asked for a forecast the CRO trusts, which costs them credibility at the next board meeting. Confirm: CRO has questioned forecast accuracy or changed the forecast process. Kill: the CRO kept the existing process and is satisfied. Score: 10.

H2. Because they are hiring 2 analysts, the team is likely spending too many hours assembling the forecast by hand, which costs them analyst time they cannot hire fast enough. Confirm: forecast prep takes days each week; analysts are hired to do assembly. Kill: the analysts are for pricing or territory work, unrelated to forecast. Score: 9.

H3. Because they came via the pricing page, they are likely in an active evaluation with alternatives already shortlisted. Confirm: named shortlist and a decision date. Kill: early research with no timeline. Score: 7, sharpened by adding "a decision tied to a board date."

**Reasoning.** H1 and H2 come from public triggers and sit on metrics the attendee owns. H3 is about deal state, not the problem, so it earns a single question at most. The must-learn is set by the senior-change trigger: what the CRO personally needs from the forecast and by when, because that decides who the real buyer is.

**The five questions.**
1. (H1) "What has your new CRO asked for from the forecast since arriving?"
2. (H1) "Walk me through what happened in the last forecast call: what got questioned?"
3. (H2) "What will the two analysts you are hiring spend most of their week on?"
4. (H3) "What else are you looking at, and what would make you decide by a particular date?"
5. (Buying process) "When the team last bought a revenue tool, how did approval work, step by step?"

**Cut on review.** "How big is your sales team?" (public, on their site). "Do you use spreadsheets for forecasting?" (closed and leading). "Our customers cut prep time in half; would that help?" (a pitch).

**Must-learn.** What the CRO needs to show the board from the forecast, and by which board meeting.

**Post-call scorecard.**

| | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Confirmed | "She threw out our commit number in her second week. She wants a call she can defend in the March board meeting." |
| H2 | Killed | "The analysts are for pricing. Forecast is me and one spreadsheet." |
| H3 | Open | "We have looked at two others. No date yet." |
| Buying process | Partial | "Anything over a certain amount goes to the CFO. Last time it took two months." |
| Must-learn | Learned | A defendable forecast for the March board meeting |

**Next step.** One confirmed, buying process partial: book a 30-minute call with the CRO, with the attendee's help, framed around the March board forecast. Ask the attendee to bring the CFO's approval steps to that call. No demo yet. H2 is killed, so drop the "analyst time" story from all follow-up.

## 10. AI assist

| Work | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Summarize public information about the account | Yes, with sources | Verify every fact you will mention on the call |
| Draft hypotheses from triggers | Yes | Pick the three; you know your product's real strengths |
| Write kill answers | Yes | Check each is something a buyer would actually say |
| Rewrite closed questions as open | Yes | Read them aloud; cut anything you would not say |
| Read the room on the call | No | Yours |
| Draft the post-call scorecard from a transcript | Yes, with quotes | Confirm each status; "open" vs "confirmed" is your call |

**Follow-up prompts to use after the Quick prompt:**

1. "For each hypothesis, show the evidence from my notes it rests on. Mark any hypothesis that rests on an assumption and tell me what to check before the call."
2. "Score each hypothesis 0-2 on: specific, evidence-based, falsifiable, owned by the attendee, solvable by us. Rewrite any that score under 8."
3. "List every question that could be answered from their website, press releases, or the attendee's public profile. Cut them."
4. "Rewrite any question that can be answered yes or no, or that suggests the answer. Keep it under 20 words."
5. After the call: "Here is the transcript or my notes: [paste]. Score each hypothesis as confirmed, killed, or open, quoting the exact words that justify each. If there is no quote, mark it open."

**Check the AI's output for these errors:**

| Error this job invites | How to catch it |
|---|---|
| Invented facts about the company (funding, headcount, tools) | Every fact must trace to a source you checked |
| Generic hypotheses that fit any company | Swap in a different company name; if it still reads true, rewrite |
| Kill answers missing or impossible | Each hypothesis has a kill answer a real buyer could say |
| Pitch disguised as questions | Look for your product's features inside questions |
| Too many questions | Count them. More than five is a list, not a plan |
| Scorecard marks "confirmed" on enthusiasm | Confirmed needs an example or number in their words |

## 11. Common mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Demo as discovery | Slides open before minute 10 | Five questions first, demo on a later call |
| Twelve questions for 30 minutes | Plan is longer than a page | Cut to five |
| Asking what the website says | Attendee answers with "as you probably saw" | Research checklist first |
| Hypotheses about you, not them | "They need a better tool" | Rewrite around their metric and trigger |
| Advancing the stage on "open" | CRM stage moves with no confirmed hypothesis | Stage moves on confirmed only |
| No buying-process question | Proposal sent with approvers unknown | Ask one every first call |
| Scorecard filled days later | Quotes are paraphrases | Fill within 30 minutes |

## 12. The 5-minute version

When the call is in five minutes:
1. Write one hypothesis with its kill answer.
2. Write one open question that tests it.
3. Write one buying-process question: "How did the last similar purchase get approved?"
4. Write the must-learn item.
5. After the call, mark the hypothesis confirmed, killed, or open, with a quote.

## The deep version

For a large account, an executive attendee, or a deal that already stalled once:
- Spend up to 20 minutes on research and complete the full known vs assumed sheet.
- Build five hypotheses, score them, and take the top three.
- Review the plan with your manager; ask them to predict the kill answer they think is most likely.
- Pull every past call with this account from your call recorder and read what was already said.
- Plan the second call before the first: who must be on it if H1 is confirmed.
- After the call, share the scorecard with your manager and log the quotes in the CRM opportunity.
