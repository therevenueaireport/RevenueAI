# Prepare for a Discovery Call

> From the [Revenue AI Report skill library](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/skills). Level: Start Here · Task: Run Deals · Setup: About 15 minutes · For: Sales Representative, SDR and BDR, Sales Leader

**Outcome:** A call plan with hypotheses, questions that test them, and the one thing you must learn before you leave.

## When to use
- Before any first or second call where the problem is not yet confirmed.
- When a deal is stalling and you realize discovery was skipped.

## Inputs you need
- Account name and what you know so far
- How the meeting was booked and who is attending
- The problem your product solves

## Quick prompt
Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or any assistant. Replace the [bracketed] parts.

```text
You are a sales coach helping me prepare a 30-minute discovery call with [ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Here is what I know: [paste notes]. Build a call plan: three hypotheses about their problem, two open questions that would confirm or kill each hypothesis, one question about how they buy (who else decides, how the last similar purchase went), and the single most important thing I must learn before the call ends. Do not script a pitch. Do not ask questions their website already answers.
```

## Process
1. Write down what you actually know versus what you assume.
2. Generate the plan, then cut questions their website answers.
3. Pick the five questions you will really ask. Thirty minutes allows about five.
4. Decide the disqualifying answer for each hypothesis before the call.
5. After the call, score each hypothesis: confirmed, killed, or still open.

## Decision rules
- A hypothesis with no disqualifying answer is a bias, not a hypothesis. Rewrite it.
- If the attendee cannot describe how purchases get approved, book someone who can before proposing anything.
- If all three hypotheses die on the call, disqualify fast and say why.

## Output
- Three hypotheses with test questions.
- One buying-process question.
- The must-learn item for the call.
- A post-call scorecard: confirmed, killed, open.

## Quality checks
- Questions are open and cannot be answered with yes or no.
- No question asks for information already public.
- Each hypothesis has a defined kill answer.

## Limitations
- The AI cannot read the room. The plan is a starting grid, not a script.
- It does not know your history with the account. Adjust before you dial.

## Worked example
**Input:** VP Sales at a 400-person SaaS firm, inbound demo request after their Series C. Attendee ran sales ops at a previous company that bought a competitor.

**Output:** Hypothesis 1: the raise created a board-mandated pipeline target the current process cannot hit. Test: what did the board ask the revenue team to prove this year? Kill answer: no new target, hiring ahead of plan. Must-learn: who owns the decision and what happened the last time they bought sales software.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Five questions or fewer?
- [ ] One buying-process question included?
- [ ] Kill answers written before the call?
