# Research a Target Account: Toolkit

**Use this file when:**
- You have a first meeting or a top-tier outbound touch in the next few days and need a brief you can defend line by line.
- An account just entered your target list and you need a working hypothesis, not a company profile.
- You are standardizing account research across a team and need one template, one rubric, and one bar.
- You want an AI assistant to do the reading while you keep the judgment.

The SKILL.md holds the process. This file holds the instruments: source checklist, brief template, scoring sheet, decision table, worked example, and the AI split.

---

## 1. The sequence, with time boxes

| Step (from SKILL.md) | What you actually do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Collect source material | Pull from the checklist in Section 2. Date every item | Source pack, numbered S1, S2, S3... | 5-8 min |
| 2. Paste and ask for the brief | Quick prompt plus the numbered pack | Draft brief | 1-2 min |
| 3. Check every claim | Trace each line to a source number. Cut what does not trace | Verified brief | 3-5 min |
| 4. Add what only you know | Last interaction, mutual contact, product usage, CRM history | "Only we know" line | 1-2 min |
| 5. Write the opening line | Turn the point of view into one sentence about their business | Opener | 1-2 min |

**The source-number rule:** number every pasted item before it goes into the AI. Ask the AI to tag each claim with the source number. A claim with no number is an assumption until you prove otherwise.

## 2. Source checklist: what to pull and how far back

| Source category | What to look for | How far back | Signal strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company website (home, product, pricing, customers pages) | What they sell, to whom, how they price, who they show as customers | Current | Medium: describes intent, not results |
| Press releases and newsroom | Launches, acquisitions, new markets, leadership hires | 6 months | High when it names a date or a commitment |
| Earnings call notes or annual report (public companies) | Stated priorities, named risks, guidance changes, cost programs | Last 2 quarters / last annual | Highest: management said it on the record |
| Job postings | Roles being built, tools named in requirements, team size hints, new functions | Open now, plus closed in last 90 days if visible | High: hiring is spending |
| Executive interviews, podcasts, posts on a professional social network | What leaders say worries them, in their own words | 6 months | Medium-high: quote it exactly or not at all |
| Industry or trade coverage | Market pressure, regulation, competitor moves | 6-12 months | Medium: about the market, not the company |
| Review sites and community forums | Their customers' complaints, which hint at their own priorities | 6 months | Low-medium: anecdotal |
| Your CRM | Past opportunities, closed-lost reasons, contacts, open tickets | All history | High, and the AI cannot see it |
| Your call recorder and email history | Last conversation, objections heard, promises made | 12 months | High, and the AI cannot see it |
| Your product usage data (if they are a free user or past customer) | Who logs in, what they use, where they stall | 90 days | Highest for expansion or reactivation |

**Minimum viable pack (rule of thumb):** at least three dated sources, at least one of them less than 90 days old, and at least one that shows spending (job posts, launch, hire, acquisition). Below that, the brief is a guess with formatting.

**Do not include:** anything behind a login you are not entitled to, scraped data that breaks a site's terms, personal details about individuals beyond their professional role, or confidential material from a former employer or another customer.

## 3. The brief template

Copy, fill, keep to one page.

```
ACCOUNT BRIEF: [Company]
Prepared by: [name]   Date: [date]   Meeting / touch: [date, type]
Source freshness: [Current | DATED: newest source is X months old, verify before meeting]

1. BUSINESS (2-3 lines)
   What they do:            [one sentence, plain words]                              [S#]
   How they make money:     [model: subscription, transaction, services, mix]        [S#]
   Who they sell to:        [segment, size, geography]                               [S#]
   Size and shape:          [headcount band, public/private, recent change]          [S#]

2. THREE LIKELY PRIORITIES THIS YEAR
   P1: [priority]  Evidence: [what they did or said]  [S#]  Confidence: [H/M/L]
   P2: [priority]  Evidence: [...]                    [S#]  Confidence: [H/M/L]
   P3: [priority]  Evidence: [...]                    [S#]  Confidence: [H/M/L]

3. ONE RISK OR PRESSURE
   [What could hurt them this year and why]  [S#]  Confidence: [H/M/L]

4. BUYING ROLES MOST LIKELY TO CARE
   Role                 Why they care (tie to P1-P3)        Named person known?   Source
   [e.g., VP RevOps]    [owns the process that breaks]      [Y/N, verified Y/N]   [S#]

5. OPENING POINT OF VIEW (two sentences)
   [Sentence 1: a pattern companies in their situation usually hit]
   [Sentence 2: what that means for them, framed as a question or observation]

6. ONLY WE KNOW
   [Last interaction / mutual contact / usage signal / CRM history. If none, write "None."]

7. ASSUMPTIONS FLAGGED
   A1: [what the brief assumes without evidence]
   A2:

8. MISSING FACTS TO CONFIRM LIVE (max 5)
   M1: [question to ask in the meeting]
   M2:

SOURCES
   S1: [title, publisher type, date, link]
   S2:
```

### Field guidance

| Field | Good entry | Weak entry |
|---|---|---|
| What they do | "Sells route-planning software to regional freight carriers, priced per vehicle" | "Leading provider of innovative logistics solutions" (their tagline, not a fact) |
| Priority | "Launching a carrier integration product; announced in a dated release, 4 integration engineer roles open" | "Growth" (every company, every year) |
| Risk or pressure | "CEO said margins are the board's focus; two cost-related roles posted" | "Competition" with no source |
| Buying role | "Head of RevOps: five open RevOps roles means the function is being built, and its owner is choosing tools" | "Decision makers" |
| Point of view | About a pattern in their business, testable in one question | About your product or your funding |

## 4. Brief quality scoring sheet

Score each brief before it is used. 0-2 per criterion, 12 points total.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sourcing | Claims with no source number | Most claims sourced, some untraced | Every claim sourced or labelled assumption |
| Freshness | Newest source over 6 months, not labelled | Labelled dated, or one recent source | At least one source under 90 days, all dated |
| Priority specificity | Generic priorities ("growth," "efficiency") | Specific but one source each | Specific, each backed by an action (hire, launch, spend) |
| Role mapping | No roles or "decision makers" | Roles named, not tied to priorities | Each role tied to a priority and a reason |
| Point of view | About your product, or missing | About their industry in general | About their situation, testable with one question |
| Only-we-know | Blank with no check made | "None" after checking CRM and recorder | A real internal signal included |

**Thresholds (rule of thumb):**

| Score | Use |
|---|---|
| 10-12 | Use for a first meeting or a top-tier personalized touch |
| 7-9 | Fix the 0s before use. Fine for a standard sequence |
| 4-6 | Not ready for a personalized touch. Light touch or more research |
| 0-3 | Discard. The account is not researched, it is described |

**Hard stop:** any score of 0 on Sourcing means the brief is not used, regardless of total. Unsourced claims in a first touch are how sellers lose credibility in one line.

## 5. Decision table

| If | Then | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A claim has no source number | Cut it. Do not soften to "may be" | SKILL.md rule: no source, no claim |
| Newest source is older than six months | Label the brief DATED; verify on their site and news before the meeting | Priorities change by quarter |
| You cannot write a point of view about their business | Use a light touch (relevant content, no claim) or wait for a signal | A forced POV reads as a template |
| The only priority evidence is their website copy | Treat priorities as low confidence; add a discovery question per priority | Marketing copy states aspiration, not budget |
| Job posts name a tool category you replace | Treat as a signal, never as an opener. Do not tell them what they lack | Job posts lag reality and enrichment data under-detects |
| An executive quote is the core of the POV | Quote exactly with source and date, or paraphrase as "leadership has said publicly" | Misquoting a buyer's executive ends the meeting |
| CRM shows a closed-lost opportunity in last 12 months | Read the loss reason first; the opener must acknowledge what changed | They remember; you look careless if you do not |
| They are an existing free user or past customer | Lead with usage or history, not public news | Your data beats anything public |
| Two sources contradict each other | Use the newer, flag the conflict in Missing Facts | Contradictions are good discovery questions |
| The brief runs past one page | Cut background first, then the weakest priority | Length signals you have not decided what matters |

### Red flags in a finished brief

| Red flag | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Revenue or headcount figures with no source | AI filled from training data. Likely stale or wrong |
| Named people with titles not in your sources | Possibly hallucinated or out of date. Verify before addressing anyone |
| All three priorities sound like any company | Source pack was too thin or too old |
| Point of view mentions your product in sentence one | It is a pitch, not a POV |
| No assumptions flagged | Either perfect sourcing (rare) or the AI was not asked to flag |
| Quotes with no date | Could be years old, could be invented |

## 6. Question bank: turning missing facts into discovery

Use these for the "Missing facts to confirm live" section. Pick the ones tied to your lowest-confidence lines.

| Gap | Question to ask live |
|---|---|
| Priority confidence low | "When you look at this year, which of [P1, P2] is actually funded?" |
| Unknown current process | "Walk me through how [process] works today, from [start] to [end]." |
| Unknown owner | "Who owns [problem] today, and who would feel it first if it broke?" |
| Unknown urgency | "What happens if this is not solved by [their stated date]?" |
| Unknown past attempts | "Have you tried to fix this before? What got in the way?" |
| Contradicting sources | "I read [A] and also [B]. Which is closer to where you are now?" |
| Unknown buying process | "How did the last purchase of this size get approved?" |

## 7. Worked example

**Situation (illustrative):** You sell revenue process software. Target: Acme Freight Tech, a fictional 800-person logistics software company. First meeting in four days with their VP of Sales.

**Source pack:**
- S1: Acme press release, dated 7 weeks ago: new carrier integration API, general availability next quarter.
- S2: Acme careers page today: five open RevOps roles, one "Director, Revenue Operations" (new function, per the posting text).
- S3: Interview with Acme's CEO in a trade publication, 4 months ago: margin pressure is "the board's first question."
- S4: Acme pricing page: per-shipment fee plus platform subscription.
- S5: Your CRM: closed-lost opportunity 14 months ago, reason "no owner, revisit when RevOps exists."

**Draft from AI (excerpt), with your review marks:**

| AI line | Source? | Action |
|---|---|---|
| "Acme sells shipment visibility and carrier integration software to mid-size shippers" | S1, S4 | Keep |
| "Acme has roughly 2,000 customers" | None | Cut. Not in pack |
| "P1: launching the carrier API" | S1 | Keep, High |
| "P2: building a RevOps function" | S2 | Keep, High: a new director role is the clearest spending signal |
| "P3: cost control" | S3 | Keep, Medium: one interview, 4 months old |
| "Their CRO recently joined from a competitor" | None | Cut. Name not in pack. Possible hallucination |
| "Pressure: margin scrutiny while funding a launch" | S1 + S3 | Keep, Medium |

**Filled brief (one page):**

```
ACCOUNT BRIEF: Acme Freight Tech (illustrative)
Source freshness: Current (newest S2, today; oldest S3, 4 months)

1. BUSINESS
   What they do: carrier integration and shipment visibility software for mid-size shippers [S1]
   How they make money: platform subscription plus per-shipment fee [S4]
   Size and shape: ~800 people, private, building a RevOps function [S2]

2. PRIORITIES
   P1: Carrier API launch next quarter. Evidence: dated release [S1]. Confidence H
   P2: Stand up RevOps. Evidence: 5 open roles incl. new director [S2]. Confidence H
   P3: Margin control. Evidence: CEO interview [S3]. Confidence M

3. PRESSURE
   Funding a launch while the board asks about margin. A new product line adds a new
   pricing component, which strains quoting and billing first. [S1, S3, S4] Confidence M

4. BUYING ROLES
   Director, RevOps (being hired): will own the process P1 stresses. Not yet named. [S2]
   VP Sales: carries the launch number. Meeting is with this role. [CRM]
   CFO: margin owner. [S3, inferred]

5. OPENING POINT OF VIEW
   Companies adding a usage-priced product line usually find quote-to-cash breaks before
   the product does. With the API live next quarter and RevOps still being staffed, is the
   quoting process ready to price per-integration deals?

6. ONLY WE KNOW
   Lost 14 months ago because "no owner." The owner is now being hired. [S5]

7. ASSUMPTIONS
   A1: The API will carry new pricing. Not confirmed in S1.
   A2: Current quoting is manual or spreadsheet-based. No evidence either way.

8. MISSING FACTS
   M1: How will the API be priced?
   M2: Who owns RevOps until the director starts?
   M3: How did the last tooling purchase get approved?
```

**Score:** Sourcing 2, Freshness 2, Specificity 2, Roles 2, POV 2, Only-we-know 2 = 12. Use as is.

**Reasoning that mattered:** the CRM loss reason (S5) turned a generic "you are building RevOps" angle into a specific "the thing that stopped us last time is changing" angle. The AI could not have found it. The two cut lines (customer count, CRO name) would each have been checkable errors in the first five minutes of the meeting.

**Opening line used:** "Last time we spoke the blocker was that nobody owned revenue process. It looks like that is changing just as you add a per-integration product, which is usually when quoting breaks first. Worth comparing notes?"

## 8. AI assist: who does what

| Task | AI | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Read and summarize the source pack | Yes | Spot-check |
| Tag every claim with a source number | Yes | Verify the tags are real |
| Propose priorities and confidence | Draft | Decide |
| Map buying roles | Draft | Confirm against CRM and the org you know |
| Find names, titles, figures | No. Never trust from training data | Verify on primary source |
| Add internal context (CRM, calls, usage) | No access unless pasted | Yes |
| Write the point of view | Draft 3 options | Pick, rewrite in your voice |
| Decide whether the account is ready | No | Yes |

### Follow-up prompts after the Quick prompt

Use in order. Each one targets a specific failure.

1. **Source tagging:** "For every sentence in the brief, add the source number [S#] it comes from. If a sentence has no source in the material I pasted, mark it [ASSUMPTION] and do not rewrite it to sound more certain."
2. **Training-data leak check:** "List every name, number, date, and title in the brief. For each, quote the exact words from my material that support it. If you cannot quote it, say 'not in material.'"
3. **Priority stress test:** "For each priority, what action (hire, launch, spend, statement on the record) proves it is funded? If the only evidence is website copy, lower the confidence to Low."
4. **POV options:** "Write three alternative two-sentence points of view. Each must describe a pattern companies in their situation hit, must not mention our product, and must end in a question they could answer in one sentence."
5. **Discovery list:** "Turn every assumption and every Low-confidence line into one question I can ask live. Max five."
6. **Freshness:** "List each source with its date. Which claims depend only on sources older than six months?"

### Errors this job invites, and how to catch them

| Error | How to spot it | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Invented figures (revenue, customer count, headcount) | Precise numbers not in your pack | Prompt 2; cut anything unquoted |
| Wrong or stale people | Named executives you did not paste | Verify on their site or professional profile the day of the meeting |
| Priority inflation | Three priorities that read like a strategy deck | Prompt 3; demand an action per priority |
| Product-first POV | Your category or product named in sentence one | Delete and regenerate with Prompt 4 |
| Confident tone on thin evidence | No "Low" confidence anywhere | Ask: "Which line would you bet least on?" |
| Merged companies | Similar names, details from a different firm | Check the website domain and industry match every source |

## 9. Common mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Researching instead of hypothesizing | Two pages of background, no POV | Write the POV first, then keep only facts that support or test it |
| Skipping step 4 | "Only we know" blank without a CRM check | Make CRM and recorder checks part of the 15 minutes |
| Opening with what they lack | "I noticed you don't use..." | Never claim absence. Ask about the process instead |
| Equal effort on every account | Top-tier and long-tail get the same brief | Full brief for top tier only; light touch for the rest |
| Brief built once, never updated | Meeting brief uses research from last quarter | Re-run freshness check the day before |
| Copying a quote as a compliment | "Loved your CEO's interview" | Use the quote's substance, not flattery |

## 10. The 5-minute version

When the meeting is in an hour.

1. Open their newsroom and careers page. Copy the two newest dated items. (2 min)
2. Check your CRM for history. (1 min)
3. Paste into the Quick prompt with: "Only use this material. Three bullets: one priority, one pressure, one question to ask." (1 min)
4. Verify each bullet against the source. Cut anything untraceable. (1 min)

Output: one priority, one pressure, one question. No POV claim you cannot back.

## 11. The deep version

For a strategic account, a large first meeting, or an executive briefing.

| Add | How |
|---|---|
| Wider pack | Last two quarterly reports or calls, all executive interviews in 12 months, full job board scan |
| Stakeholder map | For each buying role: known person, tenure in role, public statements, prior relationship |
| Competitor and incumbent check | What they likely use today, labelled as assumption unless confirmed |
| Three hypotheses, not one | Write three POVs and the evidence that would confirm or kill each |
| Peer review | A colleague scores the brief on Section 4 blind; resolve any criterion where you differ by 2 |
| Pre-meeting refresh | 24 hours before: re-check news and job posts, update freshness line |
| Post-meeting close-out | Record which priorities and assumptions were confirmed or wrong; feed back into the next brief |
