# Competitor Messaging Toolkit: Collection, Teardown Sheet, Phrase Grid, and Claim Scoring

**Use this file when:**
- You are about to compare your message with one competitor's and need the sheets, not just the prompt.
- A homepage or campaign rewrite is starting and you want to know which words to stop using before anyone writes.
- Sellers say "buyers can't tell us apart" and you need to show why, phrase by phrase.
- You have a candidate differentiating claim and need to test whether it is defensible or just a slogan.

The SKILL.md holds the logic. This file holds the instruments. Run them in the order below.

---

## 1. The Teardown Sequence

| Step | What you do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Collect both companies' public copy and date-stamp it | Source pack | 15 min |
| 2 | Collect buyer language from your own calls and reviews | Buyer phrase list | 15-30 min |
| 3 | Fill the teardown sheet for each company | Two teardown sheets (Section 3) | 10 min each |
| 4 | Build the phrase grid | Shared / theirs / ours, crossed with buyer use (Section 4) | 15 min |
| 5 | Run the blind swap test | Pass or fail | 10 min |
| 6 | Score candidate claims | One defensible claim (Section 6) | 15 min |
| 7 | Write the stop-saying list | Retire list with replacements | 10 min |
| 8 | Review with someone outside marketing | Signed-off brief | 20 min |

**One competitor at a time.** Comparing against three at once blurs the shared vocabulary list and inflates the "unique" column.

## 2. Steps 1-2: The Source Pack

### Their copy and yours (same pages for both)

| Source | What to capture | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage hero | Headline, subhead, first CTA, verbatim | The claim they paid most to make |
| Homepage section headings | Every H2, in order | The argument they think wins |
| Top 2-4 product or solution pages | Headline, first paragraph, proof blocks | Where the claim gets specific or does not |
| Pricing page | Plan names, what is gated, who each plan is for | The buyer they actually serve |
| Two recent campaigns | Landing page, ad copy, or launch post from the last 90 days | Where the message is moving |
| Customer stories page | Story titles, outcome numbers, industries | The proof they can actually show |
| Comparison or "vs" pages (theirs about you, yours about them) | Every claim made about the other side | What they think your weakness is |

**Date-stamp every capture.** Campaign copy ages in weeks. A teardown with no capture date cannot be trusted next quarter.

### Buyer language

| Source category | What to pull | How far back |
|---|---|---|
| Call recorder (discovery calls) | The buyer's answer to "what prompted you to look now?" and "what would good look like?" | 90 days, 10-20 calls |
| Closed-lost notes in your CRM | The stated reason, in the buyer's words where recorded | 2 quarters |
| Win interviews | Why they chose you, verbatim | 2-4 quarters |
| Public review sites (yours and theirs) | "What do you like best" and "what problem is it solving" answers | 12 months |
| Support tickets and community posts | How customers describe the job the product does | 90 days |
| RFPs and inbound forms | The words buyers wrote before talking to anyone | 12 months |

**Rule:** record buyer phrases verbatim, with the source. A paraphrase in your own words is not buyer language.

## 3. Step 3: The Teardown Sheet (one per company)

```
TEARDOWN SHEET: [Company]       Captured: [date]       Sources: [list]

Core claim (one sentence, in their words where possible):
Category label they use:          [what they call the thing they sell]
Buyer they speak to:              [role, company size, situation, inferred from copy and pricing]
Pain they name:                   [the problem, as written]
Alternative they attack:          [status quo, spreadsheets, a competitor type, nothing]
Proof offered (list each):        [logo, quote, number, benchmark, award, analyst mention]
Highest proof level (Section 3b): [0-5]
Primary CTA:                      [demo, trial, calculator, content]
Tone:                             [technical, executive, playful, fear-based]
Three most repeated phrases:      [verbatim]
```

### 3b. The proof ladder

Score the strongest proof each company offers for its core claim.

| Level | Proof type | Example | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | None or superlative | "The leading platform" | Slogan |
| 1 | Feature assertion | "Built-in AI scoring" | Says what it is, not what it does |
| 2 | Logos | A strip of customer logos | Social proof, no outcome |
| 3 | Testimonial | A customer quote with a role | Outcome implied, not measured |
| 4 | Customer result with a number | "Cut forecast prep from two days to two hours" with a named customer type | Outcome, measured |
| 5 | Checkable data | A published benchmark, method shown, that a buyer can verify | Outcome, auditable |

**Rule of thumb:** if your proof level and theirs are equal, proof is not a differentiator. If yours is two or more levels higher, proof itself can carry the message.

## 4. Step 4: The Phrase Grid

### Extraction rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Extract noun phrases and verb phrases of 1-4 words that carry a claim ("trusted data," "close faster") | Single generic words ("platform") add noise |
| Count a phrase as shared if both use it or a near-identical variant ("faster pipeline" / "accelerate pipeline") | Buyers hear variants as the same claim |
| Record where each phrase appears (hero, H2, body) | A shared phrase in a hero costs more than one in body copy |
| Mark each phrase Y/N for "appears in buyer language" | This decides what to keep |

### The grid

| | Buyers use it | Buyers do not use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Both companies use it** | **Table stakes.** Say it once, low on the page, to qualify. Never lead with it | **Retire.** Category jargon. It costs space and signals sameness |
| **Only they use it** | **Watch.** They own a buyer word. Decide whether to contest it with stronger proof or concede it | **Ignore.** Their jargon. Do not borrow it |
| **Only you use it** | **Lead candidate.** Score it in Section 6 | **Translate.** Accurate but buyers do not say it. Rewrite into buyer words before using it |

### Phrase log template

| Phrase | Ours (where) | Theirs (where) | Buyer uses? (source) | Grid cell | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

## 5. Step 5: The Blind Swap Test

1. Strip logos, product names, and colors from both homepages' hero and first three section headings.
2. Show both to two people outside marketing (a seller, a CSM, someone in finance) and one customer if you can.
3. Ask: "Which of these is us?" then "What is different about the two?"

| Result | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Both identify correctly and name a real difference | Message is distinct | Teardown is a refinement exercise |
| Correct guess, but the difference named is visual or tone only | Distinct style, same claim | Proceed. The claim is the problem |
| Wrong guess or "I can't tell" | Messages are interchangeable | The stop-saying list is the priority deliverable |

---

## 6. Step 6: Scoring Candidate Claims

List every claim from the "only you use it" row plus any claim your proof supports that neither company makes. Score each 0-2.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unique** | They could say it truthfully today | They could say it with a stretch | They cannot support it |
| **Provable** | No evidence held | Evidence exists but is anecdotal or internal | Customer outcome with a number, or checkable data, in hand |
| **Buyer-relevant** | No buyer phrase maps to it | Maps loosely | Uses or directly answers a recorded buyer phrase |
| **Testable** | Superlative or vague ("best," "smarter") | Specific but hard to verify | A buyer could check it |
| **Durable** | They could match it in one quarter | Two to four quarters | Rooted in something hard to copy (data, model, team, customer base) |

| Total (rule of thumb) | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Lead with it |
| 7-8 | Lead after you close the weakest criterion. Name the evidence needed |
| 5-6 | Supporting point, not the headline |
| 0-4 | Drop |

**Hard stops regardless of total:** Provable = 0 means slogan (SKILL.md rule). Buyer-relevant = 0 means it does not go in the headline (SKILL.md rule).

   Claim: [one sentence, testable]

## 8. Worked Example: Acme vs Competitor B

*Illustrative only. Acme is a fictional 400-person B2B software company selling revenue forecasting software to mid-market sales leaders. "Competitor B" is fictional. All copy and figures are invented.*

### Inputs

Both homepages, three product pages each, two recent campaign pages each, and 22 buyer lines from 15 discovery calls and the last two quarters of closed-lost notes.

### Teardown sheets (condensed)

| Field | Competitor B | Acme |
|---|---|---|
| Core claim | "AI-powered revenue intelligence that helps you close faster" | "Trusted forecasts powered by AI, so you can close faster" |
| Category label | Revenue intelligence | Revenue intelligence |
| Buyer | Enterprise CRO and RevOps (pricing starts at 100 seats) | Mid-market VP Sales (pricing from 20 seats) |
| Pain named | "Missed number" | "Forecast surprises" |
| Alternative attacked | Point tools | Spreadsheets |
| Proof | 24 logos, two testimonials | 9 logos, one benchmark: forecast error before and after across 60 customers, method published |
| Proof level | 3 | 5 |

**Reasoning:** the category label and the closing verb are identical. The real differences are the buyer (enterprise vs mid-market) and the proof (logos vs checkable data). Neither difference is in either headline.

### Phrase grid (extract)

| Phrase | Acme | Competitor B | Buyer uses? | Cell | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| close faster | Hero | Hero | No (0 of 22) | Both / not used | Retire |
| AI-powered | Hero | Hero | No | Both / not used | Retire |
| revenue intelligence | H2 | Hero | No | Both / not used | Retire |
| trusted data | H2 | H2 | Yes (4 of 22, as "data I can trust") | Both / used | Table stakes |
| single source of truth | Body | Body | No | Both / not used | Retire |
| missed number | | Hero | Yes (6 of 22, as "we missed the number") | Theirs / used | Watch |
| forecast surprises | Subhead | | Yes (5 of 22, as "surprises at end of quarter") | Ours / used | Lead candidate |
| forecast error | Benchmark page | | No | Ours / not used | Translate |

### Blind swap

Two sellers and one finance analyst saw both heroes with names removed. Two of three picked wrong. The finance analyst said, "They both say AI and faster." **Fail.** The retire list is the priority.

### Claim scoring

| Candidate claim | Unique | Provable | Buyer-relevant | Testable | Durable | Total | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "The most accurate forecasts in the market" | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 4 | Drop (superlative) |
| "No surprises at quarter end: we publish our customers' forecast accuracy" | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 10 | Lead |
| "Built for mid-market teams" | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 6 | Supporting point |

**Reasoning on the winner:** it uses the buyer's word ("surprises"), turns the untranslated "forecast error" into something buyers say, and rests on the level-5 proof Competitor B does not have. Durable scores 2 because the benchmark needs a customer base and a published method, which takes more than a quarter to copy.

### Output summary

- **Retire:** close faster, AI-powered, revenue intelligence (as a headline label), single source of truth. Replace in the hero by end of month.
- **Defensible claim:** "No surprises at quarter end. We publish our customers' forecast accuracy."
- **Evidence held:** the 60-customer benchmark with published method.
- **Evidence needed:** two customer results with a named role and before-and-after figure, approved for public use (Customer marketing lead, six weeks).
- **Watch:** Competitor B owns "missed number." Do not contest it head-on; "no surprises" answers the same fear.

## 9. Decision Table and Red Flags

| If you see | It means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| More than half your hero phrases are in the "both / not used" cell (rule of thumb) | You are writing in category jargon | Lead with the retire list, not the new claim |
| Your best claim needs proof you do not have | It is a roadmap, not a message | Put the evidence on a plan with an owner. Use the next-best claim meanwhile |
| They own the buyer's main word | They listened better | Contest only if your proof is higher on the ladder; otherwise answer the same fear in other words |
| Both companies use the same category label | Buyers will compare on price and logos | Consider naming the problem instead of the category in your hero |
| The competitor's comparison page attacks a weakness you recognize | They found a real gap | Pass it to product and sellers. Do not answer it in your hero |
| Buyer language comes mostly from your own marketing team | You are testing copy against itself | Re-pull from calls and reviews |
| Your proof level is lower than theirs | Claims will not stick | Fix proof first. A new headline will not help |

## 10. AI Assist: Splitting the Work

| Task | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Extract phrases from pasted copy | Yes | Spot-check 10 against the source |
| Cluster near-identical variants | Yes | Confirm each cluster is one claim |
| Count buyer-phrase matches | Yes | Verify the top five counts |
| Fill teardown sheets | Drafts | Infers the buyer from pricing and gating; the model often misses this |
| Score claims | Drafts scores | Owns Provable and Durable, which need knowledge the model lacks |
| Decide what to stop saying | Suggests | Decides, with whoever owns the homepage |

### Follow-up prompts after the Quick prompt

1. "Build a table of every claim-carrying phrase of one to four words. Columns: phrase, appears in our copy (where), appears in theirs (where), appears in buyer language (count and one example line). Treat near-identical variants as the same phrase and list the variants."
2. "Place every phrase in this grid: both use / only they use / only we use, crossed with buyers use / buyers do not use. Then give the action for each: table stakes, retire, watch, ignore, lead candidate, translate."
3. "Rate the strongest proof each company offers on this ladder: 0 none, 1 feature assertion, 2 logos, 3 testimonial, 4 customer result with a number, 5 checkable published data. Quote the proof."
4. "Score each candidate claim 0 to 2 on unique, provable, buyer-relevant, testable, durable. Mark any claim with provable 0 as a slogan. Show reasoning per score."
5. "Check your own output: list every quoted phrase and confirm it appears verbatim in the material. Remove any that do not."
6. "Write our hero headline and subhead twice: once using only retired phrases, once using the defensible claim and buyer words. Keep each under 20 words."

### Errors this job invites, and how to check

| AI error | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Quotes a phrase that is not in the material | Search the source for the exact string | Run follow-up prompt 5 every time |
| Paraphrases buyer language into marketing language | "Buyers want streamlined workflows" | Ask for verbatim lines with source |
| Proposes a superlative as the defensible claim | "Best," "leading," "most" | Testable score must be 2 |
| Overcounts uniqueness | "Only you" list is long | Ask: "Could the competitor say this truthfully today?" |
| Judges the product, not the message | Output mentions features neither page states | Restate: compare messages, not products |
| Treats the competitor's copy as performance evidence | "Their message is working" | Public copy shows intent, not results |

## 11. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starting with the new claim instead of the retire list | No stop-saying section | SKILL.md rule: decide what to stop saying |
| Using last year's screenshots | No capture date | Re-capture; date-stamp |
| Picking the claim marketing likes best | Highest-scoring claim was not chosen | Score in front of an outside reviewer |
| Buyer language from a single source | All lines come from reviews, or all from wins | Mix calls, losses, reviews |
| Copying the competitor's buyer words wholesale | Your new hero reads like theirs | Answer the same fear in your own proof |
| Treating a clearer competitor message as a better product | Sellers start conceding features | Remind the team this is messaging, not product |

## 12. The 5-Minute Version

1. Paste both heroes and first three headings side by side.
2. Circle every phrase both use. That is your retire list.
3. Ask: "Which phrase would a buyer say on a call?" Keep only those.
4. Ask: "What can we prove with a number that they cannot?" Write it in buyer words.
5. Label it "draft, no buyer data checked."

## 13. The Deep Version

For a rebrand, a homepage rewrite, or a category launch.

| Addition | What it adds |
|---|---|
| 40-60 buyer lines across calls, losses, reviews, and RFPs | Phrase counts you can trust |
| Teardown of the top three competitors, each run separately, then a combined shared-vocabulary list | Category jargon, not just one rival's |
| Blind swap test with five customers | Buyer view, not internal |
| Proof audit: every claim on your site mapped to a proof level | Finds slogans hiding below the hero |
| Message test: two headline variants on paid traffic or in outbound | Behavioral evidence, not opinion |
| Quarterly re-capture of competitor copy | Spots when they move toward your claim |
