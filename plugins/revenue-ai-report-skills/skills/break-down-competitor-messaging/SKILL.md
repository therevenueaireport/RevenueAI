# Break Down Competitor Messaging

> From the [Revenue AI Report skill library](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/skills). Level: Intermediate · Task: Build Campaigns · Setup: About 45 minutes · For: Marketing, Executive and Founder, Enablement

**Outcome:** A clear read of how a competitor positions itself, where your message overlaps, and the claim only you can make.

## When to use
- Your message and a competitor's read the same to an outsider.
- You are rewriting the homepage or a campaign and need to avoid the category's shared vocabulary.
- Sellers say buyers cannot tell the difference between you.

## Inputs you need
- The competitor's homepage, product pages, and two recent campaigns or posts
- Your own homepage and primary message
- The words buyers use in your own calls or reviews

## Quick prompt
Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or any assistant. Replace the [bracketed] parts.

```text
You are a positioning analyst. Using only the material I paste below, compare how [COMPETITOR] and my company describe ourselves. Produce: their core claim in one sentence, the buyer they are speaking to, the proof they offer, the same three for us, a list of phrases we both use, and the claims only one of us makes. End with the single claim we can make that they cannot support, and say what evidence we would need to make it credible. Quote only text that appears in the material.

Material:
[paste their pages, our pages, buyer language]
```

## Process
1. Collect both sets of public copy. Homepage, product pages, two recent campaigns.
2. Add the buyer's own words from calls or reviews, so you can score claims against how buyers talk.
3. Run the comparison. Mark every phrase that both companies use.
4. Pick one claim only you can support, and write the evidence that makes it credible.
5. Decide what to stop saying. Shared vocabulary is the cost, not the win.

## Decision rules
- If both companies use a phrase, it is table stakes, not differentiation.
- A claim you cannot prove with a customer outcome is a slogan. Do not lead with it.
- If buyers never use a term, do not put it in the headline no matter how accurate it is.

## Output
- A side by side of core claim, buyer, and proof.
- A shared-vocabulary list to retire.
- One defensible claim with the evidence needed to support it.

## Quality checks
- Every quoted phrase appears in the pasted material.
- The defensible claim is testable, not a superlative.
- The recommendation says what to stop saying, not only what to add.

## Limitations
- Public copy shows intent, not performance. It does not tell you what is working for them.
- Campaign snapshots age in weeks.
- This compares messages, not products. A clearer message can sit on a weaker product, and often does.

## Worked example
**Input:** Pasted: both homepages, four product pages, six recent posts, and twenty lines of buyer language from call notes.

**Output:** Both lead on faster pipeline and trusted data. Their proof is logos, ours is a published benchmark. Shared phrases to retire: five listed. Defensible claim: published outcome data by function, evidence needed is two named customer results.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Did someone outside marketing read both messages and say which is which?
- [ ] Is the defensible claim backed by evidence you already hold?
- [ ] Is there a decision about what to stop saying?
