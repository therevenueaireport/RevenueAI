# Map Which Revenue Work Stays After AI

> From the [Revenue AI Report skill library](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/skills). Level: Intermediate · Task: Plan · Setup: About an hour · For: Executive and Founder, Revenue Operations, Enablement, Sales Leader

**Outcome:** A read across the eight areas of revenue work showing what AI absorbs, what a human keeps, and where your coverage is thin.

## When to use
- Headcount is flat or falling and the work is not.
- You are planning next year's revenue org and want the work mapped before the boxes.
- One area is quietly carrying work nobody has named.

## Inputs you need
- Headcount and named owners across sales, marketing, operations, enablement, customer success, partnerships, CRO, CEO and founder, and finance
- The AI tools each area uses now
- The work each area says takes the most time

## Quick prompt
Paste into any AI assistant. Replace the [bracketed] parts.

```text
You are a revenue organization analyst. Using the details I paste below, go through eight areas of revenue work in order: sales, marketing, revenue operations, enablement, customer success, partnerships, CRO, CEO and founder, finance. For each, list the work AI can absorb today with evidence from what I gave you, the work that needs a human decision, the current owner, and the coverage risk if that person leaves. End with the two areas where my coverage is thinnest and what I should do first.

Details:
[paste]
```

## Process
1. Collect the inputs above. Thin input produces a confident, wrong answer.
2. Sales: Pipeline, forecast, deal execution, deal desk.
3. Marketing: Demand, brand, category, buyer trust.
4. Revenue operations: Systems, data, agent architecture, orchestration across the stack.
5. Enablement: Skill velocity, adoption, coaching, playbook currency. The role is shifting from governing a technology to building the agents that reduce friction.
6. Customer success: Retention, expansion, health, outcome delivery.
7. Partnerships: Ecosystem leverage, co-sell, embedded distribution.
8. Executive: Narrative, positioning, capital, board.
9. Finance: Unit economics, gross margin, spend efficiency, revenue quality.
10. Write the verdict and the next action with an owner and a date.
11. Have one person who did the work review the output before you share it.

## Decision rules
- Map the work first, then the people. Never the other way around.
- Work that needs a judgment call with money or a customer on the line stays with a human.
- An area with one owner and no backup is a coverage risk regardless of tooling.
- Do not cut a seat because a tool covers part of its work.

## Output
- Eight rows: area, absorbed work, human-owned work, owner, coverage risk.
- The two thinnest areas with a first action.
- An open-questions list for work you could not place.

## Quality checks
- Every area has a named owner or an explicit gap.
- Absorbed work is tied to a tool you actually run.
- The map names risks, not only opportunities.

## Limitations
- The map reflects what you describe. Undocumented work stays invisible.
- It sizes the work, not the budget.

## Worked example
**Input:** Sales: 12 reps, 2 managers. Enablement: one person covering onboarding, content, and tooling. Partnerships: no dedicated owner, split between the CRO and a marketing manager.

**Output:** Thinnest areas: enablement and partnerships. Enablement has one owner across three domains with no backup. First action: move onboarding content generation to a documented AI workflow and name a second reviewer.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Did each area owner review their own row?
- [ ] Is any human-owned work listed as absorbed?
- [ ] Is the coverage risk shared with the people who can fund it?
