# Revenue Work Map Toolkit: Work Inventory, Task Sort, and Coverage Risk

**Use this file when:**
- Headcount is flat or falling and the work is not, and you need to see where it actually sits.
- You are planning next year's revenue org and want the work mapped before anyone draws boxes.
- A leader wants to cut a seat because "AI covers that now" and you need to check what the seat really does.
- One person or one area is quietly carrying work nobody has named.

This file holds the instruments. The SKILL.md holds the logic. The eight areas follow the Eight-Seat Read ([the Eight-Seat Read, a framework from The Revenue AI Report](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/frameworks/eight-seat-read)): sales, marketing, revenue operations, enablement, customer success, partnerships, executive (CRO, CEO, and founder), and revenue finance. The order of work is fixed: **map the work first, then the people.**

---

## 1. The mapping sequence

| Step | What you do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pull headcount, owners, and the AI tools each area runs | Input sheet | 15 min |
| 2 | List the work in each area, as tasks | Work inventory | 15 min, plus owner input |
| 3 | Sort each task: absorbed, human decision, human for trust, unplaced | Task sort | 15 min |
| 4 | Check every "absorbed" task against a tool you actually run | Evidence column | 5 min |
| 5 | Score coverage risk per area | Risk score | 10 min |
| 6 | Name the two thinnest areas and a first action for each | Verdict | 10 min |
| 7 | Each area owner reviews their own row | Signed map | 1-2 days elapsed |

---

## 2. Step 1: What to pull

| Data | System category | How far back | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headcount and reporting lines by area | HR system, org chart | Current | Who sits where |
| Named owner per area and per major task | Leaders, org chart | Current | Ownership is often assumed, not assigned |
| AI tools in use, by area | SSO app list, expense reports, admin consoles | Last 90 days of use | "Absorbed" must point to a tool someone uses |
| Usage per tool on a named workflow | Tool admin consoles | Last 90 days | Bought is not the same as running |
| Where time goes | Calendar sampling, a short survey, manager estimate | Two typical weeks | The work each area says takes the most time |
| Open roles and recent departures | HR system | Last 12 months | Work that moved silently when someone left |
| Process documentation | Shared drive, wiki | Current | Undocumented work stays invisible |

---

## 3. Step 2: The work inventory

Ask each area for tasks, not job titles. "Which parts of your week would you hand off?" gets honest answers. "Could AI do your job?" gets defensive ones.

### Seed list by area (adapt to your org)

| Area | Typical work to list |
|---|---|
| Sales | Prospecting, discovery, deal execution, multi-threading, forecast calls, pipeline review, deal desk and pricing approvals, contract redlines, call notes and CRM updates |
| Marketing | Demand programs, content production, brand and positioning, category narrative, buyer trust content, campaign reporting, website, events |
| Revenue operations | CRM and data administration, routing, territory and quota models, reporting, integrations, agent and workflow architecture, tool governance |
| Enablement | Onboarding and ramp, content and playbook upkeep, coaching programs, tool adoption, certification, building agents that remove friction |
| Customer success | Onboarding customers, health monitoring, renewals, expansion, escalations, outcome reviews, handoffs from sales |
| Partnerships | Partner recruiting, co-sell, partner enablement, marketplace and embedded distribution, partner pipeline tracking |
| Executive | Company narrative, positioning decisions, capital allocation, board reporting, executive relationships on top deals, hiring leaders |
| Revenue finance | Unit economics, gross margin, spend efficiency, commission calculation, revenue quality, AI spend tracking, budget variance |

### Hidden work detector

Ask every owner these four questions. Write down anything that is not already on the list.

| Question | What it surfaces |
|---|---|
| If you were out for two weeks, what would break first? | Single-owner work |
| What do people come to you for that is not in your job description? | Unnamed work |
| What did you pick up when someone left? | Work that moved without a decision |
| What do you do every month that nobody has asked you to stop doing? | Legacy work that may not need doing at all |

## 4. Step 3: The task sort

| Bucket | Definition | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Absorbed** | AI does it today with a tool you run, and a human checks only exceptions | Name the tool, the workflow, and the usage evidence |
| **Assisted** | AI drafts or prepares it; a human finishes and owns it | Name the tool; the human still spends real time on it |
| **Human decision** | A judgment call with money or a customer on the line | Would a wrong call cost revenue, margin, or a relationship? |
| **Human for trust** | A machine could do it, but the buyer, customer, board, or team expects a person | Would the other party object if they learned a machine did it? |
| **Unplaced** | You cannot tell yet | Goes to the open-questions list, not to a guess |

**Decision rule from the SKILL.md:** work that needs a judgment call with money or a customer on the line stays with a human. When a task is part judgment and part preparation, split it: the preparation can be absorbed or assisted, the call cannot.

### Evidence levels for "absorbed"

| Level | Description | Counts as absorbed? |
|---|---|---|
| Running and measured | Tool in production on this task, usage and quality tracked | Yes |
| Running, unmeasured | Tool in production, no quality check | Yes, flagged as a risk |
| Piloting | Limited users or a trial | No. List as assisted or candidate |
| Bought, not running | Licenses exist, little use | No |
| Possible | A tool could do it; you do not run one | No. Note as an opportunity, not coverage |

---

## 5. Step 5: Coverage risk score

Score each area 0, 1, or 2 on each line. Higher is riskier.

| Line | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owners | Two or more people can own the area's critical work | One owner, a partial backup | One owner, no backup, or no owner |
| Concentration | Each owner covers one domain | An owner covers two domains | An owner covers three or more domains |
| Documentation | Critical processes are written down and current | Partly documented | Lives in someone's head |
| Human-decision load | Human decisions are spread across several people | Concentrated in two people | Concentrated in one person |
| Departure impact | A departure slows the area | A departure stops one critical process | A departure stops the area |
| Absorption quality | Absorbed work is measured | Some absorbed work is unmeasured | Absorbed work is claimed with no tool evidence |

| Total (rule of thumb) | Read |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Covered |
| 4-6 | Watch. Name a backup within the quarter |
| 7-12 | Thin. Candidate for the two thinnest areas |

**Decision rule:** an area with one owner and no backup is a coverage risk regardless of tooling. It is scored "Thin" at minimum, whatever the total says.

---

## 6. Step 6: Choosing the two thinnest areas and first actions

| Tie-break order | Why |
|---|---|
| 1. Highest risk score | The measure |
| 2. Most human-decision work with money or a customer on the line | A gap there costs revenue first |
| 3. Fewest owners | Single points of failure |
| 4. Least documented | Hardest to recover after a departure |

### First-action library

| Risk pattern | First action (small, owned, dated) |
|---|---|
| No owner | Name one interim owner in writing and list the work they now own |
| One owner, no backup | Name a second reviewer and have them shadow the most critical process once |
| Work lives in someone's head | Record the owner walking through it; have an AI assistant draft the process document; owner corrects it |
| Owner spread over three domains | Move the most repeatable domain to a documented AI-assisted workflow; keep the judgment work with the owner |
| Absorbed work unmeasured | Add a weekly spot check of ten outputs against a written standard |
| Human-decision work concentrated in one person | Write the decision criteria down so a second person can make the call |

**Rule:** do not cut a seat because a tool covers part of its work. A seat that loses its repeatable tasks usually keeps its judgment tasks, and those need to be done better, not by fewer people by default.

## 8. Decision table

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| An area has no named owner | It is an explicit gap in the map and a thinnest-area candidate |
| A task is listed as absorbed but no tool is named | Move it to "possible" and remove it from coverage |
| A task involves pricing, contract terms, renewal risk, or board numbers | Human decision, whatever the tool can draft |
| An owner covers three or more domains | Score concentration at 2 and check documentation |
| Someone proposes cutting a seat because a tool covers part of it | List the seat's remaining human-owned work first. Decide only after the owner reviews the row |
| Work has no owner and nobody misses it | Put it on the open-questions list as a candidate to stop doing |
| The map shows no risks | The map is incomplete. Run the hidden work detector again |

---

## 9. Red flags

| Red flag | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Rows written from the org chart, not from tasks | People were mapped first; work is missing |
| "AI handles it" with no tool and workflow named | Absorption is assumed, not observed |
| Negotiation, forecasting calls, or renewals marked absorbed | Human-owned work listed as absorbed |
| Partnerships or finance rows are blank | Often the areas carrying unnamed work |
| One name appears in three or more rows | Concentration risk across areas, not just within one |
| No area owner has reviewed their row | The map reflects the author's view, not the work |

## 10. Worked example: Acme (illustrative)

Acme is a fictional 400-person B2B software company with a revenue org of about 120. All details are illustrative.

**Inputs.** Sales: 30 AEs, 8 SDRs, 4 managers, a VP, and one deal desk analyst. Marketing: 12. RevOps: 4, including one GTM engineer. Enablement: 1. Customer success: 18 CSMs, 2 managers, a VP. Partnerships: no dedicated owner; split between the CRO and a marketing manager. Executive: CRO and CEO. Revenue finance: one FP&A analyst, about half-time on revenue. AI tools in production: call recorder with summaries, AI assistant seats for all, an AI drafting feature in the sales engagement tool, a support-ticket classifier in CS.

**Map (abridged).**

| Area | Absorbed (evidence) | Human-owned | Owner / backup | Risk | One-line risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | Call notes and CRM activity logging (call recorder, running and measured) | Discovery, negotiation, forecast calls, pricing approvals | VP Sales / managers | 4 | Deal desk is one analyst; pricing approvals stop if they are out |
| Marketing | First drafts of nurture emails (assistant, running, unmeasured) | Positioning, category narrative, campaign choices | VP Marketing / director | 3 | Draft quality unmeasured |
| RevOps | Lead routing rules (configuration, measured) | Territory and quota design, tool governance | Director / GTM engineer | 4 | Only the GTM engineer can change integrations |
| Enablement | None running | Onboarding, coaching programs, content upkeep | Enablement lead / none | 8 | One person, three domains, no backup |
| Customer success | Ticket classification (classifier, measured) | Renewals, escalations, expansion calls | VP CS / managers | 2 | Covered |
| Partnerships | None | Co-sell decisions, partner recruiting | CRO and a marketing manager / none | 10 | No owner; partner pipeline untracked |
| Executive | Board deck first draft (assistant, running, unmeasured) | Narrative, capital allocation, board answers | CEO / CRO | 3 | Covered |
| Revenue finance | None running | Unit economics, commission calculation, AI spend tracking | FP&A analyst, half-time / none | 10 | One part-time owner; commission model lives in one spreadsheet nobody else understands |

**Corrections made during review.** Marketing first listed "competitive positioning" as absorbed because the assistant drafts battlecards. The review moved it to human-owned: the drafts are assisted, the positioning call is a judgment with customers on the line. The hidden work detector surfaced that the deal desk analyst also reconciles pricing exceptions against signed contracts each month, work nobody had named. Added to the sales row.

**Two thinnest areas.**

1. **Revenue finance (10).** Chosen over partnerships on the tie-break: it carries human-decision work with money on the line (commissions, unit economics) and has no documentation. **First action:** the analyst records a walkthrough of the commission model; an AI assistant drafts the process document from the recording and spreadsheet; the analyst corrects it; a RevOps analyst is named as backup and runs one month's calculation in parallel. Owner: CFO. Due: end of next month.
2. **Partnerships (10).** No owner at all. **First action:** name one interim owner in writing (the marketing manager, with 20 percent of time formally assigned) and list the partner work now owned, including tracking partner-sourced pipeline in the CRM. Owner: CRO. Due: two weeks.

**Watch list.** Enablement at 8 goes on next quarter's plan: the first move is to document onboarding and name a second reviewer, the same pattern as finance.

**Open questions.** Who owns AI spend tracking across areas: finance or RevOps? Does anyone still use the monthly marketing attribution report?

**Why no seat cuts.** The SDR team's drafting is assisted, not absorbed; qualification calls and meeting quality remain human-owned. The map gives no basis to cut, and the SKILL.md rule says not to cut a seat because a tool covers part of its work.

## 11. AI assist

### Split the work

| Task | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Turn org chart and survey answers into a task inventory | Draft | Each owner adds missing tasks |
| Sort tasks into buckets | Propose with reasons | Owner confirms, especially decision versus assisted |
| Match absorbed tasks to tools | Cross-reference your tool list | Confirms usage evidence in admin consoles |
| Score coverage risk | Compute from your inputs | Checks the owner and backup lines are true |
| Draft process documentation for thin areas | Draft from recordings and files | Owner corrects and signs |

### Follow-up prompts after the Quick prompt

```text
For every task you listed as absorbed, name the tool from my list that does it and the evidence I gave you. If I did not name a tool for it, move the task to "possible" and say so.
```

```text
List every task in the map that involves a judgment with money or a customer on the line. Confirm each one is human-owned. Flag any you placed elsewhere.
```

```text
Score each area 0-2 on owners, concentration, documentation, human-decision load, departure impact, and absorption quality, using only my inputs. Where an input is missing, score it as unknown, not as 0.
```

```text
Write four hidden-work questions for the owner of [area], tailored to what I told you about that area.
```

### Check the AI's output for these errors

| Error this job invites | How to spot it |
|---|---|
| Absorption from general knowledge | "AI can handle X" with no tool from your list |
| Mapping people first | Rows organized by job title, not by work |
| Judgment work marked absorbed | Negotiation, pricing, renewals, or board answers in the absorbed column |
| Missing areas scored as covered | Blank partnerships or finance rows with a low risk score |
| Seat recommendations | The output suggests cuts; the skill sizes the work, not the budget |

## 12. Common mistakes and how to spot them

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Drawing boxes first | Map starts from the org chart | Start from the work inventory |
| Counting bought tools as coverage | Absorbed column lists tools with little use | Apply the evidence levels |
| Treating "one owner" as covered | Owner column full, backup column empty | Score owners line at 2 |
| Skipping owner review | No sign-off line | Each owner reviews their row before sharing |
| Showing only opportunities | No risks in the summary | Lead the summary with the two thinnest areas |
| Using the map as a cut list | Seats named for removal | Use it to place work; budget is a separate decision |

---

## 13. The 5-minute version

1. Write the eight areas down the page.
2. For each, write the owner and whether there is a backup. Mark any area with one owner and no backup.
3. For each, write one task AI does today and the tool that does it. If no tool, write "none."
4. For each, write the one decision a human must keep.
5. Circle the two areas with no backup and the most money or customer risk. Write one first action and an owner for each.

## The deep version (annual planning or a reorg)

- Run the hidden work detector with every owner, in person, and have each team sort its own tasks in the room.
- Pull 90 days of usage evidence for every tool claimed as absorbing work.
- Score coverage risk for all eight areas and check for one name appearing across several rows.
- Have every area owner sign their row, then share the risk summary with the people who can fund the fixes.
- Re-run quarterly. Once a quarter of production data exists, add the Eight-Seat Read metrics (dollar, adoption, quality, reversal) per area to see what the absorbed work is actually producing.
