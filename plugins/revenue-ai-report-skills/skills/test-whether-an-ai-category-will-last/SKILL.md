# Test Whether an AI Product Category Will Last

> From the [Revenue AI Report skill library](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/skills). Level: Advanced · Task: Evaluate AI · Setup: About 45 minutes · For: Executive and Founder, Revenue Operations, Revenue Finance, GTM Engineering

**Outcome:** A read on whether the tool you are about to buy is a durable category or a feature the platform absorbs next year.

## When to use
- A multi-year contract is on the table for a young AI category.
- Your platform vendor has announced overlapping functionality.
- Two tools in the stack are converging on the same job.

## Inputs you need
- The tool, the category, and the contract length under discussion
- What your existing platforms already do in that area
- The workflow the tool would own end to end
- What happens to the work if the vendor disappears

## Quick prompt
Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or any assistant. Replace the [bracketed] parts.

```text
You are a technology buyer's analyst. Using the details I paste below, test whether this is a durable category or an absorbable feature. Check: is there existing buyer intent for the category itself, does the tool unlock economics my platforms cannot, does it own a whole workflow or one step, where does the human decision sit, and what is the switching cost if it is absorbed. Give a verdict of durable, contested, or absorbable, with the evidence for each check. Then give me the contract terms that protect me if the verdict is contested.

Details:
[paste]
```

## Process
1. Collect the inputs above. Thin input produces a confident, wrong answer.
2. Replace or amplify: Does it make a person obsolete or make a person more powerful? Amplification compounds. Replacement plays lose to larger models over time.
3. Existing intent: Does it meet buyers where they already are, or does it have to manufacture the demand? Manufactured demand stretches cycles and inflates acquisition cost.
4. New work: Could this work exist without AI, or is it a faster version of old work? Genuinely new work creates categories. Faster old work gets compressed.
5. Economics unlocked: Does it make something previously unaffordable affordable? Unlocked economics create new buyers. Cost reduction alone is copyable.
6. Where does the human go: In the amplified workflow, where does the human's remaining leverage sit? If the answer is nowhere, the seat evaporates and so does the anchor for the relationship.
7. Write the verdict and the next action with an owner and a date.
8. Have one person who did the work review the output before you share it.

## Decision rules
- A tool that owns one step of a workflow is easier to absorb than one that owns the whole workflow.
- If your platform already ships a weak version, assume a stronger version arrives within a year.
- Contested verdicts get short contracts, not discounts.
- Switching cost is a risk to price, not a reason to sign longer.

## Output
- A verdict of durable, contested, or absorbable, with per-check evidence.
- The contract terms that fit the verdict, including length and exit.
- The signal to watch that would change the verdict.

## Quality checks
- The verdict cites what your own platforms do, not the market in general.
- No invented roadmap claims about any vendor.
- The exit path is written before signature.

## Limitations
- Vendor roadmaps are not public and should not be guessed.
- A category can be absorbable and still worth buying for two years. The test prices the risk.

## Worked example
**Input:** Tool: AI meeting-notes product, three-year contract proposed. Our CRM ships basic summaries. The tool also owns coaching scorecards and manager workflows.

**Output:** Verdict: contested. Summaries are absorbable, the coaching workflow is not yet. Terms: one year with a renewal option, exit on data export within 30 days. Watch signal: the CRM shipping manager scorecards.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Did procurement see the exit terms?
- [ ] Is the watch signal assigned to someone with a review date?
- [ ] Are any vendor roadmap claims stated as fact?
