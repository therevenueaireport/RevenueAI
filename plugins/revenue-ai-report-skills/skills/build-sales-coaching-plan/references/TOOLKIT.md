# Coaching Plan Toolkit: Diagnosis Map, Observation Sheet, Practice Menu, and the Day-30 Verdict

**Use this file when:**
- You are about to build a 30-day plan for one rep and need to choose the one skill from evidence, not a hunch.
- A rep is missing number and you need to find where in the funnel it breaks before you coach anything.
- One-on-ones have drifted into deal status and you need a ten-minute weekly structure that coaches.
- You have an AI-drafted plan and need to check it picked the right skill and an honest measure.

The SKILL.md holds the process and the decision rules. This file holds the instruments.

---

## 1. The Sequence

| Step | What you do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pull the rep's numbers against team numbers | Funnel comparison | 15 min |
| 2 | Observe 2-3 real calls or deals with the observation sheet | Scored observations | 20 min per call |
| 3 | Ask the rep for their own view first | Rep self-assessment | 10 min, in the one-on-one |
| 4 | Map the drop to a candidate skill | One skill, with evidence | 10 min |
| 5 | Choose two practice activities | Practice plan | 10 min |
| 6 | Agree the day-30 measure with the rep | Shared measure, written down | 10 min |
| 7 | Run weekly ten-minute reviews | Practice adjusted, target unchanged | 10 min weekly |
| 8 | Day-30 verdict | Worked / re-diagnose / next skill | 20 min |

---

## 2. Step 1: Numbers to Pull

Compare the rep to the team median for the same role and segment. Pull from your CRM and, where available, your call recorder and sales engagement tool.

| Metric | Source (category) | Window | What a gap suggests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activity volume (calls, emails, meetings booked) | Sales engagement tool, CRM | Last 90 days | Effort or prioritization |
| Meeting-to-opportunity conversion | CRM | Last 2 quarters | Qualification or discovery |
| Stage-by-stage conversion | CRM | Last 2 quarters | Which stage breaks |
| Days in each stage | CRM | Last 2 quarters | Where deals stall |
| Win rate and average deal size | CRM | Last 2-4 quarters | Late-stage skill or deal selection |
| Discount at close | Quotes or orders | Last 2-4 quarters | Value framing, negotiation |
| Multi-threading (contacts per opportunity) | CRM contact roles, call recorder | Open and last 2 quarters | Single-threading risk |
| Talk-to-listen ratio, questions asked | Call recorder | Last 30 days | Discovery habits (use as a pointer, not a verdict) |

**Sample size rule of thumb:** a stage rate based on fewer than about 10 deals is a pointer, not proof. Say so in the plan and lean harder on observation.

---

## 3. Step 4: Diagnosis Map (Where It Breaks to What to Coach)

Find the first stage where the rep falls well below the team, then confirm with observation. Coach the earliest real break: fixing a later stage does not help if deals never reach it.

| Where the rep's numbers drop | Candidate skill | Confirm on calls by looking for |
|---|---|---|
| Low activity, normal conversion | Prioritization and time management | Calendar and task review, not a call skill |
| Normal activity, few meetings booked | Opening and relevance in outreach | Generic messages, no reason to meet now |
| Meetings held, few become opportunities | Discovery: finding a real problem and its cost | Feature talk early, no quantified pain |
| Early stage fine, mid stage drops | Confirming the buying process and the decision makers | Proposal before process is known |
| Mid stage fine, late stage drops or slips | Building the business case; multi-threading | One contact, no economic buyer met |
| Wins at normal rate, deep discounts | Value framing and negotiation | Price raised by rep first, concessions without trades |
| Long cycles everywhere | Next-step discipline | Calls end without a dated, mutual next step |
| Good numbers, poor forecast accuracy | Deal qualification honesty | Commits that do not match evidence |

**Decision rules (SKILL.md):** one skill per 30 days. If the data and the rep disagree, coach the data and show the rep the numbers that led you there.

## 4. Step 2: Call Observation Sheet

Use one sheet per call. Score only behaviors tied to the candidate skill, plus a free note. Secondhand impressions do not count as observations.

```
OBSERVATION SHEET
Rep: [ ]   Call/deal: [ ]   Stage: [ ]   Date: [ ]   Observer: [ ]
Candidate skill: [ ]

Behavior                                   Score (0-3 or N/A)   Evidence (timestamp or quote)
B1: [verb + object + condition]
B2:
B3:

The moment that mattered most: [timestamp, what happened, what could have happened]
One thing the rep did well (specific):
```

### Behavior scoring scale

| Score | Label | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Absent | Did not happen when the situation called for it |
| 1 | Attempted | Started but incomplete, mistimed, or dropped when the buyer pushed back |
| 2 | Executed | Done correctly |
| 3 | Adapted | Done correctly and tailored to this buyer |
| N/A | Not applicable | Situation never called for it. Do not score as 0 |

### Example behaviors for common skills

| Skill | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| Discovery | Asks about the cost of the problem; asks what happens if nothing changes; summarizes the problem back and gets agreement |
| Confirming buying process | Asks how a similar past purchase was approved; names approvers by role; walks the buyer's date back through the steps |
| Multi-threading | Asks who else is affected; secures a meeting with a second role; maps the economic buyer |
| Next-step discipline | Proposes a dated next step with an agenda; confirms it before the call ends; records it in the CRM |
| Value framing | Ties price to the buyer's stated cost of the problem; trades concessions for commitments |

**Rule of thumb:** if a skill needs more than five behaviors to describe, it is two skills. Pick one.

## 5. Step 3: Rep Self-Assessment Questions

Ask before showing your view. Write the answers down verbatim.

1. Where do your deals usually get stuck?
2. Which of your recent deals did you lose that you thought you would win? What happened?
3. What do you do on a call that you think works best?
4. If you could get better at one thing this month, what would make the biggest difference to your number?
5. What gets in the way of doing that already?

### When the rep and the data disagree

| Pattern | Likely meaning | How to handle |
|---|---|---|
| Rep names the same skill as the data | Rep sees the gap | Move fast; the rep can co-design the practice |
| Rep names a later-stage skill, data shows an earlier break | Rep sees symptoms, not cause | Walk through the funnel comparison together; coach the data |
| Rep names something outside their control (leads, pricing, territory) | Could be true, could be deflection | Check it against team data. If true, escalate it separately and still pick one skill |
| Rep says "nothing, I am fine" with a clear gap | Rep does not see it yet | Use one recorded moment, played together, rather than numbers alone |

## 6. Step 5: Practice Activity Menu

Choose two activities per week. Each must happen in real work or a short focused drill, not an extra project. At least one should be real-call application, not only rehearsal: behavior change most often stalls between practicing a skill and applying it on live calls, and loses the feedback loop after that ([LOPAFT, a framework from The Revenue AI Report](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/frameworks/lopaft): Learn, Observe, Practice, Apply, Feedback, Teach). The weekly review in Section 9 is the feedback step.

| Activity | Time | Best for | How it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live-call commitment | 0 extra min | Any behavior | Rep commits to doing B1 on every relevant call this week and logs it in the CRM |
| Recorded moment review | 15 min | Discovery, process, value | Rep finds one moment from their own call where the behavior should have happened and rewrites what they would say |
| Short role-play | 10 min | Objection-heavy moments | Manager plays the buyer for the one moment that matters; repeat three times |
| Peer listen | 20 min | Seeing what good looks like | Rep listens to a peer's call where the behavior was done well and names what made it work |
| Pre-call plan | 5 min per call | Process, multi-threading, next steps | Rep writes the one thing they must learn before the call ends |
| AI practice partner | 10 min | Rehearsal volume | Rep rehearses the moment with an AI assistant playing a skeptical buyer, then compares against the rubric |

## 7. Step 6: The Day-30 Measure

The measure must be observable and agreed by the rep (SKILL.md). Use two parts: a behavior measure the rep controls and an outcome measure the business cares about.

| Part | Example | Why both |
|---|---|---|
| Behavior (leading) | B1 executed (score 2+) on at least 4 of 5 observed relevant calls | Moves within 30 days; the rep controls it |
| Outcome (lagging) | Stage 2 to 3 conversion within 20 percent of team rate | The reason for coaching; may need more than 30 days if deal volume is low |

**Low-volume rule of thumb:** if the rep will have fewer than about 10 deals passing the relevant stage in 30 days, make the behavior measure the day-30 verdict and track the outcome for another 30 to 60 days. Write this down at the start so nobody moves the goalposts later.

## 8. The 30-Day Plan Template

```
30-DAY COACHING PLAN
Rep: [ ]   Manager: [ ]   Start: [date]   Day-30 verdict: [date]

FOCUS SKILL (one)
  [skill]

EVIDENCE
  Numbers: [rep vs team, stage, sample size]
  Observations: [call 1 score + moment] [call 2] [call 3]
  Rep's view: [verbatim]   Agree with data? [Y/N, and how we discussed it]

BEHAVIORS WE ARE BUILDING
  B1: [ ]  B2: [ ]  B3: [ ]

PRACTICE (two per week)
  1. [activity, when, how logged]
  2. [activity, when, how logged]

WHAT THE MANAGER WATCHES ON CALLS
  [behaviors, which calls, how many per week]

DAY-30 MEASURE (agreed by rep: Y/N)
  Behavior: [ ]
  Outcome: [ ]   (verdict now or extended tracking? [ ])

WEEKLY REVIEW
  [day, time, 10 minutes, on the calendar: Y/N]
```

## 9. Step 7: The Ten-Minute Weekly Review

| Minute | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | Rep reports practice done: count, not adjectives |
| 2-6 | Look at one moment from this week's calls together |
| 6-8 | Adjust the practice if it is not happening or not working |
| 8-10 | Confirm next week's two activities and the call the manager will observe |

**Rule (SKILL.md):** adjust the practice, not the target. Deal status goes in a different meeting.

| Week signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Practice not happening | Make it smaller or attach it to an existing routine |
| Practice happening, behavior scores flat | Change the activity type (from commitment to role-play, for example) |
| Behavior scores rising | Keep going; add a harder call to the observation set |
| Rep raises a different problem | Note it for the next 30-day plan; do not switch skills mid-cycle |

## 10. Step 8: The Day-30 Verdict

| Result | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|
| Behavior and outcome both moved | Worked | Move to the next skill; check this one holds in 60 days |
| Behavior moved, outcome not yet (low volume) | On track | Track outcome for 30 more days while starting the next skill |
| Behavior moved, outcome did not, with enough volume | Wrong diagnosis: the behavior was not the cause | Re-diagnose from the funnel; do not extend |
| Behavior did not move, practice happened | Practice did not fit the skill, or the skill needs a prerequisite | Re-diagnose; check the prerequisite skill |
| Behavior did not move, practice did not happen | Follow-through failure (rep or manager) | Name it directly; restart with smaller practice |

**Rule (SKILL.md):** no movement at day 30 means the diagnosis was wrong. Re-diagnose; do not extend the same plan.

## 11. Red Flags

| Red flag | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Plan lists three skills | Manager could not choose | Use the diagnosis map; take the earliest break |
| Evidence is "seems to struggle with" | Impression, not observation | Observe two calls before building the plan |
| Measure is "more confident" or "better discovery" | Not observable | Rewrite as a behavior count or a stage rate |
| Weekly review keeps turning into deal status | Old habit wins | Separate meetings; first two minutes are practice counts |
| Coaching topic appears in a comp or performance discussion | Rep will optimize for the score, not the skill | Keep coaching data out of compensation conversations |
| AI call scores used as the only evidence | Scores may not match how your managers judge the skill | Check AI scores against a manager's score on a few calls first |

## 12. Worked Example: Acme, Rep A

*Fictional and illustrative. All numbers are made up for the example.*

**Context:** Acme, a 400-person B2B software company. Rep A, an account executive in the mid-market team, is at 70 percent of quota for the half. The manager's impression: "needs to close harder."

**Step 1, numbers (last two quarters, Rep A vs team median):**

| Metric | Rep A | Team | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meetings to stage 1 | 48% | 45% | At team |
| Stage 1 to 2 | 62% | 60% | At team |
| Stage 2 to 3 (proposal accepted into evaluation) | 22% | 44% | Half the team rate |
| Stage 3 to win | 41% | 43% | At team |
| Contacts per opportunity | 1.6 | 2.8 | Low |
| Deals entering stage 2 per month | about 12 | | Enough volume for an outcome verdict |

**Reasoning:** the break is at stage 2 to 3, not at close. "Close harder" would coach the wrong stage. Low contacts per opportunity is a second signal that fits the same stage.

**Step 2, observations (three stage-2 calls):** strong discovery on all three (B-scores 2-3). On two calls the rep promised a proposal before asking how decisions get made. On one call the buyer said "I'll need to run this by our CFO" and the rep did not ask about the CFO's process. Process behaviors: 0, 1, 0.

**Step 3, rep's view:** "I need better pricing conversations; buyers go quiet after the proposal." Partly agrees with the data: the rep sees where deals die but attributes it to price.

**Step 4, skill:** confirming the buying process before proposing. Evidence: deals die after the proposal, not before it; observed process behaviors scored 0-1; low contacts per opportunity. Shown to the rep with the stage table and the CFO moment played back.

**Behaviors:** B1 asks how the last similar purchase was approved; B2 names at least one approver besides the champion; B3 sends a proposal only after B1 and B2, and logs the process in the CRM.

**Practice:**
1. Live-call commitment: B1 on every stage-2 call; answer recorded in the CRM process field.
2. Recorded moment review, weekly: rep finds one moment where the process question should have been asked and writes the line.

**Day-30 measure (agreed):** behavior: B1 and B2 executed on at least 8 of 10 stage-2 calls, checked from CRM fields and two observed calls. Outcome: stage 2 to 3 conversion for deals entering stage 2 in the window, within 20 percent of team rate, read at day 45 to let decisions land.

**Weekly reviews:** week 1, practice happened on 5 of 9 calls. The rep forgot on calls with existing contacts. Adjusted: added a one-line pre-call reminder. Week 3: 9 of 10. A pricing objection came up; noted for the next cycle, no switch.

**Day 30:** behavior measure hit (9 of 10). Outcome read at day 45: stage 2 to 3 at 37 percent, within 20 percent of the team's 44 (the threshold is 35.2). Verdict: worked. Next 30 days: multi-threading to the economic buyer, which the data still flags.

## 13. AI Assist

### Split the work

| Task | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Funnel comparison | Compute rep vs team gaps from an export | Supply clean data; check sample sizes |
| Observation | Draft a first-pass score from transcripts, with quotes | Watch the key moments; own the final score |
| Skill choice | Propose a skill using the diagnosis map | Decide; explain it to the rep |
| Practice | Suggest activities; play a buyer for rehearsal | Hold the rep to it |
| Weekly review | Summarize the week's logged behaviors | Run the conversation |
| Verdict | Compute measures | Judge and decide next step |

### Follow-up prompts to use after the Quick prompt

1. "Compare this rep's stage-by-stage conversion to the team numbers I pasted. Name the earliest stage where the rep is well below team, and give the sample size behind each rate."
2. "From these transcripts, find every moment where the rep should have [behavior]. Quote the moment and say whether it happened, scored 0 to 3. Return 'not discussed' instead of guessing."
3. "The rep believes the problem is [rep's view]. Write a short, respectful explanation, using their own numbers, of why the data points somewhere else."
4. "Write three observable behaviors for this skill, each starting with a verb, each something I could see in a recording or a CRM field."
5. "Rewrite the day-30 measure as two parts: a behavior count the rep controls and a stage outcome. Tell me whether 30 days gives enough deals to judge the outcome."
6. "Play a skeptical buyer at stage 2 for practice. Stay in role. After five exchanges, score the rep's attempt against these behaviors: [paste]."

### Errors this job invites, and how to catch them

| AI error | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Picks the skill the rep named, ignoring the data | Plan echoes the self-assessment | Prompt 1, then ask which stage the skill affects |
| Several skills bundled as one ("discovery and qualification and closing") | Behaviors span stages | Prompt 4; one stage, one skill |
| Generic practice ("role-play objections") | Not tied to the chosen behaviors | Require each activity to name B1, B2, or B3 |
| Inferred call behavior | Scores without quotes or timestamps | Prompt 2; discard unquoted scores |
| Outcome measure on tiny samples | Stage rate from a handful of deals | Prompt 5 |

## 14. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Coaching the stage where the pain is felt, not where it starts | Coaching close when the break is mid-funnel | Use the earliest break |
| Telling the rep the skill before asking them | Self-assessment column empty | Ask first, in their words |
| Measure set by manager alone | Rep cannot state the measure | Agree it together, write it in the plan |
| Extending a plan that did not work | "Another 30 days" on the same skill | Re-diagnose |
| Skipping weekly reviews | Nothing on the calendar | Book all four on day one |

## 15. The 5-Minute Version

1. Put the rep's stage conversions next to the team's. Circle the earliest big gap.
2. Ask the rep: "Where do your deals get stuck?"
3. Run the Quick prompt with both.
4. Keep one skill, one behavior, one practice activity.
5. Agree one count you will both check in 30 days, and book four ten-minute reviews.

## 16. The Deep Version

For a rep on a performance plan, a new-manager cohort, or a team-wide coaching rollout.

| Addition | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Five or more observed calls, including the hardest deal | Shows whether the skill holds under pressure |
| Two observers score the same call and compare | Makes sure the score measures the rep, not the observer |
| AI-scored calls checked against manager scores before use | Trustworthy scale-up; drop any rubric line where they often disagree |
| Team-level view: which skill is the most common earliest break | Turns individual plans into a team enablement priority |
| 60-day check that the skill held | Separates real change from a month of effort |
