# Review a Deal: Toolkit

**Use this file when:**
- You run a weekly deal review or forecast call and want every deal read the same way in ten minutes.
- A deal already slipped its close date once.
- Someone wants to discount to save a deal and you need to know whether price is the real problem.
- You are a rep preparing for your manager's review and want to see the deal the way they will.

The SKILL.md holds the logic. This file holds the instruments: the proven-versus-assumed sheet, the six deal facts, stage-evidence checks, the close-date test, the next-action test, the discount gate, and a worked example.

---

## 1. The sequence, with time boxes

| Step (from SKILL.md) | What you actually do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Paste the notes | CRM fields plus last three interactions, with buyer quotes where you have them | Deal pack | 2 min |
| 2. Read proven vs. assumed first | Fill the six-fact sheet (Section 3). This is the review | Proven / assumed split | 3 min |
| 3. Challenge the close date | Run the close-date test (Section 5) | Justified / not justified | 1 min |
| 4. Agree one next action | Run the next-action test (Section 6). Owner and date | One action | 2 min |
| 5. Update the CRM | Stage, close date, next step, contact roles changed to match the review | Updated record | 2 min |

**The quote rule:** "proven" means you can quote the buyer's words or point to something the buyer did. Your summary of their mood is not evidence.

## 2. Evidence levels

| Level | Definition | Example | Counts as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Did** | Buyer took an action | Introduced the VP; sent the security questionnaire; shared their budget template | Proven |
| **Said, on record** | Buyer said it in writing or on a recorded call | Email: "We need this live before Q1 planning" | Proven |
| **Said, rep-reported** | Rep remembers the buyer saying it, nothing recorded | "She told me budget is approved" | Assumed, until confirmed in writing |
| **Inferred** | Rep concluded it from signals | "They're engaged, they always reply fast" | Assumed |
| **Hoped** | No evidence | "This should close this quarter" | Assumed, and named as a hope |

## 3. The six deal facts: proven-versus-assumed sheet

Six facts most deals turn on. Adapt the names to your own qualification framework; keep the count small so reps actually fill them.

| Fact | Question | Proven looks like | Assumed looks like | Status | Evidence (quote or action, date) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | What problem, in their words, and what does it cost them? | Buyer quote describing the problem and its impact | "They need better reporting" | P / A | |
| **Metric** | What measurable result do they expect? | Buyer named a number or target | "They want efficiency" | P / A | |
| **Economic buyer** | Who can say yes to the money, and have we met them? | Named, met, or introduced by champion | Title guessed from org chart | P / A | |
| **Decision process** | What steps between now and signature? | Buyer listed the steps (legal, security, procurement) | "Standard process" | P / A | |
| **Decision criteria** | How will they choose? | Buyer stated criteria or shared an evaluation sheet | Rep assumes features matter most | P / A | |
| **Alternative** | What else could they do, including nothing? | Buyer named competitors or the status quo option | "We're the only one" | P / A | |

**Plus two relationship checks:**

| Check | Question | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Champion | Has someone inside taken an action that costs them something for this deal? | P / A |
| Multi-threaded | Has the champion introduced power (economic buyer or their boss)? | Yes / No |

**Reading the sheet (rule of thumb):**

| Pattern | Read |
|---|---|
| 5-6 proven, champion acted, multi-threaded | Healthy. Review the close date and move on |
| 3-4 proven | Normal for mid-stage. The next action targets the most important assumed fact |
| 0-2 proven at stage 3 or later | Stage is overstated. Drop a stage |
| Economic buyer assumed and close date inside 30 days | Top risk regardless of other facts |
| Notes too thin to fill three or more rows | Say so. List the three facts to go get (per the Quick prompt) |

## 4. Stage evidence check

The stage should match what the buyer has done, not how long the deal has been open. Replace with your own stage definitions; the principle is that each stage has a buyer-verified exit.

| Stage (generic) | Buyer-verified exit evidence | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Buyer confirmed the problem and agreed to a next meeting | Stay in stage 1 |
| 2. Qualified | Pain and metric proven; economic buyer named | Back to stage 1 |
| 3. Solution / evaluation | Decision criteria proven; buyer engaged in demo or trial | Back to stage 2 |
| 4. Proposal | Economic buyer met; decision process proven | Back to stage 3 |
| 5. Negotiation / commit | Pricing in discussion with buyer; paper in their process; date confirmed by buyer | Back to stage 4 |

**SKILL.md rule:** no buyer-verified next step means the deal drops a stage. Hope is not a stage.

## 5. The close-date test

| Question | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| What buyer action is the date tied to? | Named action (board meeting, budget cycle, go-live date, contract expiry) | "End of quarter," "rep's target" |
| Did the buyer state or confirm the date? | Yes, quoted | Rep chose it |
| Do the remaining process steps fit before the date at the buyer's usual pace? | Yes, walked backward step by step | Steps unknown, or they do not fit |
| Has it slipped before? | No, or slipped once with a buyer-stated reason | Slipped with no reason recorded |

**Verdict:** all four pass = justified. Fail on the first question = move out a full cycle (SKILL.md rule). Any other fail = not justified; the next action should fix it.

**Walking the date backward (example):** signature 9/30 needs procurement (2 weeks at this buyer), legal (2 weeks), security review (3 weeks), economic buyer approval (1 week). Working back from 9/30, the security review had to start by mid-August. If it has not been scheduled, the date is not real.

## 6. The next-action test

One action. It must pass all five.

| Test | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer does something | "Champion books the VP meeting" | "Rep sends follow-up email" |
| Named owner | A person on each side | "We" or "the team" |
| Date | A specific day | "Next week," "soon" |
| Moves or kills honestly | The outcome will either advance the deal or show it is not real | Keeps it alive without learning anything |
| Targets the top risk | Addresses the most important assumed fact | Addresses something already proven |

**Good next actions, by top risk:**

| Top risk | Next action |
|---|---|
| Single-threaded | Champion introduces economic buyer in a meeting by [date] |
| Decision process unknown | Buyer walks you through the last purchase of this size by [date] |
| Close date untied | Buyer confirms in writing the event the date depends on |
| Security or legal unscheduled | Buyer books the review with their team by [date] |
| Metric unproven | Buyer shares the baseline number for the business case |
| Alternative unknown | Ask directly what happens if they do nothing, in the next meeting |

## 7. Decision table

| If | Then | Source |
|---|---|---|
| No buyer-verified next step | Drop a stage | SKILL.md rule |
| Close date not tied to a buyer action | Move out a full cycle | SKILL.md rule |
| Champion has never introduced power | Single-threaded is the top risk until fixed | SKILL.md rule |
| Deal slipped once already | Ask the buyer for the reason; record it; re-run the close-date test | Judgment |
| Deal slipped twice | Remove from commit; next action is a direct "is this still a priority?" conversation | Judgment |
| Discount requested to save the deal | Run the discount gate (Section 8) first | Judgment |
| Next action cannot involve the buyer | The deal is stalled; say so | Judgment |
| Proven facts conflict with the rep's stage | Stage follows evidence, not the rep | Judgment |

### Red flags

| Red flag | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Every fact marked proven, no quotes | Happy ears |
| Every fact assumed late in the cycle | Rep is sandbagging, or the deal is not real |
| Notes describe mood ("great call," "really excited") | No buyer evidence was captured |
| Next step field reads "follow up" | No next step |
| Amount changed without a note | Scope or pricing moved and nobody recorded why |
| Champion is the only contact after several meetings | Single-threaded; the champion may lack access to power |

## 8. The discount gate

Use before any discount offered to save a deal.

| Question | If no |
|---|---|
| Has the buyer said price is the obstacle, in their words? | The problem is not price. Find the real obstacle first |
| Is the economic buyer engaged? | A discount to the champion does not reach the person who decides |
| Is the decision process proven? | The discount may be spent before the real approval step |
| Will the buyer give something in return (date, term, scope, reference)? | A discount with nothing in return trains the buyer to wait |

**Rule of thumb:** if any answer is no, fix that first. Discounting a deal with an assumed economic buyer lowers the price of a deal that may not exist.

## 9. The deal review output template

```
DEAL REVIEW: [Account]   Amount: [$]   Stage: [n]   Close: [date]
Reviewed by: [name]   Date: [date]

PROVEN (quote or action, date)
  - [fact]: "[buyer words]" / [buyer action], [date]
ASSUMED
  - [fact]: [what we believe and why]

TOP RISK: [one line]
CLOSE DATE: [Justified | Not justified | Move out one cycle]  Reason: [ ]
STAGE: [unchanged | dropped to n]  Reason: [ ]

NEXT ACTION: [buyer does what]  Owner (theirs/ours): [ / ]  Date: [ ]
  If it does not happen by the date: [close date moves / stage drops / deal reviewed for disqualification]

FACTS TO GO GET (if notes too thin): 1. [ ]  2. [ ]  3. [ ]
CRM UPDATED: [Y]  Fields changed: [ ]
```

## 10. Worked example

**Situation (illustrative):** Acme, a fictional 400-person B2B software company. Deal: $120k, stage 3, close date 9/30. Notes: champion is a director who likes the product. No meeting with the VP yet. Security review mentioned but not scheduled. Reviewed on 9/2.

**Six-fact sheet:**

| Fact | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | Proven | Director on recorded call 8/14: "Our team rebuilds the same report every Monday by hand." |
| Metric | Assumed | Rep believes they want to save time; no number from the buyer |
| Economic buyer | Assumed | VP named on org chart; never met |
| Decision process | Assumed | Security review "mentioned"; no other steps known |
| Decision criteria | Assumed | No criteria stated |
| Alternative | Assumed | Not discussed |
| Champion | Partly | Director likes the product; has not taken an action that costs anything |
| Multi-threaded | No | Only the director |

**Reading:** 1 of 6 proven at stage 3. Stage evidence check: stage 3 needs criteria proven; stage 2 needs pain, metric, and economic buyer named. Only pain is proven. The stage is overstated.

**Close-date test:** 9/30 is tied to nothing the buyer has said. The security review alone, if it runs three weeks at this buyer, would need to start by 9/9 and it is not scheduled. Fail on question 1 and question 3.

**Verdict:**
- Top risk: single-threaded with an unscheduled security review (SKILL.md rule: never introduced power).
- Close date: not justified. Moves out one cycle unless the next action happens.
- Stage: drop to 2 until the economic buyer is met.

**Next action:** champion books a meeting with the VP and a security scoping call by Friday 9/6. Owner: director (theirs), AE (ours). If it does not happen by Friday, the close date moves out one cycle.

Next-action test: buyer does something (yes), named owners (yes), date (yes), moves or kills honestly (yes: a champion who will not introduce the VP is telling you something), targets top risk (yes). Pass.

**CRM updated:** stage 3 to 2; close date flagged for move pending Friday; next step "Director to book VP + security scoping by 9/6"; contact roles: director = champion, VP = economic buyer (not met).

**Reasoning that mattered:** the notes sounded positive ("likes the product"), which is why the deal sat at stage 3. Splitting proven from assumed showed that only one fact was proven. The rep did not need a better pitch; they needed the director to spend a little political capital.

## 11. AI assist: who does what

| Task | AI | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Split notes into proven and assumed | Yes | Check each "proven" has a real quote or action |
| Map facts to the six-fact sheet | Yes | Add what you know but did not write down |
| Run close-date and stage checks | Yes | Supply the buyer's usual pace for each step |
| Pick the next action | Draft 2-3 options | Choose; agree it with the rep |
| See politics, relationships, history not in the notes | No | Yes |
| Update the CRM | Draft the field changes | Make them |

### Follow-up prompts after the Quick prompt

1. **Quote check:** "For every item you marked proven, quote the exact buyer words or name the buyer action from my notes. If you cannot, move it to assumed."
2. **Six facts:** "Map my notes to these six facts: pain, metric, economic buyer, decision process, decision criteria, alternative. Mark each proven or assumed with the evidence. List any I gave you nothing for."
3. **Close date:** "Walk the close date backward through every process step you can find in the notes. Where a step's duration is unknown, say so. Is the date achievable?"
4. **Next action:** "Give me three candidate next actions. For each: what the buyer does, owners on both sides, a date, and which assumed fact it tests. Then pick one and say why."
5. **Discount:** "The rep wants to discount [x]%. Using my notes, has the buyer said price is the obstacle? Is the economic buyer engaged? What should we ask for in return?"
6. **CRM:** "List the CRM field changes this review implies: stage, close date, next step, contact roles."

### Errors this job invites

| Error | How to spot it | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Rep summaries promoted to proven | "Proven: budget approved" with no quote | Prompt 1 |
| Mood read as evidence | "Strong engagement" as a proven fact | Ask: what did the buyer do? |
| Next action on the rep only | "Send recap," "follow up" | Next-action test, row 1 |
| Generic risk | "Competition" or "timing" with no deal specifics | Ask for the risk in terms of one assumed fact |
| Missing the thin-notes call | Confident review of a deal with three lines of notes | Ask: which facts could you not fill? |

## 12. Common mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Reviewing the rep's story instead of the evidence | Review is a narrative, not a sheet | Fill the six-fact sheet before any discussion |
| Multiple next actions | List of five things to do | One action. The rest can wait |
| CRM not updated after review | Same stage and date next week | Update during the review, not after |
| Discounting first | Price cut offered before the economic buyer is met | Run the discount gate |
| Treating every slip as normal | Date moved three times, no reasons recorded | Two slips leaves commit; ask the buyer directly |

## 13. The 5-minute version

1. Who is the economic buyer, and have we met them? (1 min)
2. What buyer action is the close date tied to? (1 min)
3. What did the buyer do or say, in writing or on record, since the last review? (2 min)
4. One next action the buyer must take, with a date. (1 min)

If 1 and 2 have no answer, the deal drops a stage and the date moves.

## 14. The deep version

For large deals, deals in commit, or a deal about to get a discount.

| Add | How |
|---|---|
| Call evidence | Search recordings for buyer statements on each of the six facts; attach quotes |
| Stakeholder map | Every person in the buying group: role, stance, last contact, who on our side owns them |
| Mutual plan | Every remaining step to signature with buyer-agreed dates, shared with the buyer |
| Slip history | Every close-date and stage change with the recorded reason |
| Pre-mortem | "This deal was lost. Why?" Get evidence against the top answer |
| Second reviewer | A manager not on the deal fills the six-fact sheet from the notes alone; compare |
