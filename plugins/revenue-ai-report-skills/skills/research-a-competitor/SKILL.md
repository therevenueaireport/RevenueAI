# Research a Competitor

> From the [Revenue AI Report skill library](https://www.therevenueaireport.com/skills). Level: Start Here · Task: Research · Setup: About 45 minutes per competitor · For: Marketing, Sales Leader, Revenue Operations, Executive and Founder

**Outcome:** A sourced profile of one competitor: what they sell, who they win with, how they price, and where they are weak.

## When to use
- A competitor starts showing up in deals and nobody can describe them accurately.
- You are about to write positioning, a battlecard, or a pricing change.
- Your current competitor notes are older than two quarters.

## Inputs you need
- The competitor's website, pricing page, and any public documentation
- Recent public material: funding news, job postings, release notes, review site pages
- Three deals you lost to them and what the buyer said

## Quick prompt
Paste into Claude, ChatGPT, or any assistant. Replace the [bracketed] parts.

```text
You are a competitive analyst. Using only the material I paste below, write a profile of [COMPETITOR]. Include: what they sell and to whom, their stated pricing model, the buyer they clearly design for, three strengths supported by the material, three weaknesses supported by the material, and what the material does not tell us. Cite the source line for every claim. Mark anything you are inferring as an inference. Do not invent prices, customer counts, or quotes.

Material:
[paste site copy, pricing page, reviews, loss notes]
```

## Process
1. Collect public material first. Site, pricing page, docs, release notes, job postings, review site pages.
2. Add private evidence you already own: loss notes, buyer quotes, recordings summaries.
3. Paste it all. Ask for the profile with a source line per claim.
4. Delete every claim that has no source. Do not soften it, cut it.
5. Write one sentence on what would change your view, then set a review date.

## Decision rules
- No source, no claim. Pricing especially: use the published figure or say it is unpublished.
- A weakness only counts if a buyer said it or the material shows it. Internal opinion is a hypothesis.
- If the competitor appears in fewer than three of your deals, keep the profile short and revisit later.

## Output
- A one-page profile: offer, buyer, pricing model, strengths, weaknesses, open questions.
- A source line for every claim.
- A review date and an owner.

## Quality checks
- Every number traces to a published page or an internal record.
- Inferences are labelled as inferences.
- The profile says what it does not know.

## Limitations
- Model training data about vendors goes stale fast. Verify anything a model produces from memory.
- Review sites skew to unhappy and incentivised reviewers. Treat them as signal, not measurement.
- Public pricing pages rarely match negotiated pricing.

## Worked example
**Input:** Pasted: competitor pricing page listing per seat tiers, twelve review site entries, notes from three lost deals where the buyer cited faster setup.

**Output:** Profile: per seat pricing published at two tiers, designed for teams under 200 people, strength is setup speed cited in all three losses, weakness is reporting depth named in seven reviews. Unknown: enterprise pricing, security posture. Review in 90 days.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Did someone check the pricing claims against the live page today?
- [ ] Is every weakness backed by a buyer statement or public material?
- [ ] Is the review date on a calendar with a name against it?
