# Research a Competitor: Toolkit

**Use this file when:**
- A competitor is showing up in deals and nobody can describe them accurately.
- You are about to write positioning, a battlecard, or a pricing change and need a sourced base to build on.
- Your competitor notes are older than two quarters, or nobody knows where a "fact" in them came from.
- An AI assistant produced a competitor summary from memory and you need to know what in it is true.

The SKILL.md holds the logic: public material first, then private evidence, a source line per claim, cut anything unsourced, set a review date. This file holds the source map, the evidence grades, the signal-reading table, the profile template, a loss-interview question bank, and a full worked example.

---

## 1. The sequence

| Step | What you do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check deal count to size the effort | Short or full profile decision | 5 min |
| 2 | Collect public material (Section 3) | Source list with dates | 15 min |
| 3 | Add private evidence you already own (Section 4) | Loss and win notes, buyer quotes | 10 min |
| 4 | Log each claim with its source and grade (Section 5) | Evidence log | 10 min |
| 5 | Generate the profile, then cut unsourced claims | Profile (Section 7) | 5 min |
| 6 | Write what would change your view, set owner and review date | Review line | 2 min |

**Sizing rule (SKILL.md decision rule 3):** if the competitor appears in fewer than three of your deals over the last two quarters, write a short profile (offer, buyer, pricing model, open questions) and revisit later. Three or more, write the full profile.

---

## 2. Before you start: what this research may and may not use

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Their public website, pricing page, docs, release notes, job postings | Posing as a buyer to get pricing or a demo |
| Public review site pages, public filings, press releases, public talks | Asking a new hire for a former employer's confidential material |
| Your own loss and win notes, buyer quotes, call recordings | Using a customer's shared copy of a competitor's confidential proposal |
| What buyers volunteer about the competitor on your calls | Scraping behind a login you agreed not to scrape |

When unsure, ask legal. A profile built on material you should not have can cost more than the deal it was meant to win.

## 3. Source map: public material

| Source | What it reveals | Reliability | Check how often |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage and product pages | Who they design for, the problem they lead with, the words they use | Their claim, not a fact | Each review |
| Pricing page | Pricing model, tiers, what is gated, published prices | Published list only; negotiated prices differ | Monthly for key competitors |
| Documentation and help center | Real features, limits, integrations, setup steps | High for capability; docs lag the product a little | Each review |
| Release notes or changelog | Where they are investing, release pace | High | Monthly |
| Job postings | Direction: segments, regions, product bets | Signal, not plan | Each review |
| Press releases and funding news | Funding, partnerships, leadership changes | Their claim | Each review |
| Review site pages | Recurring praise and complaints, buyer roles | Skews to unhappy and incentivized reviewers; signal, not measurement | Quarterly |
| Public talks, webinars, podcasts by their staff | Strategy in their own words | Their framing | When found |
| Integration marketplaces (your CRM's app listing, for example) | Install base hints, ratings, supported objects | Moderate | Quarterly |
| Public filings (if listed) | Revenue, customer counts, segments, risks | High | Each filing |

**Snapshot rule:** save a dated copy or screenshot of the pricing page and key product pages at the time you read them. Pages change, and the profile must show what the page said on the day you cited it.

## 4. Private evidence: what you already own

| Source | What to pull | Lookback |
|---|---|---|
| CRM opportunities with this competitor recorded | Count, stage reached, outcome, deal size, segment | Last two quarters minimum, four if volume is low |
| Closed-lost reasons and notes | Stated reason, buyer quotes | Same |
| Call recordings and transcripts | Every mention of the competitor by a buyer | Same |
| Win notes | Why you beat them, in the buyer's words | Same |
| Customers who switched from them | Why they left | Any time |
| Rep and SE notes | What the competitor did in the deal | Same, graded as internal opinion |

### Loss interview question bank

Use after a loss, with the buyer's permission. Ask open questions; do not argue.

| Purpose | Question |
|---|---|
| Decision driver | "What was the deciding factor in the end?" |
| Their strength | "What did they show you that we did not?" |
| Our gap | "Where did we fall short of what you needed?" |
| Price reality | "How did the commercial terms compare, in whatever detail you are comfortable sharing?" |
| Process | "Who had the most influence on the choice, and what mattered most to them?" |
| Risk accepted | "What concerns did you have about them, and how did they address those?" |
| Counterfactual | "What would we have had to do differently to win?" |

## 5. Evidence grades and the evidence log

Every claim in the profile gets one grade. The grade decides how the claim may be written.

| Grade | Source type | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| A | Their own published material (pricing page, docs, filings, release notes) | State it, with the source and date |
| B | A buyer's statement in your records (loss notes, call transcript, interview) | State it as "buyers said," with count |
| C | Third-party review site pages | State it as a recurring theme with a count of reviews, never as a measurement |
| D | Inference from A to C material (job postings, release patterns) | Label "Inference:" and name what it rests on |
| E | Internal opinion with no external source | Not a claim. Move to the hypotheses list (SKILL.md decision rule 2) |

### Evidence log template

```
| # | Claim | Source (page, record, or quote) | Date seen | Grade | Used in profile? |
|---|-------|---------------------------------|-----------|-------|------------------|
| S1 | [ ] | [URL or record ID] | [ ] | A-E | Yes / Cut / Hypothesis |
```

**Cut rule (SKILL.md process step 4):** if a claim has no source line, delete it. Do not soften it into "reportedly" or "some say." Grade E claims go to the hypotheses list, where they wait for evidence.

## 6. Reading signals: what public material usually means

These are inferences. Write them with the "Inference:" label and the source.

| Signal | Possible meaning | What would confirm it |
|---|---|---|
| Many enterprise AE or solutions engineer postings | Moving upmarket | Enterprise tier added, security docs published, larger logos in buyer mentions |
| Postings in a new region or language | International expansion | Local pricing, local support hours |
| Release notes concentrated on one area | Their current product bet | Same area featured on homepage and in demos buyers describe |
| Long gaps between releases in an area | Underinvestment there | Reviews complaining about that area |
| Pricing page drops published prices for "contact us" | Moving to negotiated, larger deals | Buyers report bigger minimums |
| New free tier or trial | Pushing product-led acquisition, likely smaller buyers | Reviews from smaller teams increase |
| Leadership change in sales or product | Strategy shift likely within a few quarters | New messaging on homepage |
| Funding round | More spend on sales and marketing ahead | Hiring surge, more events, discounting in deals |

## 7. Profile template

```
COMPETITOR PROFILE: [Competitor]          Owner: [Role]   Date: [ ]   Review by: [Date]
Deals in last two quarters: [Count] (won [ ], lost [ ], open [ ])   Profile type: Short / Full

1. WHAT THEY SELL AND TO WHOM
   Offer:                [ ]  (S#)
   Buyer they design for:[Role, company size, segment]  (S#)
   Problem they lead with: [Their words]  (S#)

2. PRICING MODEL
   Model:                [Per seat / usage / platform fee / tiers]  (S#, date)
   Published prices:     [Exact figures from the page, or "unpublished"]  (S#, date)
   What is gated:        [Features by tier]  (S#)
   Negotiated reality:   [Only from your deal records, with count]  (S#)

3. STRENGTHS (three, each sourced)
   1. [ ]  (S#, grade)
   2. [ ]
   3. [ ]

4. WEAKNESSES (three, each sourced from a buyer or public material)
   1. [ ]  (S#, grade)
   2. [ ]
   3. [ ]

5. INFERENCES (labelled)
   Inference: [ ]  Rests on: (S#)

6. WHAT WE DO NOT KNOW
   [Open questions, and how we could find out]

7. HYPOTHESES WAITING FOR EVIDENCE (internal opinion, not claims)
   [ ]

8. WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR VIEW
   [One sentence]
```

## 8. Decision table

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| Fewer than three deals with them | Short profile; revisit when count reaches three |
| Pricing is not published | Write "unpublished." Add negotiated figures only from your own deal records, with the count |
| Only reviews support a weakness | Grade C; write it as a theme with a count; look for a buyer statement before using it in a battlecard |
| Reps insist on a weakness nobody else can source | Hypotheses list; ask the next three losses about it |
| AI output contains a price, customer count, or quote you did not paste | Delete it, then check the live page |
| Profile contradicts the old notes | Trust the dated source; record what changed |
| Competitor ships a major release or price change | Update the affected lines now; do not wait for the review date |
| You lose three deals in a row for the same reason | Pull the review date forward and run loss interviews |

---

## 9. Red flags

| Red flag | What it usually means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Every weakness came from your own team | Wishful thinking | Move to hypotheses |
| A specific price with no page or record | Rumor or model memory | Cut it, or find the source |
| "They are cheaper" with no deal data | Possibly a list price compared with your discounted price | Compare like for like from deal records |
| Profile has no "what we do not know" section | False confidence | Add at least three open questions |
| Strengths are all things you also do | Nobody asked buyers | Use loss quotes |
| Review-site counts quoted as percentages | Treating signal as measurement | State counts and the skew |
| No review date or owner | The profile will go stale and still be used | Put both on a calendar |

## 10. Worked example: Acme vs Competitor C

*Illustrative. Acme is a fictional 400-person B2B software company selling a revenue forecasting platform. "Competitor C" stands in for a real rival. All figures below are invented for the example.*

**Sizing.** Competitor C appeared in 9 Acme deals over two quarters: 4 won, 5 lost. Full profile.

**Evidence log (abridged).**

| # | Claim | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Two published tiers: $60 and $95 per seat per month; enterprise is "contact sales" | Pricing page, snapshot dated this week | A |
| S2 | Leads with "forecast in a day" on homepage | Homepage, same date | A |
| S3 | 3 of the last 4 releases add AI forecast summaries; none add integrations | Release notes, last 6 months | A |
| S4 | 6 open roles: 4 enterprise AEs, 2 solutions engineers in a new region | Careers page | A |
| S5 | 4 of 5 lost buyers cited setup in days | Acme closed-lost notes and 2 call transcripts | B |
| S6 | 2 of 5 lost buyers cited lower price | Acme closed-lost notes | B |
| S7 | 3 of 4 won buyers cited reporting depth and multi-currency support | Acme win notes | B |
| S8 | 7 of 14 review pages mention shallow custom reporting | Review site, read this week | C |
| S9 | "Their AI is just a wrapper on a public model" | AE in pipeline review | E |
| S10 | "They have 2,000 customers" | Rep memory | No source |

**Cuts.** S10 has no source: deleted. The homepage was checked and states no customer count. S9 is internal opinion: moved to the hypotheses list.

**Profile.**

1. *Offer and buyer.* Forecasting software for sales teams; the homepage leads with fast setup (S2). Inference: designed for teams that want value in the first week with little admin help (rests on S2, S5).
2. *Pricing.* Per seat, two published tiers at $60 and $95 per seat per month, enterprise unpublished (S1, dated). Negotiated reality: 2 lost buyers said price was lower than Acme's; no figures shared (S6). Not enough to say they are cheaper at comparable scope.
3. *Strengths.* Setup speed, cited by 4 of 5 lost buyers (S5). Active investment in AI forecast summaries (S3). A simple two-tier price that is easy to approve (S1, inference on approval ease).
4. *Weaknesses.* Custom reporting depth: 3 of 4 Acme wins and 7 of 14 reviews (S7, S8). Multi-currency, cited in Acme wins (S7). Integrations: no new integrations in 6 months (S3); weak evidence of a weakness, flagged for a buyer test.
5. *Inferences.* Inference: moving upmarket and into a new region (S4). Confirm if an enterprise tier and security documentation appear.
6. *What we do not know.* Enterprise pricing. Security posture. Whether setup speed holds for teams over 200 sellers. Renewal and churn experience.
7. *Hypotheses.* "AI is a thin wrapper" (S9). Test: ask the next two buyers who saw both demos what the AI summaries did for them.
8. *What would change our view.* If an enterprise tier with multi-currency and custom reporting ships, our two strongest win reasons weaken; re-run this profile and the battlecard that day.

**Owner and review.** Product marketing manager. Review in 90 days, or on any pricing page change.

**Reasoning.** The strongest finding is not a weakness; it is the setup-speed strength, because it comes from the buyers who chose them. The weaknesses that will survive a rep's use on a live call are the ones buyers named (reporting depth, multi-currency). Integrations stay a soft weakness until a buyer confirms it.

## 11. AI assist

| Work | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Extract claims from pasted pages and notes into the evidence log | Yes, with source lines | Spot-check every grade A claim against the live page |
| Grade claims A-E | Draft | Confirm, especially B vs E |
| Spot signals in job postings and release notes | Yes, labelled as inference | Decide which inferences matter |
| Write the profile from the log | Yes | Cut anything without a source line |
| Recall facts about the competitor from memory | No. Model memory about vendors goes stale | Paste current material instead |
| Run loss interviews | No | Yours |

**Follow-up prompts to use after the Quick prompt:**

1. "Turn the profile into an evidence log: one row per claim, with the exact source line from my paste and a grade: A published by them, B buyer statement in our records, C review site, D inference, E internal opinion."
2. "List every price, customer count, percentage, and quote in your profile. For each, quote the line in my paste it came from. Delete any you cannot quote."
3. "From the job postings and release notes I pasted, list up to five inferences about their direction. Label each 'Inference:' and name the evidence and what would confirm it."
4. "What are the five most important things this material does not tell us, and how could we find each out without misrepresenting ourselves?"
5. "Write the one sentence that would change our view, and the trigger that should prompt an update before the review date."

**Check the AI's output for these errors:**

| Error this job invites | How to catch it |
|---|---|
| Invented prices, customer counts, or funding figures | Prompt 2; every number has a quoted source line |
| Facts from model memory, now out of date | Anything not in your paste is cut or verified live |
| Review themes reported as percentages of all users | Counts only, with the skew noted |
| Internal opinion presented as a weakness | Check each weakness has a B or C source |
| Inferences written as facts | Search for "Inference:" labels on every forward-looking claim |
| Strengths that are generic ("easy to use") | Each strength has a buyer quote or a page behind it |

## 12. Common mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starting from opinion, then finding support | Weaknesses all match what the team already believed | Collect material first, write claims second |
| Comparing their list price with your negotiated price | "They are 30 percent cheaper" with no deal data | Compare like for like |
| Profile as a feature table | Long grid, no buyer, no pricing model | Use the template; buyer and pricing first |
| Treating reviews as a survey | Percentages from 14 reviews | Counts and themes only |
| No dates on sources | Nobody knows if the price is current | Date every source and snapshot the pricing page |
| One-off profile, never updated | Last edited three quarters ago | Owner, review date, and update triggers |
| Hiding what you do not know | No open-questions section | At least three open questions |

## 13. The 5-minute version

1. Open their pricing page. Write the model and any published prices, with today's date.
2. Open their homepage. Write who they design for and the problem they lead with.
3. Read your last three loss notes against them. Write the reason each buyer gave.
4. Write one strength and one weakness, each with a source. Nothing without a source.
5. Write what you do not know and a review date.

## The deep version

For a top competitor, a positioning rewrite, or a pricing change:
- Pull every deal with them from the last four quarters and compute win rate, deal size, and cycle length against them by segment.
- Run five loss interviews and three win interviews with the question bank.
- Read every release note for the last 12 months and chart where they invest.
- Snapshot their pricing page monthly and keep the history.
- Set update triggers (price change, major release, leadership change, three losses for one reason) in addition to the review date.
- Hand the profile to whoever builds the battlecard and the messaging breakdown, so both inherit the same sources.
