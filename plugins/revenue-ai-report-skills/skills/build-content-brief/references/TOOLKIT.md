# Content Brief Toolkit: Question Test, Evidence Gate, Citation Bar, and Draft Review

**Use this file when:**
- You are commissioning an article, guide, or report and the writer needs more than a topic and a keyword.
- Content volume is up and pipeline from content is flat, and you need a gate that stops filler before it is written.
- An AI assistant is drafting content and you need a brief strong enough that the draft cannot be generic.
- A draft came back and you need to check it against the brief before anyone edits prose.

The SKILL.md holds the logic. This file holds the instruments. Run them in the order below.

---

## 1. The Brief Sequence

| Step | What you do | Output | Time (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pick a real buyer question and test it | Question that passes Section 2 | 5 min |
| 2 | Inventory the evidence you can bring; run the gate | Go or stop (Section 3) | 5 min |
| 3 | Check what the first page of answers already says | Cut list and gap (Section 4) | 5-10 min |
| 4 | Write the answer in under 100 words; decide length | Answer and format (Section 5) | 5 min |
| 5 | Design sections, each with its evidence | Section plan (Section 6) | 5 min |
| 6 | Set the citation bar and the kill criteria | Bar and kill lines (Sections 7-8) | 5 min |
| 7 | Hand over the brief | Completed template (Section 9) | n/a |
| 8 | Review the draft against the brief before prose edits | Scorecard (Section 10) | 10 min |

---

## 2. Step 1: The Buyer Question

### Where real questions come from

| Source category | What to pull | How far back |
|---|---|---|
| Call recorder | Questions buyers ask in discovery and evaluation calls, verbatim | 90 days |
| Sales and CS email | Questions forwarded by reps and CSMs ("a prospect asked me...") | 90 days |
| Support tickets and community posts | How-to and "should I" questions | 90 days |
| RFPs and security questionnaires | Questions buyers put in writing | 12 months |
| Site search and search console queries | Question-form queries that reach your site | 6 months |
| Win and loss interviews | "What we were trying to figure out" | 2-4 quarters |

### The question test (all four must pass)

| Test | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| **Asked** | A buyer asked it, in words close to these, with a source | A keyword or a topic ("AI SDR best practices") |
| **Decision-linked** | The answer changes something the buyer will decide or do | Curiosity only |
| **Non-obvious** | Reasonable people would answer it differently | Everyone gives the same answer |
| **Right to answer** | You have evidence or experience others lack (Section 3) | You would be summarizing others |

**Rewrite pattern:** keyword "sales forecasting best practices" becomes buyer question "How many forecast categories should a 40-rep sales team use?"

---

## 3. Step 2: The Evidence Gate

No original evidence, data, or expert means no piece (SKILL.md rule).

### Evidence types and strength

| Type | Strong | Medium | Weak (does not pass the gate alone) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proprietary data | Your own dataset, method stated, sample size known | Internal data, small sample, method informal | A vendor's or third party's figure you cannot check |
| Customer outcomes | Two or more customer results, approved for use | One result, or results not yet approved | A logo or a quote with no outcome |
| Expert | Named internal or customer practitioner who has done the work and will be quoted | Expert available for background only | "Experts say" |
| Post-mortems or field experience | Documented cases of what failed and why | One anecdote | General observation |
| Original method | A framework or decision rule you use and can show working | A method borrowed and adapted, with credit | A listicle of tips |

**Gate rule (rule of thumb):** at least one Strong item, or two Medium items of different types. Otherwise stop, and record what evidence would make the piece possible.

### Evidence inventory sheet

| Evidence | Type | Strength | Owner / access | Approved to publish? | Which section uses it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 4. Step 3: The First-Page Check

Cut anything a competitor's first page already says (SKILL.md process).

| What to capture | How |
|---|---|
| Top 5-10 search results for the question | Read headings and the first 200 words of each |
| The AI answer engines' summary for the question | Ask two assistants the exact question; record their answer |
| Competitor and vendor posts on the question | Note their main claim and their evidence |

| Output | Use |
|---|---|
| **Cut list** | Points every source already makes. Mention once at most, or not at all |
| **Contested points** | Where sources disagree. Your evidence can settle one of these |
| **Gap** | What no source answers with evidence. This is the piece |

**Rule of thumb:** if the gap is empty and your evidence only confirms the consensus, the piece is filler. Stop or find a different question.

---

## 5. Step 4: The Answer and the Length

### The 100-word answer

Write the direct answer before the brief goes out. The writer starts from it.

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| The answer, as a decision | "Use four categories, not six." |
| The condition | "For teams under 50 reps with one forecast call a week." |
| The evidence in one clause | "Across our customers, teams with four categories had lower forecast error than teams with six." |
| The exception | "Add a fifth only if you sell multi-year deals with separate approval." |

### Length decision

| Answer shape | Format |
|---|---|
| Fits in one paragraph with its evidence | One paragraph. Post it, answer the question, stop (SKILL.md rule) |
| One answer, several conditions | Short piece: answer, conditions table, how to apply |
| Answer depends on a method the reader must run | Guide: answer, method steps, worked example, template |
| Answer rests on a dataset | Report: answer, method, findings, limits |

---

## 6. Step 5: Section Design

Each section carries one claim and names its evidence.

| Field | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Section claim | One sentence the reader should believe after reading it |
| Evidence | Item from the inventory sheet, by name |
| What the reader can do | The action or decision this section enables |
| What not to include | Points from the cut list that tempt the writer here |

**Three sections is the default (Quick prompt).** Add a fourth only for a worked example or a template.

---

## 7. Step 6a: The Citation Bar

What would make someone cite or forward this piece (SKILL.md process). The brief names at least two.

| Citation hook | Description |
|---|---|
| Original number | A figure from your data that others will quote with credit |
| Decision rule | "If X, do Y" the reader can apply tomorrow |
| Threshold | A specific line ("kill it if... by day 60") |
| Template or checklist | Something the reader copies |
| Contrarian finding with proof | Disagrees with the first page, and shows why |
| Worked example | The method applied end to end |

---

## 8. Step 6b: Kill Criteria for the Piece

Written before writing starts (SKILL.md quality check). If any is true at draft review, the piece does not ship as is.

| Kill criterion | How you check it |
|---|---|
| The answer is not in the first 100 words | Count |
| A section has no named evidence | Map sections to the inventory |
| It says what the first page already says | Compare with the cut list |
| It reads like a vendor blog | Product is mentioned before the answer is complete |
| A factual claim has no source | Run the claim list (Section 10) |
| It could be published by any competitor with their logo swapped | Swap test with a colleague |

## 9. The Brief Template (ready to fill)

```
CONTENT BRIEF: [working title]          Owner: [name]   Writer: [name]   Due: [date]

1. BUYER QUESTION
   "[verbatim or near-verbatim]"   Source: [call / ticket / RFP, date]
   Decision it informs: [what the buyer will do differently]

2. AUDIENCE AND CHANNEL
   Who: [role, company size, situation]   Where they read it: [site, email, social, sales follow-up]
   What they already know: [so the writer can skip it]

3. THE ANSWER (under 100 words)
   [Decision + condition + evidence clause + exception]

4. FORMAT AND LENGTH
   [Paragraph / short piece / guide / report] because [answer shape]

5. SECTIONS
   | # | Claim | Evidence (from inventory) | Reader can | Do not include |

6. EVIDENCE INVENTORY
   | Evidence | Type | Strength | Owner | Approved? |

7. CUT LIST (first page already says this)
   - [point]

8. CITATION BAR (at least two)
   - [hook]

9. KILL CRITERIA
   - [criterion]

10. CLAIM LIST (writer fills during drafting)
   | Claim | Source | Checked by |
```

## 10. Step 8: Draft Review Scorecard

Run this before editing prose. If the draft fails the brief, fix the draft, not the brief (SKILL.md rule).

| Check | Pass | Fail action |
|---|---|---|
| Answer in first 100 words, matching the brief's answer | Yes | Move it up; cut the preamble |
| Each section makes its stated claim | Yes | Rewrite the section to the claim |
| Each section uses its named evidence | Yes | Add the evidence or cut the section |
| Nothing from the cut list beyond one mention | Yes | Delete |
| At least two citation hooks present | Yes | Add from the brief |
| Every factual claim on the claim list has a source | Yes | Source it or remove it |
| No kill criterion is true | Yes | Do not ship. Return to writer with the criterion named |
| Length matches the format decision | Yes | Cut padding |

## 11. Worked Example: Acme

*Illustrative only. Acme is a fictional 400-person B2B software company selling sales forecasting software to mid-market sales teams. All data and names are invented.*

### Question selection

| Candidate | Asked | Decision-linked | Non-obvious | Right to answer | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Sales forecasting best practices" | No (keyword) | No | No | No | Reject |
| "Should we let reps self-report their forecast category?" | Yes, 4 discovery calls | Yes | Yes | Medium (one expert, no data) | Hold until data exists |
| "How many forecast categories should a 40-rep team use?" | Yes, 6 discovery calls and 2 RFPs | Yes, changes CRM setup | Yes, first page splits between 3 and 7 | Yes (see evidence) | **Go** |

### Evidence inventory

| Evidence | Type | Strength | Owner | Approved? | Section |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Category count vs forecast error across 60 customer instances, anonymized | Proprietary data | Strong | Head of data | Yes, aggregated only | 1 |
| Two customers who cut from six categories to four, with before-and-after error | Customer outcomes | Strong | Customer marketing | One approved, one pending | 2 |
| Head of RevOps who designed the default setup | Expert | Strong | Head of RevOps | Yes | 3 |

**Gate:** passes (three Strong items).

### First-page check

- **Cut list:** "align on definitions," "review the forecast weekly," "use your CRM's built-in stages." Every top result says these.
- **Contested:** the right number of categories (sources range from three to seven, none with data).
- **Gap:** nobody shows category count against forecast error. That is the piece.

### The answer (65 words)

> Use four forecast categories: commit, best case, pipeline, and omitted. In our data from 60 customer teams, teams using four categories had lower forecast error than teams using six or more, and the gap was widest for teams under 50 reps. Extra categories mostly split "best case" into shades reps cannot tell apart. Add a fifth only if multi-year deals need a separate approval path.

### Format

Short piece with one chart and a setup checklist. **Reasoning:** one answer with a condition and an exception; a method guide would be padding.

### Sections

| # | Claim | Evidence | Reader can | Do not include |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fewer categories, lower error, up to four | 60-customer data, chart of error by category count | Decide their count | Definitions of each stage (cut list) |
| 2 | The gain comes from removing ambiguous middle categories | Two customer before-and-after results | Spot which of their categories to merge | Generic "align your team" advice |
| 3 | Here is how to set four categories up in a week | Head of RevOps walkthrough | Copy the setup checklist | Product pitch |

### Citation bar

Original number (error by category count from 60 teams); threshold (four, with the multi-year exception); checklist (one-week setup).

### Kill criteria

The chart is cut for any reason; the second customer result is not approved and the piece leans on one story; the setup section names the product before step four.

### Draft review outcome

The first draft opened with 180 words on why forecasting matters. **Fail:** answer not in the first 100 words. The draft also cited an industry forecast-accuracy figure the writer found online with no source. **Fail:** claim list. Both fixed in the draft; the brief stayed unchanged.

## 12. Decision Table and Red Flags

| If you see | It means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| The brief starts from a keyword | No buyer behind it | Find the question a buyer asked that the keyword stands for |
| Evidence inventory is empty or all Weak | Filler in waiting | Stop (SKILL.md rule). Log what evidence would make it possible |
| The answer will not fit in 100 words | You do not know the answer yet | Narrow the question |
| Every section uses the same evidence | One finding stretched | Shorter format |
| The first-page check finds no gap | Nothing new to say | Different question, or a one-paragraph answer that links to your evidence |
| Requests for "a 2,000-word piece on X" | Length set before the answer | Decide length from answer shape (Section 5) |
| Content volume up, content-sourced pipeline flat | Producing filler at scale | Apply the evidence gate to the whole calendar |

## 13. AI Assist: Splitting the Work

| Task | AI assistant | Human |
|---|---|---|
| Turn keywords into candidate buyer questions | Yes | Confirm each was actually asked, with a source |
| Summarize first-page results into cut list, contested, gap | Yes, from pasted text | Check the gap is real |
| Draft the 100-word answer | Drafts from your evidence | Owns the answer; the model cannot supply evidence (SKILL.md limitation) |
| Design sections | Yes | Map each to a real inventory item |
| Write the claim list from a draft | Yes | Check every source |
| Judge the citation bar | Suggests | Decides |

### Follow-up prompts after the Quick prompt

1. "Test this question: was it asked by a buyer, does the answer change a decision, would reasonable people answer it differently, and do we have evidence others lack? Answer each with yes or no and why."
2. "Here is what the top results say: [paste]. Give me three lists: points every source makes (cut), points where sources disagree (contested), and what no source answers with evidence (gap)."
3. "Rewrite the answer in under 100 words as: the decision, the condition, the evidence in one clause, the exception. Use only the evidence I gave you."
4. "For each section, name the evidence item from my list that supports it. If a section has none, remove it."
5. "From this draft, list every factual claim as a table: claim, source given, or 'no source.' Do not judge the claims, just list them."
6. "Review this draft against the brief. For each kill criterion, say true or false with the line that proves it."

### Errors this job invites, and how to check

| AI error | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Invents evidence to fill a section | Statistics or quotes you did not supply | Follow-up prompt 4; delete anything without an inventory match |
| Writes a generic answer | Answer would fit any company | Require the evidence clause from your data |
| Recommends long form by default | 2,000-word outline for a one-paragraph answer | Apply the length decision (Section 5) |
| Calls the consensus a gap | Gap list repeats the cut list | Ask for the evidence that no source provides |
| Cites unnamed "studies show" | Claim list has vague sources | Remove or source |

## 14. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Brief is a topic and a word count | No answer field filled | Write the answer first |
| Evidence promised, not secured | "Customer story TBD" | Approved evidence before writing starts |
| Editing prose before checking the brief | Polished draft that misses the question | Scorecard first (Section 10) |
| Changing the brief to fit the draft | Brief version history after drafting | Fix the draft, not the brief |
| Counting pieces shipped as success | Volume reported, pipeline not | Track citations, forwards, and content-sourced pipeline |

---

## 15. The 5-Minute Version

1. Write the buyer question and who asked it.
2. Write the one piece of evidence only you have. If none, stop.
3. Write the answer in three sentences.
4. Write one thing the piece must not say (what everyone says).
5. Write one reason it should not ship. Check it at review.

## 16. The Deep Version

For flagship reports, cornerstone guides, or content meant to be cited by answer engines and analysts.

| Addition | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Question research across 30+ calls and tickets, ranked by frequency | Picks the question buyers ask most |
| Dataset with method note, sample size, and limits | Citable original number |
| Two customer results approved before drafting | No pending-approval gaps |
| Expert interview recorded and transcribed for quotes | Real voice, not paraphrase |
| Pre-publication review by one buyer in the audience | Tests whether it answers their question |
| Post-publication tracking: citations, forwards, content-sourced pipeline at 30 and 90 days | Tells you whether the bar was met |
