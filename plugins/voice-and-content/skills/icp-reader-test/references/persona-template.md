# Reader Persona Construction Template

**Use this file when:**
- You have no reader persona and need one before running the ICP Reader Test.
- Your persona is older than your latest subscriber export, survey or closed-won analysis.
- The persona keeps approving content that real readers ignore (it is modeling someone who is not on your list).

The persona is one specific person, named by role. It is built from your data first and your reasoning second, and it says which is which.

---

## 1. Gather the evidence first

Build from the strongest sources you have. Work down the list; stop when you have enough to fill Sections 2 to 8 with at least half the traits tagged [Measured] or [Reported].

| Source | What it tells you | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Subscriber or follower export (title, company, size, industry) | Who is actually on the list, in what share | Titles are self-reported and messy; normalize before counting |
| Engagement by segment (opens, re-opens, replies, filtered votes) | Which segment reads hardest, not just which is biggest | Raw clicks are polluted by email security scanners; opens by privacy features. Compare segments to each other, not to an absolute |
| Paid conversions, renewals, upgrades | Who actually pays | Small numbers early; note the sample size |
| CRM closed-won and closed-lost | Who buys your product, who walks, and why | Win reasons recorded by reps are often guesses |
| Reply threads and comments | Their words, their objections, their jokes | Loud repliers are not the median reader |
| Surveys | Stated priorities and budgets | People overstate willingness to pay |
| Interviews (5 to 10 is enough to start) | Pressures, fears, how they buy, who they forward to | Interview people who match the segment, not friendly fans |
| Sales and success call recordings | Real buying process and objections | Ask permission and strip identifying details |

**The segment-selection rule:** the primary persona is the segment where **engagement and buying power intersect**, not simply the largest segment. A segment that is a small share of the list but reads far harder than average and holds budget is usually a better primary than the large, average-engagement middle. The large middle becomes the broad-reader secondary persona.

## 4. Pressures and fears

```
Measured on:            [The 2-4 metrics their boss judges them by]
This year's mandate:    [e.g. "grow without new headcount," "prove the AI spend"]
Who is asking:          [Board, CEO, investors, customers, their own team]
How often they're asked:[e.g. every quarterly board meeting]
Biggest fear #1:        [e.g. being the leader who missed the shift]
Biggest fear #2:        [e.g. being the leader who wasted budget on a tool that got reversed]
What a win looks like:  [What they want to be able to say in their next update]
```

Fears are the strongest predictor of what gets opened. Write them in the persona's words if you have interview or reply data.

## 5. How they read

```
Device and moment:      [e.g. phone, between meetings; desktop, Sunday triage]
Time available:         [Realistic minutes per piece]
Inbox volume:           [e.g. pitched by vendors every week; dozens of newsletters]
Opens because of:       [Subject line patterns that work for them]
Skims:                  [What they read in full vs skim: headers, first lines, tables, bolded numbers]
Skips entirely:         [Formats or topics they never read]
Re-opens when:          [Signals the piece is reference material: a table, a template, a checklist]
Clicks:                 [Often rarely; note if measured]
```

**What they skim for.** Write the three things this persona's eye looks for during the skim. For most leaders it is a number with a source, a named decision, and something they can forward. For most operators it is a template, a step list, and a tool or configuration.

## 6. Forward and unsubscribe triggers

These two lists do more work in the test than any other section.

**Forwards when** (and to whom):

| Trigger | Forwards to | With what note |
|---|---|---|
| [e.g. a one-page test or scorecard] | [e.g. their ops lead] | ["can we do this?"] |
| [e.g. a benchmark with a source] | [e.g. their CEO] | ["this is where we stand"] |
| [e.g. a kill criterion for a pilot] | [e.g. their peer or team] | ["use this before the renewal"] |

**Unsubscribes, unfollows or ignores when:**

- [e.g. hype, trend lists, "the future of" pieces with no action]
- [e.g. material aimed at the people who report to them]
- [e.g. another tool to log into, another stream to manage]
- [e.g. unsourced or recycled statistics]
- [e.g. a pitch dressed as content, or undisclosed sponsor copy]
- [e.g. asks for their time with no deliverable attached]

## 8. How they buy

```
Signs alone up to:      [Amount they can approve without their boss or finance]
Expenses without procurement: [Professional development or tool expense range]
Needs a business case above: [Threshold]

Price bands:
  Self-serve band:      [e.g. under $X a year]
  Expense band:         [e.g. $X to $Y]
  Program band:         [e.g. $Y and up]

Alternatives they already have (and what each costs):
  - [A general AI assistant subscription]       [cost]
  - [Free reports or benchmarks in the category] [free]
  - [Peer community or membership]               [cost]
  - [Advisory or research subscription]          [cost]
  - [An internal analyst or ops person's time]   [cost]
  - [Doing nothing]                               [the cost of the status quo]

Signs vs uses:          [Who signs; who would actually use it]
Proof they'd need at renewal: [The one number they'd point to]
```

Fill price bands from real data when you can (survey, closed-won deal sizes, what peers report expensing). Mark them [Assumed] until then, and treat any verdict that depends on them as provisional.

---

## 9. Secondary personas

Build a short card (Sections 2, 4, 6 and 8 only) for each:

- **The forwardee:** who the primary forwards to, what they need that the primary does not, what they buy.
- **The broad reader:** the large, average-engagement segment. Why they read, what they want (often career signal and proof they are not behind), and their role in your business (reach, sponsors, job-board traffic) even if they rarely buy.

---

## 10. Validate before you trust it

1. **Backtest.** Pick your five best and five worst past pieces by a clean metric (replies, filtered votes, paid conversions). Run each through the persona's 10-second read without looking at the results. If the persona cannot sort them, find the traits that would have predicted the difference and revise.
2. **Read it to a real one.** Show the persona card to two or three people who match it. Ask "what's wrong or missing?" Their corrections become [Reported] traits.
3. **Count the tags.** If more than half the traits are [Inferred] or [Assumed], say so at the top of every test output until you gather more data.
4. **Date it.** Record the date of every data source. Refresh when new data arrives, or at least every two quarters.

---

## Common persona mistakes

| Mistake | Symptom in the test | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Built on the largest segment, not the most engaged buyer | Approves broad, safe content that nobody forwards | Re-select using the segment-selection rule |
| A character, not a role | Feedback is about personality, not decisions | Name by role; anchor every trait to a decision or behavior |
| All aspiration, no constraint | Approves every premium offer | Fill Section 8 with real limits and real alternatives |
| No unsubscribe triggers | Never deletes anything | Mine unsubscribe reasons, unfollows and cold replies |
| No forwardee | Approves operator content for a leader | Build the forwardee card and route content to the right one |
| Never refreshed | Verdicts drift away from real results | Date the data; backtest each quarter |
