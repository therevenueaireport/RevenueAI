# Two Illustrative Reader Personas

**Both personas below are fictional.** They show the depth a working persona needs and how evidence tags are used. The numbers, bands and shares are invented for illustration, so evidence tags are left off; in your own persona, replace them with your data and tag each trait [Measured], [Reported], [Inferred] or [Assumed].

Each persona ends with a short worked test so you can see how the persona changes the verdict on the same kind of asset.

---

## Persona A: The Head of RevOps

**One line:** Runs revenue operations at a 200 to 800 person B2B software company, reports to the revenue leader, and has just been handed "make the AI tools actually work" on top of the day job.

### Identity

```
Title variants:       Director of RevOps, Head of Revenue Operations, Sr. Manager Sales Ops, GTM Systems Lead
Company size band:    200-800 employees
Industry mix:         B2B software, IT services, fintech
Reports to:           The revenue leader, who reports to the CEO
Owns:                 CRM, routing, territories, forecasting process, the GTM tool stack, reporting
Team size:            2-6 analysts and admins; serves 40-120 sellers and managers
Tenure pattern:       Often came up through sales ops or finance; deep in the systems
Share of audience:    A mid-sized slice of the list that reads harder than average (illustrative)
```

### Decisions owned

| Decision | How often | Who else is involved | Cost of a wrong call |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recommend which tools to buy, renew or cut | Monthly | Revenue leader signs | Owns the blame when adoption fails |
| Configure and roll out AI features in existing tools | Weekly | Sales managers, IT | Data quality damage, rep revolt |
| Define the forecast process and its data | Quarterly | Revenue leader, finance | Missed forecast traced back to the system |
| Build the dashboards leadership reads | Ongoing | Revenue leader | Leadership stops trusting the numbers |

### Pressures and fears

```
Measured on:            Forecast accuracy, data completeness, tool adoption, time-to-report
This year's mandate:    Get value from the AI features already paid for before buying more
Who is asking:          The revenue leader, weekly; the CEO, through the revenue leader
Biggest fear #1:        Rolling out an AI feature that writes bad data into the CRM
Biggest fear #2:        Being seen as the bottleneck while every vendor promises "no ops needed"
What a win looks like:  "We turned on X, adoption is at Y, and here's the hours it returned"
```

### How they read

```
Device and moment:      Desktop, mid-morning, with the CRM open in another tab
Time available:         Will spend 15-20 minutes on something they can implement
Opens because of:       A specific tool, configuration, field, or failure mode in the subject line
Skims for:              Step lists, screenshots, templates, field definitions
Re-opens when:          It is reference material they'll use during a build
Clicks:                 More than leaders do: on templates and downloads
```

### Forward and unsubscribe triggers

| Forwards when | To | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A rollout checklist or data-readiness test | Their analyst | "Let's run this before we turn it on" |
| A one-page summary of what a feature does to the data | Their revenue leader | "This is why I want two weeks" |
| A template for a field, routing rule or prompt | Peers in an ops community | "Steal this" |

Unsubscribes or ignores when:
- Strategy pieces with no build steps ("AI will reshape RevOps")
- Content that assumes they have a data team
- Tool roundups with no configuration detail or failure modes
- Anything that treats ops as the blocker

### Values and rejections

Values: exact steps; failure modes named; the data impact of a change; templates; honest effort estimates; peers' real configurations.
Distrusts: "no-code, set up in minutes" claims; vendor benchmarks without method.
Vocabulary they use: fields, objects, routing, hygiene, sync, source of truth.
Vocabulary that repels: "revolutionize," "unlock," "AI-powered everything."

### How they buy

```
Signs alone up to:       Small: self-serve tools and templates on a card
Expense band:            Courses, template packs and community memberships, without procurement
Needs a business case:   Any platform or anything that touches the CRM
Alternatives:            A general AI assistant subscription; free templates in ops communities;
                         the vendor's own documentation; their own time
Signs vs uses:           Uses everything; signs only the small things. Recommends the big ones upward
Proof at renewal:        Hours returned per week, or a data-quality metric that moved
```

### Secondary personas

- **Forwardee:** Their analyst or admin. Wants the exact click path.
- **Broad reader:** Sales and marketing managers. Want to know what is coming to their tools; rarely buy.

### Worked test: subject line

Asset: *"5 AI trends every revenue team should watch this year"*

- **10-second read:** Deletes. No tool, no field, no failure mode. Reads as a trend list aimed at someone else.
- **Scores:** Decision relevance 1, Evidence 1, Actionability 1, Upward value 2, Trust 3, Time cost 2. Blockers: three 1s.
- **Top fix:** *"Before you turn on AI call summaries: the 4 CRM fields that break first"*. Names a live rollout, promises a failure mode, implies a checklist.
- **Real-reader test:** Send both subject lines to a small slice of the list; compare opens and replies, not clicks.

## Persona B: The Founder-Seller

**One line:** Co-founder and CEO of a 15 to 60 person B2B company, still closes most of the large deals personally, and is trying to hand sales to a first team without the revenue dropping.

### Identity

```
Title variants:       CEO, Co-founder, Founder and CEO, Managing Partner (services firms)
Company size band:    15-60 employees
Industry mix:         Vertical software, B2B services, agencies, data products
Reports to:           The board or investors; at bootstrapped firms, the bank balance
Owns:                 Everything, including the biggest deals; one or two early sellers
Team size:            0-4 sellers, maybe one marketer
Tenure pattern:       Technical or domain founder; learned sales by doing it
Share of audience:    A small slice of the list; very high reply rate (illustrative)
```

### Decisions owned

| Decision | How often | Who else is involved | Cost of a wrong call |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hire the first sales leader or the next seller | Once or twice a year | Co-founder, investors | A year lost and a burned territory |
| Set pricing and packaging | Quarterly | Co-founder | Margin, positioning, churn |
| Choose which deals to work personally | Weekly | Nobody | The quarter |
| Decide what to automate vs hire | Quarterly | Co-founder, investors | Cash runway |
| Report growth to investors | Monthly or quarterly | Board | The next round |

### Pressures and fears

```
Measured on:            Revenue growth, runway, the next round or the next profitable month
This year's mandate:    Get sales out of the founder's head and into a repeatable process
Who is asking:          Investors every update; the team every week
Biggest fear #1:        Hiring a sales leader who fails and costs a year
Biggest fear #2:        Spending on tools and process before the motion is proven
What a win looks like:  "Two reps closed deals this quarter without me on the call"
```

### How they read

```
Device and moment:      Phone, late evening or early morning, between everything else
Time available:         3-5 minutes; will save something longer if it promises a decision
Opens because of:       Their exact stage named ("first sales hire," "founder-led sales")
Skims for:              A decision rule, a mistake to avoid, a number from someone at their stage
Re-opens when:          It is a hiring scorecard, a pricing test or an investor-update line
Clicks:                 Rarely; replies often, with a direct question
```

### Forward and unsubscribe triggers

| Forwards when | To | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A first-sales-hire scorecard | Co-founder, an investor | "Use this for the interviews" |
| A pricing test they can run in a week | Co-founder | "Should we try this?" |
| A sharp line on what to automate before hiring | Their first seller | "Read this before Monday" |

Unsubscribes or ignores when:
- Enterprise playbooks that assume a RevOps team, enablement and a 12-tool stack
- Benchmarks from companies 50 times their size
- Anything that sells a platform before the motion exists
- Long pieces with the point at the end

### Values and rejections

Values: stage-specific advice; what founders at their size actually did; decision rules over frameworks; cheap experiments; candor about failure.
Distrusts: big-company advice, consultants, "you need a process for that" without a lightweight version.
Vocabulary they use: runway, first hire, founder-led, the motion, close rate, cash.
Vocabulary that repels: "enterprise-grade," "alignment," "operationalize."

### How they buy

```
Signs alone up to:       Anything, but cash-constrained; every dollar competes with a hire
Self-serve band:         Comfortable on a card for tools that pay back in a month
Program band:            Advisory or fractional help, only when a hire is the alternative
Alternatives:            A general AI assistant; founder peer groups; investor advice;
                         a fractional sales leader; doing it themselves
Signs vs uses:           Signs and uses; low patience for setup
Proof at renewal:        A deal closed without them, or a hire that worked
```

### Secondary personas

- **Forwardee:** The first seller or co-founder. Wants scripts and the exact next step.
- **Broad reader:** Aspiring founders and early employees. Engage on social; rarely buy.

### Worked test: product idea

Asset: *A $400-a-month AI dashboard that benchmarks your pipeline against peers.*

- **Buying rules:** Who uses it? The founder would have to log in weekly; they will not. What decision does it change? None at their stage; they know their pipeline by name. Cold start: how many peers at 15 to 60 employees are in the benchmark on day one? Unstated.
- **Verdict:** Pass. Deciding rule: rule 1 (a product the signer must log into weekly fails) and rule 5 (empty benchmarks kill trust).
- **What would flip it:** A monthly email that names the three deals most likely to slip and what founders at the same stage did about it, priced in the self-serve band. Verdict becomes **Buy if** it reaches them in the inbox with no login and states its peer count.
- **Real-reader test:** Five calls with founders at that size, then a waitlist with a small refundable deposit.

## The same asset, two personas

Asset: *A landing page headline: "The AI Revenue Operating System for Modern GTM Teams."*

| | Head of RevOps | Founder-Seller |
|---|---|---|
| 10-second read | Skims; suspicious of "operating system" claims that ignore the CRM | Deletes; "modern GTM teams" is not their 4-person team |
| Lowest score | Actionability: no hint of what gets configured | Decision relevance: no decision at their stage |
| Fix | "Turn on AI call summaries without breaking your CRM. Setup checklist included." | "Get your first two reps closing without you on the call." |

The lesson: one headline cannot serve both. Pick the primary persona for the page, and route the other to a page of its own.
