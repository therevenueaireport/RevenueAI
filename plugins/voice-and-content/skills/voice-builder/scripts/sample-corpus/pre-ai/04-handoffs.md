# The handoff is the product

I used to think the sales-to-onboarding handoff was a form. It's not a form; it's a promise somebody else has to keep.

We learned this the hard way. In one quarter, 9 of our 31 new customers asked the onboarding team a question the seller had already answered on a call. Nine times the customer had to repeat themselves. Two of those accounts churned within a year.

Was the form bad? A little. The real problem was that nobody owned the gap between "closed" and "kicked off."

Here is the fix we landed on:

1) The seller records a five-minute walkthrough of the deal, in their own words.
2) The onboarding lead watches it before the kickoff, not during.
3) The customer hears one sentence at kickoff: "We already know X, Y and Z, so let's start from there."

That last line does more for trust than any slide we own.

Watch your last three handoffs. Count how many times the customer repeats something. That count is your baseline.
