# Rethinking Forecasting in the Age of AI

In today's rapidly evolving revenue landscape, forecasting has become one of the most critical capabilities a go-to-market organization can develop — and one of the most misunderstood.

Here's the thing: forecasting isn't just a reporting exercise, it's a strategic discipline that shapes how leaders allocate resources, set expectations, and build trust with the board.

Imagine a sales manager reviewing a pipeline of forty opportunities on a Friday afternoon. Without a structured approach, that manager must rely on intuition, anecdotal updates, and incomplete data — a recipe for inconsistency.

AI changes this equation. By leveraging historical win patterns, engagement signals, and stage velocity, modern forecasting models can surface risk earlier and with greater precision than traditional methods.

The implications are significant. Teams that embrace AI-assisted forecasting can unlock more accurate commits, reduce time spent in review meetings, and create a more transparent operating rhythm across the organization.

In conclusion, the question isn't whether AI will transform forecasting — it's whether your team is ready to harness it.
