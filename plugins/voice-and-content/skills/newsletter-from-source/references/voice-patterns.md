# Voice Patterns: The Default Newsletter Voice

**Which voice wins:** if the user has a personal voice profile (for example, one built with the `voice-builder` skill in this pack), it overrides the defaults in this file. The forbidden patterns at the end of this file apply in every voice.

**The default voice:** spartan, conversational professional, consulting-grade insight, humor-led but never corny, zero fluff, zero jargon.

---

## Core voice characteristics

### Opening hooks (never generic)

- **Contrarian claim:** "Most teams run the same discovery script on every buyer. That's why their calls all sound the same."
- **Surprising observation:** "We gave twelve sales managers two days to ship an AI coaching plan for their own teams. Nine shipped. None of them used the plan they'd sketched on day one."
- **Pattern interrupt:** lead with the unexpected result, not the setup.

**Never use:**
- "In today's landscape..."
- "As AI continues to evolve..."
- "The rise of [X] has created..."
- "Many professionals struggle with..."

### Structural DNA

```
Opening hook (1-2 sentences)
  |
"Quick observations:" / "This framework breaks it down:"
  |
Numbered points (3-5)
  - Each starts with the insight
  - Then the evidence or example
  - Then the tactical implication
  |
"Why this matters:" / "The tactical shift:"
  |
Punchy closer (1-2 sentences)
```

### Sentence style

**Short. Tactical. Punchy.**
- "Adapted questions are leverage."
- "One script for every buyer is lazy."
- "Stop copying prompts between tools."

**Credibility markers (earned, not bragging):**
- "I've reviewed hundreds of AI-drafted follow-ups from real deals."
- "I've run this workshop with managers at a dozen companies."
- Experience leads to insight. Credentials do not lead to authority.

**No filler transitions:**
- Not "As we move forward into this next section..."
- Not "It's important to note that..."
- Not "Let's dive deeper into..."
- Jump straight to the point.

### Humor DNA

**Clever, not corny:**
- A playful coinage that reveals the idea (for example, calling a rushed AI rollout "launch-and-pray")
- A personality match rather than a joke setup (for example, "treat the skeptical buyer like a candid friend who owes you nothing")

**Never:**
- Emojis for emphasis
- "Just my two cents!"
- Forced wordplay
- Self-deprecating hedges ("This might be controversial, but...")

## Worked examples (fictional, illustrative)

### Example 1: a buyer-adaptation framework

**Opening:**
"Most teams run the same discovery script on every buyer. That's why their calls all sound the same."

**Structure:**
- Sets up the framework: "This framework breaks it down:"
- Four buyer types (the skeptic, the champion, the delegator, the committee)
- Each follows one pattern: buyer type, then question style, then specific tactics, then why it works
- Bridges to the implication: "Why this matters:"
- Closes with an action: "Stop running one script. Start adapting your questions to the buyer in front of you."

**Voice elements:**
- No throat-clearing intro
- Evidence-backed ("I've listened to hundreds of discovery calls")
- A numbered structure with clear headers
- Tactical imperatives ("Lead with the decision they have to make, not the product")
- A punchy close that reframes the shift

### Example 2: a workshop observation

**Opening:**
"We gave twelve sales managers two days to ship an AI coaching plan for their own teams."

**Structure:**
- "Quick observations:" (signals that numbered insights are coming)
- Four numbered insights, each with:
  - The core observation
  - The supporting evidence or example
  - The implication
- A meta-observation: "The hardest thing to teach was..."
- An honest close: "I still don't have a clean framework for that part."

**Voice elements:**
- Immediate context: what happened
- Real stakes and a real timeline (two days)
- Numbered insights that build on each other
- A specific comparison ("What they built in two days would have been a quarter-long project a few years ago")
- Candor in the close: admits the gap

## Voice checklist

**Structure:**
- [ ] Opens with a surprising, contrarian or specific claim, not generic context
- [ ] Uses a numbered framework (3-5 points)
- [ ] Each point has an insight, evidence and a tactical implication
- [ ] Includes a credibility marker from real experience
- [ ] Closes with a punchy action or reframe, not a summary

**Sentence level:**
- [ ] Zero filler transitions
- [ ] No jargon unless defined
- [ ] Short sentences for emphasis
- [ ] Active voice; imperative when giving advice
- [ ] No hedging ("might," "perhaps," "arguably")

**Tone:**
- [ ] Confident from experience, not credentials
- [ ] Helpful without condescension
- [ ] Witty when natural, never forced
- [ ] No rambling, no repetition

## Forbidden patterns (apply in every voice)

### Never use em dashes

Em dashes are banned. They are one of the most recognizable tells of machine-written text.

Instead of:
"The new model, released just yesterday, changes everything" written with dashes around the middle phrase,

write:
"The new model dropped yesterday. It changes everything."

or:
"The new model (released yesterday) changes everything."

### Classic AI phrasing

**Opening cliches:**
- "AI is transforming the way we work..."
- "In an era of rapid technological change..."
- "As leaders navigate the evolving landscape..."
- "In today's fast-paced business environment..."
- "The landscape of [X] is shifting..."
- "It's no secret that..."
- "Let's face it..."
- "Here's the thing..."

**Transition filler:**
- "It's worth noting that..."
- "One thing to keep in mind is..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "The bottom line is..."
- "Moving forward..."
- "With that in mind..."
- "That said..."
- "To put it simply..."

**Hedge language:**
- "arguably"
- "potentially"
- "possibly"
- "might want to consider"
- "you may find that"
- "it's important to note"

**Weak closes:**
- "So those are my thoughts on [topic]"
- "What do you think? Let me know in the comments!"
- "I hope you found this helpful"
- Generic calls to action ("Subscribe for more insights!")
- "In conclusion..."
- "To sum up..."

### Tells to eliminate

**Overly formal constructions:**
- "One must consider..." becomes "Consider this..."
- "It is essential to understand..." becomes "Here's what matters..."

**Weak passives:**
- "The tool can be utilized by teams..." becomes "Teams use the tool to..."

**Abstract generalizations:**
- "This approach offers numerous benefits..." becomes the specific benefit: "This cuts prep from a half hour to five minutes." (Use the source's numbers.)

**Cautious hedging:**
- "This could potentially improve outcomes..." becomes a direct, sourced claim: "This improved win rates for the guest's team last quarter."

**The "it's not this, it's that" construction:**
- Avoid framing every insight as a correction of a strawman ("It's not about tools. It's about habits."). Once per issue at most; zero is better.
