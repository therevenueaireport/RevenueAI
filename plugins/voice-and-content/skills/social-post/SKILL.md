# Social Post

Every post is a brand impression on the people you most want to reach. The defaults below are tuned for LinkedIn, the primary channel for most B2B professionals; the platform adaptation table shows how to fit the same post to other feeds. This skill turns an idea, a finding, a story or a source document into a post that earns the first two lines, delivers one useful thing, and ends with somewhere to go.

## Step 1: Load the voice

1. **If the user has a voice profile** (for example, one built with the `voice-builder` skill in this pack, or a voice or brand document they point you to), load it and treat its rules as binding. The post must sound like them, not like a template.
2. **If not,** write in a neutral professional voice: plain words, short sentences, a clear opinion, no hype, no jargon, no em dashes. Offer once to build a voice profile with `voice-builder` if they will be posting regularly.

Then confirm three things before drafting (ask only for what is missing):

- **Who is the one reader?** A role and a situation, not "leaders" in general.
- **What is the one idea?** If it takes two sentences to say, it is two posts.
- **What should the reader do or feel after?** Reply, save, try something, rethink something, click.

## Step 2: Pick the post type

| Type | Use when | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Insight post** | Sharing a framework, finding or observation | 150-300 words |
| **Story post** | A real experience that teaches a lesson | 200-400 words |
| **Contrarian take** | Challenging a widely held belief in your field | 150-250 words |
| **Data post** | Sharing a statistic or research finding, with its source | 100-200 words |
| **List post** | A numbered framework or ranked takeaways | 200-350 words |
| **Question post** | Starting a discussion; engagement first | 75-150 words |
| **Behind the scenes** | Building in public: what you are working on, what broke, what you learned | 150-300 words |
| **Carousel or document** | A multi-slide visual post (you write the script) | 6-12 slides |

Default to 150-300 words. Go longer only when a story earns it.

## Step 3: Write the hook

The first one or two lines decide everything. LinkedIn and most other feeds cut a post off after the first two or three lines (often around 140 characters on mobile) and hide the rest behind a "see more" link. If the hook does not stop the scroll, nothing below it gets read.

### Hook formulas (starting points, not templates)

**The contrarian**
```
Most [people in a role] [common belief].
They're wrong.
```
*Example: "Most revenue teams think more AI tools means more productivity. They're wrong."*

**The surprising number**
```
[Number] of [audience] [surprising fact]. (Source in the body.)
```
*Example pattern: "[x] in [y] sales managers we surveyed said they had never seen a rep's AI-drafted email before it was sent." Use only a number you can source.*

**The bold claim**
```
[A strong declarative statement that challenges an assumption.]
```
*Example: "The best AI-enabled sales teams I've seen aren't using the most tools. They're using the fewest."*

**The pattern interrupt**
```
[An unexpected opening that opens a gap the reader needs to close.]
```
*Example: "I got this wrong for two years. Here's what I missed."*

**The stakes frame**
```
[A short sentence on what is at risk or changing right now.]
```
*Example: "Revenue teams that don't build AI fluency this year will be competing against ones that did."*

**The direct observation**
```
Something I keep seeing: [observation].
```
*Example: "Something I keep seeing: leaders buying AI tools before their teams know how to use them."*

### Hook rules

- Never open with "I" as the first word. It reads as self-focused before the reader knows why to care.
- Never open with "In today's landscape," "In today's fast-paced world," or any scene-setting throat-clear.
- Never open with a question the reader can answer "no."
- Short sentence. Then a shorter one. Rhythm stops the thumb.
- The hook should stand alone as a complete short post.
- Write five hooks, then pick one. The first hook is rarely the best.

## Step 4: Build the body

### Insight post

```
[Hook: 1-2 lines]

[Context: 1-2 sentences on why this matters now]

[The insight: the actual thing, numbered or flowing]

[Evidence: a number, an example or an experience]

[So what: the takeaway the reader can act on]

[Closer]
```

### Story post

```
[Hook: drop into the moment, never "let me tell you a story"]

[The situation: brief, specific, grounded]

[The tension or mistake: what went wrong or surprised you]

[The turn: what changed or what you learned]

[The lesson: stated plainly, 1-2 lines]

[Application: how the reader uses it]
```

### Contrarian take

```
[Hook: the belief, then the challenge]

[Why people believe it: be fair to the other side in 1-2 lines]

[What you've seen instead: the evidence]

[The better approach: specific]

[Closer: invite the strongest counter-argument]
```

### Data post

```
[The number, first, bolded]

[Where it comes from: source, sample size, date]

[The non-obvious implication: what it means, not what it says]

[What to do about it: 1-3 specific actions]
```

### List post

```
[Hook: lead with the value of the list, not "here are X things"]

[Why this list matters: 1-2 lines]

1. **[Label]:** [1-2 line explanation]
2. **[Label]:** [1-2 line explanation]
3. ...

[Closing line: a synthesis or a call to action]
```

### Question post

```
[Hook: the question, made specific and hard to answer with one word]

[Your own answer or the context that makes it interesting: 2-4 lines]

[The ask: "What's yours?" framed narrowly enough that people can answer in one comment]
```

### Behind the scenes

```
[Hook: the result, the mistake or the decision, not "an update"]

[What you're building or trying]

[What happened: numbers if you have them]

[What you'd tell someone about to do the same thing]

[Closer: what's next, or a question for people a step ahead]
```

## Step 5: Format for the feed

### Line breaks

- One idea per line when you want impact.
- White space is not wasted. It is what makes a post readable on a phone.
- Never more than two or three sentences in one paragraph.

```
WRONG:
AI training is a stronger predictor of adoption than budget, which means leaders who
invest in teaching their teams will see much better outcomes than those who simply buy
more tools without the enablement to go with them.

RIGHT:
Training beats budget.
Every time.

The teams that taught their reps first got adoption.
The teams that bought more tools got logins.
```

### Emphasis

- Bold sparingly: three or four instances per post at most.
- Avoid italics in the post body; they render inconsistently across apps and devices.
- Numbers stand out on their own. Let them.

### Hashtags

- Three at most, at the very end. Never mid-post.
- Use tags your one reader actually follows, not the broadest ones available.
- On platforms where hashtags do little, skip them.

### Emojis

- Optional. Follow the user's voice profile.
- If used: as list markers or single punctuation, never several per line, never in place of words.

### Links

- Many feeds show posts with external links to fewer people. If the post's goal is reach, put the link in the first comment and say so in the post ("link in the first comment"). If the goal is clicks, keep the link in the post and accept the trade. Check the user's own analytics before treating either as a rule.

## Step 6: Close with somewhere to go

The last line is the second most important line. It decides whether the reader comments, saves, shares or scrolls.

| Closer type | Use when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The narrow question** | You want comments | "What's the one AI tool your team stopped using this year, and why?" |
| **The challenge** | You want action | "Pick one report you build by hand. Try automating it this week." |
| **The reframe** | You want the idea to stick | "Adoption is a habit you build, one workflow at a time." |
| **The save prompt** | The post is reference material | "Save this for your next pilot review." |
| **The pointer** | There is a deeper resource | "The full scorecard is in the first comment." |

Avoid weak closers: "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "Let me know in the comments!", "I hope this helps," "Follow for more." They signal that the post ran out of ideas.

## Voice calibration for social

Apply the user's voice profile first. These hold in any voice:

**Write to one person, not a crowd.**
"If you're a revenue leader with a board meeting next month..." beats "Revenue leaders should..."

**Opinions over observations.**
Do not just report. Have a take. People follow people who say what others will not.

**Credibility through specificity.**
"[n] revenue leaders surveyed" beats "many leaders."
"[n] people trained over [period]" beats "a large community."
"Adoption rose from [x] to [y] in [z] weeks" beats "significantly higher."
Use the user's real numbers, with sources. Never invent one to make a post land.

**Self-awareness is allowed.**
Admitting a mistake, a wrong prediction or a work in progress builds trust faster than a polished win.

**No performance.**
Do not write for applause. Write to help one person. Readers can tell the difference.

## Carousel or document posts

Carousels and document posts often earn longer attention than text posts because each swipe is a small commitment. Check the user's own analytics rather than assuming.

### Slide 1: the hook slide

- A bold headline that follows the hook rules
- A subhead with the promise: what they will learn or be able to do
- The user's brand background and colors

### Slides 2 to N-1: the content slides

- One idea per slide
- A large headline that states the insight
- Two to four lines of supporting copy
- One visual element: an icon, a number, a simple diagram or an example

### Slide N: the takeaway slide

- "What to take away:" with two or three bullets
- One call to action: follow, comment, save, message, or a link
- The logo, small, in a corner

### Carousel rules

- Slides 1 to 3 must be strong enough to earn the next swipe.
- Every slide should stand alone, because people screenshot single slides.
- No slide should need the previous one to make sense.
- Use the user's brand palette and type. If there is none, two colors and one typeface.
- Deliver the script as a table: slide number, headline, body copy, visual note.

## Platform adaptation

Write the core post once, then fit it to each format's constraints. Limits change often, so check the current limit before relying on a number below.

| Format | Constraint | How to adapt |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn and other long-post professional feeds (primary) | Truncates after 2-3 lines; allows ~3,000 characters | Full structure above; hook carries everything |
| Short-post feeds (e.g. X, Threads, Bluesky) | Hard character limit (often a few hundred) | Hook plus one line of evidence; or a numbered thread where each post stands alone |
| Community or forum | Rewards depth and penalizes self-promotion | Lead with the useful part; drop the call to action; answer questions in replies |
| Team chat or internal channel (e.g. Slack, Teams) | Readers are colleagues, skimming between work | First line is the takeaway; bullets; one link |
| Video caption | The video carries the hook | Caption adds context and the call to action only |

## Topic pillars

Help the user define three to five pillars so posts build a recognizable point of view rather than a scattered feed. A pillar is a topic the user has earned the right to talk about.

```
Pillar:          [e.g. AI adoption in revenue teams]
Why me:          [Experience, data or work that gives the user credibility here]
Recurring forms: [e.g. data posts from own surveys; behind-the-scenes builds]
Signature ideas: [Frameworks or phrases the user owns and repeats]
```

**Usually strong:** non-obvious readings of real data; honest tool and rollout outcomes; the gap between hype and what actually happened; frameworks the user has named and uses; building in public.

**Usually avoid:** generic "AI is transforming everything" takes; politics; attacking named competitors; personal content unrelated to the user's professional pillars (unless their voice profile says otherwise).

## Series planning

For a multi-post series (for example, "five days of pilot-review frameworks"):

- **Post 1:** The big contrarian claim. Sets the frame.
- **Posts 2 to 4:** Evidence, frameworks and practical application, one per post.
- **Post 5:** The synthesis, plus a pointer to a deeper resource (a newsletter issue, a guide, a template).

Each post must stand alone and also reward people who follow the whole series. Reference the series lightly ("Day 3 of 5") without making earlier posts required reading.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] One reader, one idea, one desired action
- [ ] Hook fits in the preview window (about 140 characters) and stops the scroll
- [ ] Does not start with "I," a throat-clear, or a yes/no question
- [ ] No em dashes and no banned phrases from the voice profile
- [ ] One idea per line, generous white space, no paragraph over three sentences
- [ ] At least one specific number, example or detail, with a source for any statistic
- [ ] Every number is real and sourced; none invented to make the post land
- [ ] Bold used three or four times at most; no italics in the body
- [ ] Closes with a narrow question, a challenge, a reframe, a save prompt or a pointer
- [ ] Hashtags: three at most, at the end, or none
- [ ] Voice check: sounds like the user, opinionated, no hedging
- [ ] Under 350 words, unless a story post earns more

## Output format

```
**Post type:** <type>   **Reader:** <one role + situation>   **Goal:** <comments / saves / clicks / reach>

**Hook options:**
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...
(Recommended: #)

**Post:**
<the full post, formatted exactly as it should be pasted>

**First comment (if used):** <link or resource>

**Why it should work:** <one or two lines tying the hook and closer to the reader>
```
