# Full scoring rubric (8 assets, 0 to 3 each, 24 max)

Load this before scoring. Every asset uses the same ladder, but the tells differ.
The universal ladder:

- **0 Absent.** The asset does not exist for this brand.
- **1 Present, category default.** It exists but looks like the category. A buyer
  could not use it to tell this brand from three rivals.
- **2 Distinctive inside the category.** It is theirs. Anyone who knows the space
  recognizes it as this brand.
- **3 Recognizable out of context.** Seen with the name removed, in a foreign
  setting, a knowledgeable person still names the brand.

Score against the brands a buyer actually confuses this one with, not against the
whole world.

---

## 1. Color

- 0: No consistent color. Random or all-neutral.
- 1: A color is used but it is a category default (another SaaS blue, another
  security dark, another fintech green).
- 2: A distinctive hue or color system, recognized inside the category before a word
  is read.
- 3: A color you would know out of context. The hue alone triggers the brand.

## 2. Logo

- 0: No mark, or inconsistent marks.
- 1: A wordmark or symbol that competitors could also run. Generic geometric icon,
  default type.
- 2: A mark that is theirs alone inside the category.
- 3: A mark recognizable with no context, no name, no category cue.

## 3. Visual device

- 0: No recurring non-logo form.
- 1: A generic shape or graphic system (gradient blobs, dot grids everyone uses).
- 2: A distinctive recurring form or graphic system that is not the logo but still
   reads as the brand.
- 3: A device recognizable out of context on its own.

## 4. Typography

- 0: No consistent type, or system defaults with no intent.
- 1: A category-default typeface (the neutral sans every design tool ships with, and
   its near-identical cousins), set without ownership.
- 2: A typeface and setting ownable enough that you could skip the logo and still
   read the brand.
- 3: Type recognizable out of context without seeing the name.

## 5. Taglines

- 0: No repeated line.
- 1: A generic phrase fifty competitors could run ("AI-powered platform", "the
   future of X"). A one-off campaign slogan that does not recur.
- 2: A line the brand repeats until it sticks. Words that come back, owned by the
   brand inside the category.
- 3: A line instantly tied to the brand out of context.

## 6. Human identity

- 0: No character, mascot, or recurring face.
- 1: A generic stock character or an interchangeable face.
- 2: A character, mascot, or recurring human recognized inside the category.
- 3: A face or character the brain latches onto and names anywhere.

## 7. Product UI motif

- 0: No signature inside the product.
- 1: A generic interface element any competitor's product also shows.
- 2: A signature inside the product, the software equivalent of packaging,
   recognizable to category insiders.
- 3: A UI motif recognizable out of context, even in a screenshot with no name.

## 8. Music / sound

- 0: No sound identity. (Most brands score here.)
- 1: Present but generic. The atmospheric, grandiose explainer-video music every
   competitor reaches for.
- 2: A sound you would know with your eyes shut. A sting, a motif, a sonic logo.
- 3: A sound instantly tied to the brand out of context.

---

## Bands (total out of 24)

- **0 to 6 Invisible.** Remove the name from the homepage and no one could name it.
- **7 to 12 Generic.** Competent, forgettable. Where most brands sit.
- **13 to 18 Recognizable.** Known inside their own category.
- **19 to 24 Ownable.** Distinctive enough to recognize out of context.

## Reading the map

- A high total driven only by color and typography is fragile. Those are the crowded
  assets and they cancel across a category.
- Points in human identity or sound are rare and durable. They are worth more per
  point than the headline total suggests.
- The gap between the current band and the next band is usually one open asset built
  with intensity, not eight small polishes.
