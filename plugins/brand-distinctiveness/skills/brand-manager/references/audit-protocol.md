# Audit protocol (two-pass, evidence-based)

This mirrors the two-pass method used in published audits of B2B brand
distinctiveness: gather the raw material first, score each asset against evidence,
then review every score adversarially by hand. Never score from memory or
homepage-only impressions.

## Step 1: Gather the surface

Pull the real artifacts before scoring. For a thorough audit, fan out (parallel
agents can pull several surfaces at once) and collect:

- Website: homepage, product pages, pricing, about.
- Product: screenshots of the actual UI, onboarding, empty states.
- Social: company profile and posts, and the leaders' personal posts (founder,
  execs). Human identity often lives here.
- Paid: the public ad libraries that the major social and search ad platforms
  publish. Search them by brand name.
- Audio and video: explainer videos, podcast intro and mid-roll, conference talks,
  webinar opens, the brand's video-platform channel.
- Sales: the deck, one-pagers, leave-behinds if available.
- Events: booth, walk-on music, swag.

Record the source for each observation so scores are defensible.

## Step 2: First pass, one asset at a time

Score each of the eight assets 0 to 3 using `scoring-rubric.md`. For each score,
write the single artifact that justifies it. If you cannot point to an artifact, the
score is 0.

Score the eight in this order so the cheap-to-fake assets do not anchor the rest:
sound, human identity, visual device, taglines, product UI motif, typography, logo,
color.

## Step 3: Calibrate to the category

Name the three to five brands a buyer actually confuses this one with. Re-check every
asset you scored a 2: is it distinctive versus those specific rivals, or only
distinctive in your own head? A blue among blues is a 1, not a 2, no matter how much
you like it.

Worked example: a fictional procurement-software company scores its color a 2 for a
deep navy with a mint accent. Its four closest rivals run navy, navy, navy with teal,
and slate blue. Recalibrated, it is a 1. The mint accent might be a 2 if it were the
primary, but it covers less than a tenth of the surface, so it cannot carry the score.

## Step 4: Second pass, adversarial review

Re-score as a skeptic. For every asset, ask: with the name removed, would a buyer who
knows the space name this brand from this asset alone? A 3 must survive out of
context. Downgrade anything that only reads as the brand because you already know the
answer. This pass is where inflated scores die.

## Step 5: Output the map

Deliver:

1. A table: asset, score 0-3, the evidence artifact, one-line note.
2. Total out of 24 and the band.
3. Contested assets (crowded, cancel out) vs open assets (empty or weak across the
   category).
4. The one asset to attack next, and why it is the open goal.
5. The single slightly-too-weird commitment for that asset.
6. A surface-by-surface consistency checklist for that asset.

Keep it spartan and data-led. Lead with the total and band, then the open asset. No
em dashes.
