# Distinctive asset: typography

The typeface and how it is set. Ownable enough that you could skip the logo and still
read the brand. Meaning-free, like every asset here. Type is a retrieval cue, not an
argument.

Typography is a crowded asset near the top of most cohorts, largely because everyone
defaults to the same neutral sans. That default is also why it is a fast, cheap lift:
most brands are sitting at a 1 and could reach a 2 with one confident choice.

## Scoring, 0 to 3

- **0 Absent.** No consistent type, or system defaults used with no intent.
- **1 Category default.** The neutral sans every design tool ships with, and its
  near-identical cousins, set with no ownership. Present, not distinctive.
- **2 Distinctive in category.** A typeface and setting ownable enough to skip the
  logo and still read the brand inside the category.
- **3 Out of context.** Type recognizable anywhere without seeing the name.

## The category-default trap

A 1 is the default neutral sans, a classic Swiss-style grotesque, or the platform
default, set without a system.
Safe, clean, invisible. If it is the font on half the category's sites, it is a 1.

## Moving up the ladder

- **1 to 2:** Choose a display face with real character for headlines and pair it
  with a workhorse for body. Or take a neutral face and own the setting: a distinctive
  scale, tracking, weight contrast, or case convention that is unmistakably the brand.
  Picture a fictional issue-tracking company that sets every headline in a tight,
  high-contrast editorial serif while every rival runs geometric sans. A cropped
  screenshot of one headline is enough to name it. That is the pattern.
- **2 to 3:** Hold the type system everywhere long enough that the setting alone reads
  as the brand out of context.

## Build playbook

1. Pick one display or headline face with character. Distinctiveness lives at the top
   of the page.
2. Pair it with one reliable workhorse for body and UI. Two families, not five.
3. Define the setting system: scale, weights, tracking, case, how headlines are set.
   The setting is half the asset.
4. Apply it across every surface, including slides and the product, where most brands
   quietly revert to defaults.

## Hold it across the surface

Website, product UI, slide masters, one-pagers, newsletter, social templates, ad
copy, docs, PDFs. The same two families and the same setting, everywhere. Watch decks
and product, the two places type consistency usually breaks.

## Anti-patterns

- Defaulting to the neutral sans because it is safe, then wondering why the brand is
  invisible.
- Five typefaces fighting each other.
- A distinctive headline font on the website that never appears in the deck or the
  product.

## Strategic note

A fast, cheap climb to a 2. Take it, then stop. Like [[brand-asset-color]], type
cancels when everyone competes on it, so do not over-invest. Put the real budget into
the open assets.
