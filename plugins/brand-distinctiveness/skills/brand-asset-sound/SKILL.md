# Distinctive asset: music / sound

A sound you would know with your eyes shut. A sting, a motif, a sonic logo.
Meaning-free by nature: three seconds of audio argue nothing and retrieve everything.

This is the emptiest asset in B2B. Published audits of B2B brands against these
eight assets find that nearly every brand scores zero on sound, the few that score at
all land on a generic 1, and a distinctive 2 is almost unheard of. It is the
highest-opportunity asset and the hardest to brief, which is precisely why it sits
open. Build one and there is almost no competitor to cancel it out.

## Scoring, 0 to 3

- **0 Absent.** No sound identity. Where nearly every B2B brand sits.
- **1 Category default.** Present but generic: the atmospheric, grandiose explainer
  music every competitor reaches for. Interchangeable library tracks.
- **2 Distinctive in category.** A sting, motif, or sonic logo you would know with
  your eyes shut, owned by the brand.
- **3 Out of context.** A sound instantly tied to the brand anywhere.

## The "our buyers watch on mute" objection

The reflex objection is: we sell to procurement and engineers, they watch on mute, so
what is our brand supposed to sound like. Answer it head on. A sonic device does not
need the product video. It lives in every audio surface the brand already runs:

- The podcast intro and outro.
- Ads run inside other people's podcasts.
- The walk-on music at a conference or workshop.
- The song or sting inside the next booth.
- The videos leaders post on professional social networks.
- Paid social video stings.
- The webinar and course-module open.
- Video cutdowns of a report or newsletter.

That is an entire asset class sitting open across the whole category.

## Build playbook

1. Build ONE short motif, roughly three to five seconds. A sonic logo, not a full
   track. Short enough to repeat, distinctive enough to know blind.
2. Derive a small kit from it: the full sting, a one-note tag, a longer bed for
   intros. All recognizably the same motif.
3. Deploy it on every audio surface above, the same way, every time.
4. Freeze it. Sound compounds only through repetition. Do not remix it every quarter.

## Hold it across the surface

Podcast intro and outro, mid-roll ads in other podcasts, conference walk-on, booth,
webinar and module opens, social video stings, video cutdowns, leaders' social
videos. One motif, held everywhere.

What it can sound like, all fictional: a cybersecurity company whose sting is two
low piano notes and a lock-click; a data-pipeline company whose motif is a rising
three-note marimba run that ends on a water-drop pluck; a forecasting tool that opens
every podcast with a metronome tick that resolves into a single bell.

## Anti-patterns

- Licensing a generic library track that fifty other explainers also use. That is a 1,
  not an asset.
- A one-off jingle for a single launch that never recurs.
- Remixing the motif so often it never becomes familiar.

## Strategic note

Sound is the clearest open goal in the entire framework. It is also the highest-effort
to brief, which is why it stays empty. If the brand has any in-house music capability,
the hardest-to-copy asset in B2B becomes the easiest one for it to own, and no
competitor can comfortably follow. A founder or team member who plays, produces, or
scores music is an unfair advantage here. See the worked baseline in
[[brand-manager]] (`../brand-manager/references/baseline-scorecard.md`), where sound is the recommended
primary investment for exactly this reason.
