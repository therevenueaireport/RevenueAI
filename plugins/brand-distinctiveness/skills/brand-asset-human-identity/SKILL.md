# Distinctive asset: human identity

A character, mascot, or recurring face. A face the brain latches onto. Meaning-free:
picture a fictional logistics-software company whose mascot is a walking stack of
orange crates. It argues nothing about route optimization. It is simply, instantly,
that brand.

This is one of the fastest assets to build recognition and one of the rarest. Few
B2B brands score a 2 or better here, and most score zero. That makes it an open goal.
Reference points, all fictional: a sales-tool company whose raccoon in a captain's
hat appears in every ad, onboarding screen, and booth; a developer-tools company whose
founder opens every launch video from the same grey workshop, same framing, same
first line; the crate-stack mascot above.

## Scoring, 0 to 3

- **0 Absent.** No character, mascot, or recurring face.
- **1 Category default.** A stock character or an interchangeable headshot.
- **2 Distinctive in category.** A character, mascot, or recurring human recognized
  inside the category.
- **3 Out of context.** A face or character the brain names anywhere.

## Two routes

There are two ways to build this asset. Pick one and commit.

1. **A character or mascot.** Invent a face and commit to it. This is where "slightly
   too weird" pays off. A raccoon in a captain's hat. A walking stack of crates. If it
   feels safe, it is probably taken.
2. **A recurring human.** Systematize a real founder or leader as the face: same role,
   same framing, consistently visible and vocal across the surface. Most B2B founders
   are half-built human assets that were never made consistent.

## Strategy before a face

Do not give a brand a face before the positioning is set. A mascot on vague
positioning is a memorable brand for nothing. Run [[brand-positioning-gate]] first,
then decide what the character or persona is there to make retrievable. Strategy
comes first. The face comes second.

## Moving up the ladder

- **1 to 2:** Commit to one character or one recurring human and give it a consistent
  design or framing. Use it as a lead element, not a footnote.
- **2 to 3:** Repeat it everywhere for years until the face alone names the brand out
  of context.

## Build playbook

1. Choose the route: character or human.
2. Define it once: for a character, a fixed design and personality; for a human, a
   fixed role, framing, and visual treatment.
3. Put it in the lead: hero, ads, product, social, event stage, video.
4. Feed it constantly. A human asset needs the person visible and vocal on a cadence.
5. Never quietly retire it. Recognition is cumulative.

## Hold it across the surface

Website hero, ads, social (company and personal), product moments, sales deck, event
stage and walk-on, video thumbnails, swag. Same face, everywhere, on repeat.

## Anti-patterns

- A mascot with no positioning behind it.
- A founder who appears once a quarter. Inconsistent is the same as absent.
- Designing something safe. Safe human identity does not latch.

## Strategic note

For a brand built around a real, recurring, vocal person (a founder who hosts the
podcast, writes the newsletter, and speaks at every event), the fastest 3 in the whole
system is usually here: systematize the human that already exists rather than
inventing a mascot to compete with them. See the worked baseline in
[[brand-manager]] (`../brand-manager/references/baseline-scorecard.md`) for this pattern scored end to
end.
