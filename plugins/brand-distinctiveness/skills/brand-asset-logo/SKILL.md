# Distinctive asset: logo

The mark itself: wordmark, symbol, or both. The logo is meaning-free. Picture a
fictional payments-infrastructure company that hides a forward arrow in the negative
space of its wordmark, and a fictional procurement platform whose symbol conceals a
handshake. Neither wins a single deal because of the hidden story. Buyers who never
notice the arrow still recognize the mark. A clever meaning is a bonus. Recognition is
the job.

## Scoring, 0 to 3

- **0 Absent.** No mark, or inconsistent marks across surfaces.
- **1 Category default.** A wordmark or symbol competitors could also run. Generic
  geometric icon, default typeface, safe.
- **2 Distinctive in category.** A mark that is theirs alone inside the category.
- **3 Out of context.** Recognizable with no name, no category cue, anywhere.

## The category-default trap

A 1 is the abstract swoosh, the interlocking-shapes icon, the set-in-a-default-font
wordmark. If it could belong to any of five rivals, it is a 1. Cleverness does not
raise the score. Distinctiveness does.

## Moving up the ladder

- **1 to 2:** Choose distinctiveness over cleverness. Make the mark unmistakably this
  brand inside the category. Lock one exact lockup and stop drifting between
  variations.
- **2 to 3:** Hold the exact mark, unchanged, long enough that it reads out of
  context. Out-of-context recognition is a function of consistency times time.
  Picture a fictional freight-software company that has run the same squat yellow
  chevron, untouched, for twenty years: drivers and dispatchers spot it on a trailer
  door at highway speed. Leaving it alone is the mechanism, not neglect.

## Build playbook

1. Decide wordmark, symbol, or combination based on how the brand actually shows up
   (a symbol earns its keep only if it appears without the name often).
2. Design for recognition, not for a hidden story. Simple, ownable, reproducible at
   favicon size.
3. Test with the name removed. If a category insider cannot name it, it is not there
   yet.
4. Define one canonical lockup, clear space, and the few approved variants. Kill the
   rest.
5. Freeze it. Resist the annual refresh.

## Hold it across the surface

Favicon, app icon, product header, sales deck, one-pagers, email signature, social
avatars, paid ads, swag, event signage. Same mark, same lockup, every time.

## Anti-patterns

- Refreshing the logo for the fifth time this year. Every refresh resets recognition.
- Chasing a hidden-meaning mark and mistaking the story for the asset.
- Letting a dozen lockup variants dilute the one people should learn.
