# Distinctive asset: taglines

A line repeated until it sticks. Words that come back, not a one-off campaign slogan.
A tagline can carry meaning, but it works as a distinctive asset only through
repetition: the same words, everywhere, long enough that the line retrieves the brand.

Reference points, all fictional: a contract-management company that ends every
asset with "Signed means signed." A data-quality company whose line is "Trust the
number." A forecasting tool that has run "Call it once." on every deck close, ad
end-card, and podcast sign-off for four years. Short, owned, repeated.

## Scoring, 0 to 3

- **0 Absent.** No repeated line.
- **1 Category default.** A generic phrase fifty competitors could run: "AI-powered
  platform", "the future of X", "work, smarter". Or a slogan that changes every
  campaign and never recurs.
- **2 Distinctive in category.** A line the brand repeats until it sticks, owned
  inside the category.
- **3 Out of context.** A line instantly tied to the brand anywhere.

## The category-default trap

A 1 is the interchangeable value-prop line and the disposable campaign slogan. If the
line could sit under any rival's logo, or if it was replaced last quarter, it is a 1.
Repetition is the mechanism. A new line every campaign resets it to zero.

## Moving up the ladder

- **1 to 2:** Write one short line, tied directly to the positioning, and commit to it
  as a permanent fixture rather than a campaign. Then repeat it past the point where
  the team is bored of it. That boredom is the asset forming.
- **2 to 3:** Keep the same line for years, across every surface, until the words alone
  return the brand out of context.

## Build playbook

1. Confirm the positioning first via [[brand-positioning-gate]]. The line points at
   the difference that is worth retrieving. A great line on vague positioning just
   makes the vagueness catchy.
2. Write for repetition: short, plain, easy to say, hard to confuse with a rival. Not
   clever for its own sake.
3. Pick ONE. Not a family of lines. One line that comes back.
4. If the brand has a voice or style guide, run the shortlist through it so the line
   sounds like the brand, then lock it. Say each candidate out loud. If it trips the
   tongue, it will not get repeated.
5. Put it everywhere and leave it alone.

## Hold it across the surface

Homepage, product, email signatures, deck cover and close, one-pagers, social bios,
ad end-cards, podcast sign-off, event opens. Same line, same words, every time.

## Anti-patterns

- A new slogan every campaign. That is marketing activity, not a distinctive asset.
- Jargon lines built from category buzzwords.
- A clever line no one repeats because it is hard to say.
